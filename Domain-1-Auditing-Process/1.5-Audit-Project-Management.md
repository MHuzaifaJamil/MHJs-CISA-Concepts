# 1.5 Audit Project Management

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Audit process
  - Gather evidence
  - Evaluate control strengths and weaknesses
  - Report to management objectively
- Audit management ensures resources, schedule and follow-up
- Audit steps
  - Define scope
  - Formulate objectives
  - Identify criteria
  - Perform procedures
  - Evaluate evidence
  - Form conclusions and opinions
  - Report after discussion with process owners
- Project management techniques
  - Plan the audit engagement
  - Build the audit plan
  - Execute the plan
  - Monitor project activity
### 1.5.1 Audit objectives
- Objectives drive scope, tests and evidence 📓
- Often confirm controls exist to minimize business risk and function as expected
- Include legal compliance and CIA of information and IT resources
- Translate broad objectives into specific IS audit objectives
- Identify key control points after documenting processes
- Identify key general and application controls
- Substantive testing of financial data via CAATs
### 1.5.2 Audit phases
- Phases: plan, define, perform, report
#### Planning
- Risk assessment is the first step 📓⭐
- Establish objectives, scope and methodology 📓
- Identify control objectives after selecting the business process 📓⭐
- Functional walk-through to understand the process 📓⭐
- Allocate resources
- Determine audit subject
- Define audit objective
- Set audit scope
  - Limit to systems, function, unit or period
  - Helps determine skills and testing points
- Perform preaudit planning
  - Risk assessment to set final scope
  - Interview auditee on areas of concern
  - Identify regulatory compliance requirements
  - Define resources: skills, budget, locations, roles, time frame
  - Identify information sources: flowcharts, policies, prior work papers
  - Points of contact and communication plan
- Determine audit procedures and steps for data gathering
  - Obtain policies, standards, guidelines
  - List individuals to interview
  - Identify methods and tools
  - Develop test scripts and evaluation criteria
  - Define method to verify test accuracy and repeatability
#### Fieldwork / documentation
- Collect and evaluate evidence
- Test controls
- Seek explanations from the auditee before concluding 📓
- Perform root cause analysis before reporting 📓⭐
- Confirm material findings with additional evidence 📓⭐
- Acquire data
  - Advance request list for evidence
  - Secure collection (fileshare)
  - GRC tool can help
- Test controls via interviews, observations, inspections, sampling
- Discover and validate issues
  - Issues are deviations from expected outcomes
  - Basis for recommendations
- Document results in audit program and work papers
#### Reporting / follow-up
- Discuss findings with auditee management to gain agreement 📓⭐
- Then report formally (see 1.9)
- Gather report requirements
  - From internal standards or external requirements
- Draft report
  - Reviewed by IS audit leadership before auditee
- Auditee responds with planned actions before final report
- Issue report
  - Retain per requirements
  - Present to oversight function such as audit committee
- Follow up on management remediation progress
### 1.5.3 Audit programs
- Step-by-step set of audit procedures and instructions
- Based on scope and objective of assignment
- Purposes
  - Formal documentation of procedures and steps
  - Repeatable procedures for others
  - Document testing type (compliance and/or substantive)
  - Meet generally accepted standards for planning
- Perspectives: security, quality, fiduciary, service, capacity
- General audit procedures
  - Understand audit area
  - Risk assessment, general plan and schedule
  - Detailed planning and timeline
  - Preliminary review of audit area
  - Evaluate audit area
  - Verify appropriateness of controls
  - Compliance testing
  - Substantive testing
  - Reporting
  - Follow-up
- Minimum skills to develop a program
  - Understand enterprise, industry, risk and threat
  - Understand IT space and technologies
  - Understand business risk to IT risk relationship
  - Basic risk assessment knowledge
  - Know testing procedures for IS controls
    - Generalized audit software (GAS)
    - Specialized software for OS, database, parameter files
    - Flowcharting for processes and automated controls
    - Audit logs and reports
    - Documentation review
    - Inquiry and observation
    - Walk-throughs
    - Reperformance of controls
- Procedures per control objective ➕
### 1.5.4 Audit work papers
- Document plans, programs, activities, tests, findings, incidents
- Bridge between audit objectives and final report
- Traceability from objectives to report and back
- Audit report viewed as a work paper
- Protect integrity of test evidence
- Apply security requirements to work papers
- Retention and destruction process per legal requirements
- Document evidence, tests and conclusions
- Supervisory review of work papers 📓
### 1.5.5 Fraud, irregularities and illegal acts
- Management responsible for controls that deter or detect fraud
- Controls fail through
  - Exploited vulnerabilities
  - Management-perpetrated weaknesses
  - Collusion
- Controls do not eliminate fraud
- Auditor needs knowledge of fraud indicators and due care
- Suspected fraud: collect sufficient evidence first 📓⭐
- Then report to appropriate management
- Major fraud or high risk: tell audit committee promptly
- Be aware of legal requirements on fraud detection and reporting
### 1.5.6 Agile auditing 📓
- Short, iterative planning-execution-review cycles 📓
- Sprints: time-boxed segments on specific risks 📓
- Continuous feedback loop with stakeholders 📓
- Flexible in rapidly changing environments 📓
- Compared with traditional (linear, structured) auditing 📓
- Benefits and comparison to assurance standards
- Agile values
  - Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
  - Working software over comprehensive documentation
  - Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
  - Responding to change over following a plan
- Blurs separation of planning and fieldwork
  - Tasks run in parallel
  - Auditees prepare data while planning finishes
- Benefits
  - Reduced end-to-end planning: weeks or days
  - Streamlined engagements: phases combined
  - Direct customer collaboration: daily standup scrum
  - Flexible audit scope with management approval
  - Real-time assurance: findings shared as discovered
  - Frequent audit plan updates: reviewed quarterly
- Complements ITAF standards
  - 1002 Organizational Independence: direct communication
  - 1003 Objectivity: not compromised, skepticism retained
  - 1005 Due Professional Care: backlog prioritized more often
  - 1006 Proficiency: staff learn business faster
  - 1402.3 Follow-up and risk acceptance: full disclosure to executives
  - Guideline 2001.2.6 QA: sprint retrospective
