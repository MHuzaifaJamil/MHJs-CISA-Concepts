# 1.3 Risk-Based Audit Planning

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Planning sets overall audit strategy and specific procedures
- Short-term planning: issues covered during the year
- Long-term planning: risk issues from changes in IT strategic direction
- Analysis of short and long-term issues at least annually
- Approved by audit committee or board
- Update plan when risk environment changes (acquisitions, regulation, market)
### 1.3.1 Individual audit assignments
- Each audit needs its own planning
- Factors affecting approach
  - Results of periodic risk assessments
  - Changes in technology
  - Evolving privacy and regulatory requirements
  - Implementation or upgrade deadlines
  - Business process owner requirements
  - IS resource limitations
- Understand business practices, functions and supporting systems
- Know the regulatory environment
#### Audit universe 📓⭐
- Comprehensive list of all auditable entities 📓
- First step in annual IS audit planning 📓
- Foundation for the annual plan 📓
- Lists all processes that may be considered for audit
- Each process gets a qualitative or quantitative risk assessment
- Risk factors influence frequency or business impact of scenarios
- Evaluation uses inputs from business process owners
- Criteria objective but subjectivity cannot be fully avoided
- Reputation example ratings
  - High: recovery over six months
  - Medium: recovery between three and six months
  - Low: recovery under three months
- Ideal annual plan includes all processes rated high
- Resource gap shows management the risk it accepts
#### Risk-based audit plan
- Prioritizes high-risk areas 📓
- Aligns with strategic organizational objectives 📓
- Uses key business processes identified by senior management 📓⭐
- When resources are limited, focus on high-risk areas 📓
- Starts with a risk assessment 📓⭐
- Deploys audit resources where risk is greatest
- Requires understanding of the organization
  - External and internal factors
  - Policies and procedures selected and applied
  - Objectives and strategies
  - Performance measurement and review
- Key components to understand
  - Strategy management
  - Business products and services
  - Corporate governance process
  - Transaction types, partners and flows
- Used to decide compliance vs substantive testing
- Determines nature and extent of testing
- Business risk may be financial, regulatory or operational
- Risk model can be simple weights or elaborate scheme
#### Audit planning steps
- Understand mission, objectives, purpose and processes
  - Include availability, integrity, security, confidentiality requirements
- Understand governance structure and practices
- Understand changes in auditee business environment
- Review prior work papers
- Identify stated policies, standards, guidelines, procedures, structure
- Perform risk analysis to design the audit plan
- Set audit scope and objectives
- Develop audit approach or strategy
- Assign personnel resources
- Address engagement logistics
- Identify opportunities for continuous audit or CAATs automation
#### Audit scope and objectives
- Statutory and regulatory requirements are the prime consideration 📓
- Audit objectives help identify key business risks 📓
### 1.3.2 Effect of laws and regulations on IS audit planning
- Statutory and regulatory requirements 📓
- SOX: financial reporting accuracy and controls 📓
- Regulatory data quality is the top audit priority 📓
- Legal obligations and audit credibility 📓
- Industry regulations affect processing, transmission, storage
- Closely regulated industries need special compliance attention
- Legal regulations on IS audit cover
  - Regulatory requirements
  - Responsibilities of entities
  - Financial, operational and IS audit functions
- Two areas of concern
  - Legal requirements applicable to audit or IS audit
  - Legal requirements placed on auditee systems, data, reporting
- Steps to determine compliance with external requirements
  - Identify requirements (electronic data, personal data, copyright, ecommerce, e-signatures)
  - Document applicable laws and regulations
  - Assess whether management considered them in plans and policies
  - Review IT documents on adherence to laws
  - Determine adherence to procedures for external requirements
  - Check contracts with IT providers reflect legal responsibilities
- Exam asks how to audit compliance, not specific laws
### 1.3.3 Audit risk and materiality
#### Audit risk components
- Audit risk: material error may go undetected
- Inherent risk: exposure without any controls 📓⭐
  - Exists independent of an audit
  - Arises from nature of the business
- Control risk
  - Material error not prevented or detected by internal controls
  - Manual log review: high control risk
  - Computerized data validation: ordinarily low
- Detection risk
  - Material errors not detected by the IS auditor
  - Reduced by proper statistical sampling or quality control
- Overall audit risk
  - Objective: limit to sufficiently low level at completion
#### Materiality and assurance
- Material items focus audit effort 📓
- Material findings: significant impact on objectives 📓
- Reasonable assurance because of sampling limits 📓⭐
- Materiality: relative significance in context of whole enterprise
- Inverse relationship: higher materiality, lower acceptable audit risk
- Small errors combined may become material
- Audit risk is not sampling risk
### 1.3.4 Risk assessment
- Identify, evaluate and mitigate risks through controls 📓
- Provides coverage of material items 📓
- Identify, quantify, prioritize risk against acceptance criteria
- Guides management action and control priorities
- Performed periodically and after significant change
- IT management responsible for conducting risk assessments
- Auditor may assist if expertise is missing
- Auditor may run a separate assessment for audit planning
### 1.3.5 IS audit risk assessment techniques
- Scoring systems
  - Prioritize audits by risk factors
  - Variables: technical complexity, control level, financial loss
  - Variables may or may not be weighted
- Judgment-based assessment
  - Based on business knowledge, executive directives, history, goals
- Qualitative and quantitative methods
  - Range from high/medium/low to numeric ratings
- Combination of techniques allowed
- Use management risk assessments with professional skepticism
- Benefits of using risk assessment
  - Allocates limited audit resources effectively
  - Gathers information from all management levels
  - Basis for managing the audit department
  - Links audit subject to overall organization and business plans
### 1.3.6 Risk analysis
- Threat × vulnerability × impact
- Residual risk: remaining after controls 📓
- Subset of risk assessment used in audit planning
- Controls address what should be achieved and what should be avoided
- Risk assessment procedures do not give evidence to support the opinion
- Risk also exists within the audit process
