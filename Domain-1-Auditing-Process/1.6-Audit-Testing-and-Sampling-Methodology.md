# 1.6 Audit Testing and Sampling Methodology

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Sampling supports valid conclusions
- Use statistical sampling to conclude on whole population
- Sample steps: design, select, perform procedures, evaluate results
### 1.6.1 Compliance vs substantive testing ⭐
- Compliance (controls) test: are controls operating?
  - Test of controls
  - Evaluates operating effectiveness in preventing or detecting weaknesses
  - Example: compare source and object versions in production library
  - Examples for sampling: user access rights, change control, logs, license audits
- Substantive test: are balances and transactions correct?
  - Test of details
  - Detects material weaknesses at assertion level
  - Substantiates integrity of actual processing
  - Examples: recompute interest, vouch transactions to documentation
- Direct correlation: strong controls allow less substantive testing
- Weak controls call for more substantive testing
- Include substantive tests if preliminary evaluation finds controls unreliable
- Substantive categories: analytical review procedures; test balances and transactions
### 1.6.2 Sampling 📓
- Used when time and cost preclude full verification
- Population: entire group examined; sample: subset
- Sample size, selection and evaluation need judgment on population
#### Core classifications
- Statistical sampling: mathematical probability 📓
  - Objective method of sample size and selection
  - Uses probability to calculate size, select items, evaluate
  - Each item has equal chance of selection
  - Results quantifiable (confidence coefficient)
- Non-statistical (judgmental): auditor's judgment 📓
  - Judgment sets method, size and selection
  - Focus on most material and risky items
- Purpose: representation of large populations 📓
#### Attribute sampling ("how often") 📓
- Used generally in compliance testing
- Presence or absence of attribute, rate of incidence
- Answers "how many"
- Example: approval signatures on access request forms
- Attribute-based sampling: frequency of control failures 📓
- Stop-or-go sampling: stop early if no errors 📓
  - Used when few errors expected
- Discovery sampling: find at least one critical or fraud instance 📓⭐
  - Error-free sample assumes no fraud
  - Single error taints entire sample
- Precision sampling: size set by desired accuracy 📓
#### Variable sampling ("how much") 📓
- Used generally in substantive testing
- Estimates monetary value or other measure (weight)
- Conclusions on deviations from norm
- Unstratified (mean-per-unit) 📓
  - Sample mean projected to estimated total
- Stratified: split the population into sub-groups 📓⭐
  - Used when distinct subgroups exist (e.g., high-net-worth vs normal customers) 📓
  - Prevents large values dominating small ones 📓
  - Smaller overall sample than unstratified
- Difference estimation 📓
  - Estimates total difference between audited and book values
- Ratio estimation 📓
- Monetary Unit Sampling (MUS): odds weighted by dollars 📓
- Classical variable sampling: normal distribution to estimate totals 📓
#### Selection techniques 📓
- Random sampling 📓
- Systematic (fixed-interval) sampling 📓
- Cluster sampling 📓
- Judgmental / subjective sampling 📓
#### Sampling parameters ➕
- Sampling risk
- Confidence level
  - Confidence coefficient: percent sample represents population
  - 95 percent is high assurance
  - Strong internal controls allow lower confidence
  - Higher confidence means larger sample
- Tolerable and expected error rate
  - Tolerable error rate: maximum error without material misstatement
  - Used for planned upper limit of compliance precision
  - Expected error rate: higher means larger sample
  - Expected error rate applies to attribute, not variable
- Precision
  - Acceptable difference between sample and population
  - Higher precision amount means smaller sample
  - Precision range and precision mean the same in substantive testing
- Level of risk
  - One minus confidence coefficient
- Sample mean
  - Sum of sample values divided by sample size
- Sample standard deviation
  - Spread of sample values around the mean
- Population standard deviation
  - Greater deviation means larger sample
  - Applies to variable sampling, not attribute
#### Sample selection steps
- Determine objective
- Define population
- Determine method
#### Special situations
- Population too small for a valid sample: redesign the test, such as testing 100% 📓⭐
### 1.6.3 Sampling risk
- Risk that the sample misrepresents the population
- Conclusion may differ from testing entire population
- Two types
  - Incorrect acceptance: weakness judged unlikely but population materially misstated
  - Incorrect rejection: weakness judged likely but population not materially misstated
- Distinct from audit risk
