# 1.1 IS Audit Standards, Guidelines, Functions and Codes of Ethics

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Credibility of IS audit depends on adherence to accepted standards
- ISACA code of ethics guides professional and personal conduct

### 1.1.1 ISACA IS Audit and Assurance Standards
- Standards inform three audiences
  - IS auditors: minimum acceptable performance
  - Management: profession's expectations of practitioners
  - CISA holders: professional performance requirements
#### Nature of standards
- Mandatory for ISACA members 📓⭐
- Define the minimum acceptable performance level 📓
- Non-compliance can lead to disciplinary action 📓
#### Standard categories ➕
- General standards (independence, objectivity, competence, due care)
  - Guiding principles for all assignments
  - Cover ethics, independence, objectivity, due care, knowledge, competency, skill
- Performance standards (planning, supervision, evidence, risk)
  - Cover conduct of the assignment
  - Planning and supervision, scoping
  - Risk and materiality
  - Resource mobilization
  - Evidence and professional judgment
- Reporting standards (report content, follow-up)
  - Types of reports
  - Means of communication
  - Information communicated
#### Standards, guidelines and tools hierarchy 📓
- Standards = mandatory 📓
- Guidelines = advisory, support the standards 📓
- Tools and techniques = practical examples 📓
- Tools and techniques do not set requirements
#### Independence and objectivity
- Organizational independence and professional independence ➕
- Independence is impaired if the auditor helped design the audited system 📓⭐
- Conflicts of interest must be disclosed to audit management 📓⭐
- External audit firm independence is the top due-diligence factor 📓
#### Exam note
- Not required to memorize standard numbering
- Exam tests applying standards within the audit process

### 1.1.2 ISACA IS Audit and Assurance Guidelines
- Advisory, not mandatory 📓⭐
- Show how to apply the standards 📓
- Provide recommended practices 📓
- Auditor duties regarding guidelines
  - Consider guidelines when implementing standards
  - Use professional judgment in applying them
  - Justify any departure from standards

### 1.1.3 ISACA Code of Professional Ethics
- Support adoption of standards and governance
- Perform duties with objectivity, diligence and professional care
- Serve stakeholders lawfully and honestly
- Maintain privacy and confidentiality of information
  - Disclosure only if required by legal authority
  - No use for personal benefit
- Maintain competency and continuing education
  - Undertake only activities one can complete competently
- Disclose all material facts
  - Disclose significant facts that would distort results if omitted
- Support professional education of stakeholders
- Exam tests understanding and application, not memorization

### 1.1.4 ITAF™ (IT Assurance Framework)
- Best-practice reference model for IS assurance
- Establishes standards on auditor roles, skills, diligence, conduct, reporting
- Defines IS assurance terms and concepts
- General standards (1000 series) ➕
  - 1001 Audit Charter
  - 1002 Organizational Independence
  - 1003 Auditor Objectivity
  - 1004 Reasonable Expectation
  - 1005 Due Professional Care
  - 1006 Proficiency
  - 1007 Assertions
  - 1008 Criteria
- Performance standards (1200 series) ➕
  - 1201 Risk Assessment in Planning
  - 1204 Materiality
  - 1204 Performance and Supervision
  - 1207 Irregularity and Illegal Acts
- Reporting standards (1400 series) ➕
  - 1401 Reporting
  - 1402 Follow-up Activities
- Guidelines and tools mapped to the standards
- Exam does not test ITAF organization

### 1.1.5 IS Internal Audit Function
- Role set by audit charter approved by board and audit committee
- IS audit may be part of internal audit, independent group, or integrated with financial audit
- Internal audit function reports to audit committee or highest management level
#### Audit charter ⭐
- Purpose, responsibility, authority, accountability
- Approved at the highest level (board or audit committee)
- Defines independence and reporting lines ➕
- States management responsibility, objectives and delegation of authority
- Covers consulting-related services the IS audit function may perform
- Changed only if thoroughly justified
- Engagement letter vs charter
  - Charter: overarching, entire scope of audit activities
  - Engagement letter: specific audit exercise and objective
  - External provider: formal contract or statement of work
#### Management of the IS audit function
- Audit planning and scheduling
- Reporting lines to the audit committee
- Audit committee oversight of audit quality 📓
- Fulfil audit objectives while preserving independence and competence
- Add value to senior management in IT management and business objectives
#### IS audit resource management
- Skills matrix and staffing ➕
- Continuous education to address emerging risks 📓
- Supervision and review of auditor work by experienced staff 📓
- Consider skills and knowledge when assigning staff
- Annual staff training plan aligned to technology direction and risk
- Review training plan periodically
- Provide specialized IT resources (tools, methodology, work programs)
#### Using the services of other auditors and experts
- Reasons: scarcity of IS auditors, need for specialists
- Experts: networking, systems integration, digital forensics, industry specialists
- Considerations before outsourcing audit work
  - Legal restrictions on outsourcing audit/security services
  - Audit charter or contractual stipulations
  - Impact on audit objectives, audit risk, professional liability
  - Independence and objectivity of experts
  - Competence, qualifications, experience
  - Scope and approach of outsourced work
  - Supervisory and audit management controls
  - Method of communicating results
  - Compliance with legal, regulatory and professional standards
- Further considerations by assignment
  - Testimonials, references, background checks
  - Access to systems, premises and records
  - Confidentiality restrictions
  - CAATs and tools used by provider
  - Standards and methodologies for work and documentation
- Third-party (external) IS audit firm
  - Due diligence before appointment: track record, certifications, expertise 📓
  - Independence of the audit firm 📓
  - Service level agreement: scope, expectations, responsibilities 📓
  - Non-disclosure agreement: protects sensitive information 📓
  - Performance monitoring: confirms audit objectives are achieved 📓
- Reliance on the work of experts
  - Related standard 1204 and guideline 2206 on work of experts
  - Review experts' work papers for planning, supervision, documentation
  - Review reports for scope, period, assumptions, management approval
  - Assess impact of significant findings on audit objectives
- Auditor remains responsible for conclusions
  - Professional liability not necessarily delegated
  - Communicate objectives, scope, methodology via formal engagement letter
  - Monitor relationship for objectivity and independence
