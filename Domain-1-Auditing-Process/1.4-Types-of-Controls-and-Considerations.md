# 1.4 Types of Controls and Considerations

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Effective control prevents, detects or contains incident and enables recovery
### 1.4.1 Internal controls
- Policies, procedures, practices and structures
- Provide reasonable assurance of control objectives
- Operate at all levels of the organization
- Board and senior management set culture and monitor effectiveness
- Control activities
  - Approvals and authorizations
  - Verifications and reconciliations
  - Reviewing operating performance
  - Securing assets
  - Separation of duties
- May be manual or automated
### 1.4.2 Control objectives and control measures
- Control objective: desired result of control activities
- Control objective is tied to company strategy
- Control measure: activity contributing to a control objective
- Objectives relate to
  - Effectiveness and efficiency of operations
  - Reliability of financial reporting
  - Compliance with laws and regulations
  - Safeguarding information assets
- Same objectives in manual and IS environments, different implementation
- Audit objective vs control objective
  - Audit objective: goal the audit must accomplish
  - Control objective: how an internal control should function
#### IS control objectives
- Safeguard assets
- Maintain data integrity
- Achieve system effectiveness and efficiency
- Comply with laws and policies
- Management choices on objectives
  - Select applicable ones
  - Decide which to implement
  - Choose how (frequency, span, automation)
  - Accept risk of not implementing others
- Specific IS control objectives
  - Safeguard assets: up-to-date, secure from improper access
  - SDLC processes repeatable, reliable, aligned to business
  - Integrity of OS environments, network management
  - Integrity of critical application environments
  - Identification and authentication of users
  - Efficiency and effectiveness of operations
  - Compliance with user requirements, policies, laws
  - Availability via BCP and DRP with backup and recovery
  - Incident response plan to protect data and systems
  - Change management for integrity and reliability
  - Outsourced services with defined SLAs and contract terms
- Application integrity objectives
  - Authorization of input: each transaction authorized, entered once
  - Validation of input
  - Accuracy and completeness of processing, proper period
  - Reliability of overall processing
  - Accuracy, completeness, security of output
  - Database confidentiality, integrity, availability
#### General control methods
- Governance and organizational controls
- Physical and logical access controls
- Managerial (administrative) controls
  - Oversight, reporting, procedures, operations of a process
  - Policies and procedures
  - Accounting controls (balancing)
  - Employee training and development
  - Compliance reporting
- Technical (logical) controls
  - Provided through technology, equipment or devices
  - Need proper managerial controls to operate correctly
  - Firewall rulesets
  - Network or host-based IDS
  - Passwords
  - Antimalware
- Physical controls
  - Physically restrict access to facility or hardware
  - Need maintenance, monitoring, ability to react to alerts
  - Access badges and locks
  - CCTV
- Balance control types
  - Firewall needs training, procedures, monitoring, testing
  - Missing coinciding controls give false sense of security
- Examples of general controls
  - Systems development methodologies and change control
  - Operations procedures
  - Systems programming and technical support
  - QA procedures
  - BCP/DRP
  - Networks and communication technology
  - Database administration
  - Protective and detective mechanisms against attacks
#### IS-specific controls
- Application controls
- Infrastructure and operations controls
- Each general method translates to IS-specific control
- Examples
  - Strategy and direction of IT function
  - General organization and management of IT function
  - Access to IT resources, including data and programs
#### Business process applications and controls
- Input, processing, output controls (see 3.4)
- Controls embedded in business application
- Assurance evaluates process and activity levels
  - Management, programmed and manual controls
- Evaluate process owner controls: SoD, periodic reviews, access approvals
- Computerized application audit challenges
  - Limited audit trails
  - Instantaneous updating
  - Information overload
- Sample application risks and controls
  - Ecommerce: SQL injection; secure coding training, code reviews, input validity checks
  - EDI: interception, manipulation; encryption
  - Email: social engineering; spam filtering, hyperlink verification, phishing training
  - ICS (SCADA, DCS, PLC): human-life impact; network segmentation, multifactor authentication
  - AI and expert systems: inherent bias; verify expertise behind assumptions
- Use authoritative guidance such as OWASP for ecommerce
- Bring external experts where skills are missing
### 1.4.3 Control classifications ⭐
- Elements considered when evaluating control strength
- Preventive controls generally stronger at mitigating risk
- Detective and preventive reduce likelihood; corrective mitigates consequences
- Mix of control types complements each other
#### Preventive controls 📓
- Stop errors, fraud or omissions proactively 📓
- Segregation of duties 📓
- Using qualified resources 📓
- Well-designed documentation 📓
- Inhibit or impede attempts to violate security policy
- Encryption, user authentication, vault-construction doors
- Multifactor authentication stops compromised password login
#### Detective controls 📓
- Identify errors after they occur 📓
- Checkpoints in production jobs 📓
- Echo messages in telecom protocols 📓
- Hidden surveillance cameras 📓
- Provide visibility into issues 📓
- Warn of violations without impeding actions
- Audit trails, IDS, checksums
#### Corrective controls 📓
- Mitigate damage after a threat occurs 📓
- Correct errors and let processing continue 📓
- Check routine that fixes errors automatically 📓
- Remediate errors, omissions, unauthorized use, intrusions
- Data backups, error correction, automated failover
#### Deterrent controls 📓
- Discourage undesirable actions 📓
- Warning signs and visible barriers 📓
- Visible cameras deter; hidden cameras only detect 📓
- Warning banners on login screens
- Acceptable use policies
- Rewards for arrest of hackers
#### Compensating controls 📓
- Offset the lack of another control
- Example: logging and independent review of developer production access 📓
- Used when baseline controls cannot meet requirement
- Isolated network segment with strong perimeter security
- Third-party challenge-response for devices without individual logins
- Make exploitation harder without addressing the vulnerability
#### Recovery controls 📓➕
- Restoring a server from backup media 📓
#### Directive controls ➕
- Policies and procedures that direct behavior
#### Control purpose
- Reduce likelihood
  - Infrastructure monitoring
  - Capacity management
  - Risk management
  - Configuration management
- Mitigate consequences
  - Backup and recovery
  - BC or IT DR plan
  - Service desk
  - Special clauses in vendor contracts
  - Spare processing site
### 1.4.4 Control relationship to risk
- Risk addressed through control; control justified by risk
- Map management's controls back to applicable risk
- Management documents and implements controls per risk assessment
- Risk understanding sets audit procedures and weakness materiality
- Vulnerability: weakness a threat can exploit 📓
- Lack of control measures indicates vulnerability 📓⭐
- Next step after finding vulnerabilities: assess threats and likelihood 📓
- Inherent vs residual risk 📓
- Add controls if risk exceeds tolerance
- Compensating control must achieve same result as the underperforming control
- Non-IT compensating controls can exist
- Risk responses
  - Avoidance (move data center out of flood zone) 📓⭐
  - Reduction/mitigation (lowers probability, activity continues) 📓
  - Transfer
  - Acceptance (needs documented senior approval) 📓⭐
- Senior business management owns risk levels 📓
### 1.4.5 Prescriptive controls and frameworks
- Prescriptive control sets give a standard set of controls to implement and assess
- Examples listed in the ORM
  - CIS 18 Critical Security Controls: prioritized cybersecurity best practices
  - OWASP SAMM: open framework for software security strategy
  - SOC reports (AICPA): service organization controls framework
  - PCI DSS: requirements for credit card data security
  - CSA Cloud Controls Matrix (CCM): cloud security control framework
- Non-applicable prescriptive controls need documented reasons and validation
- Also relevant: COBIT, ISO 27001, NIST (see 2.2 and 5.1) ➕
- Prescriptive control sets give standard controls to implement and assess
- CIS 18 Critical Security Controls: prioritized best practices
- OWASP SAMM: software security strategy framework
- SOC reports: AICPA framework for service organizations
- PCI DSS: requirements for credit card data handling
- CSA Cloud Controls Matrix: cloud security control framework
- Document reasons and validation when controls are not applicable
### 1.4.6 Evaluation of the control environment
- Management control monitoring
  - Identifies deviations before less frequent audits
  - Ensures requirements met, standards followed, policies complied with
  - Auditor reviews management monitoring
    - Exceptions remediated, lessons learned applied
    - Metrics based on management risk assessment
    - Metrics identify specific quantifiable outputs
    - Independence considered for completeness and accuracy
    - Thresholds for effectiveness tracked over time
- Independent evaluation of the control environment
  - Review evidence to judge if well controlled and effective
  - Assess strengths and weaknesses against control objectives
  - Requires judgment and experience
