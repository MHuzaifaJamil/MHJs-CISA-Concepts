# 1.2 Types of Audits, Assessments and Reviews

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Audits give higher assurance than assessments and reviews
- Assessments and reviews carry less stigma, focus on improvement opportunities
- May be performed by internal or external groups
### 1.2.0 Audit types
- Internal vs external audit
- Financial audit
  - Assesses accuracy of financial reporting
  - Often detailed substantive testing
  - Relates to integrity and reliability of financial information
- Operational audit
  - Evaluates internal control structure of a process or area
  - Example: IS audit of application controls or logical security
- Compliance audit
  - Tests controls for adherence to regulations, standards or practices
  - May focus on particular systems or data
- Integrated audit
  - Combines financial and operational audit steps
  - May or may not include an IS auditor
  - Includes compliance tests and substantive steps
- Forensic audit
  - Discovers, discloses and follows up on fraud and crime
  - Develops evidence for law enforcement and judicial review
- IS (IT) audit
  - Evaluates safeguarding of systems and resources
  - Evaluates data and system integrity and availability
  - Evaluates reliable information and efficient resource use
  - Evaluates internal controls give reasonable assurance
- Third-party assurance reports (SOC 1, SOC 2, SOC 3) ➕
- Administrative audit: efficiency of operational productivity
- Specialized audit: examples are fraud or third-party services
- Third-party service audit
  - Audits outsourced processes at service providers
  - Issues opinion on service organization's description of controls
  - Delivered as service auditor's report
- Fraud audit: specialized, uses tools and data analysis for schemes
- Computer forensic audit
  - Analyzes electronic devices to gather and preserve evidence
  - IS auditor can verify compliance with evidence collection procedures
- Functional audit
  - Independent evaluation of software against requirement specifications
  - Done before delivery or after implementation
- Readiness assessment
  - Reviews current compliance state with documented standards
  - Focuses on control design, not operating effectiveness
  - Yields remediation items before a formal audit
### 1.2.1 Control Self-Assessment (CSA)
- Assessment of controls by staff and management of the unit
- Methodology to review key objectives, assess risk, ensure controls manage risk
- Formal, documented, collaborative process
- Implementation methods
  - Questionnaires and surveys
  - Facilitated workshops
  - Informal peer reviews
- Facilitator role in workshops: supports decision-making
- Process owners evaluate controls against objectives and risk appetite
- Objectives
  - Leverage internal audit by shifting control monitoring to functional areas
  - Enhance, not replace, audit responsibilities
  - Educate management on control design and monitoring, high-risk areas
  - Measure success for each phase (planning, implementation, monitoring)
  - Critical success factor: meeting with business unit representatives
- Benefits
  - Early detection of risk
  - More effective and improved internal controls
  - Cohesive teams through employee involvement
  - Sense of control ownership, less resistance
  - Increased awareness of objectives, risk and controls
  - Better communication between operational and top management
  - Increased employee motivation
  - Improved audit rating process
  - Reduction in control cost
  - Assurance to stakeholders, customers and top management
- Disadvantages
  - Could be mistaken for audit function replacement
  - May be seen as additional workload
  - Failure to act on suggestions damages morale
  - Lack of audit knowledge may limit weak-control detection
- IS auditor's role in CSA
  - Facilitator and internal control professional
  - Management client is the participant
  - Must understand the business process assessed
  - Guides auditees using risk-based control objectives
  - Explains risk when managers suggest removing preventive controls
### 1.2.2 Integrated Auditing
- Combines financial, operational and IT audit
- Auditor works with business process specialists
- Appropriate audit disciplines combined to assess key controls with a risk focus
- Process steps
  - Identify risk for the area audited
  - Identify relevant key controls
  - Review and understand design of key controls
  - Test IT system support for key controls
  - Test operational effectiveness of management controls
  - Combined report or opinion on control risk, design, weaknesses
- IS auditor identifies risk in information management, IT infrastructure, IT governance, IT operations
- Team discusses emerging risk, impact and likelihood
- IT systems often form first line of preventive and detective controls
- Benefits
  - Single audit and one comprehensive report
  - Staff development and retention through variety
  - Process owners see link between controls and audit procedures
  - Top management sees control effectiveness and IT resource use
  - Shareholders see governance link to reliable financial statements
