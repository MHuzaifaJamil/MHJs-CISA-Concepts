# 1.9 Reporting and Communication Techniques

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Communication skills affect reporting effectiveness
- Needed techniques
  - Facilitation
  - Negotiation
  - Conflict resolution
- Understand materiality of findings
- Consider recipients' motivations and perspectives
### 1.9.1 Communicating audit results
- Discuss findings with management first 📓⭐
- Exit interview at end of audit
  - Ensure facts are correct and material
  - Ensure recommendations are realistic and cost-effective
  - Recommend implementation dates
- Auditor answers to senior management and audit committee
- Denying access by lower levels limits independence
- Gain agreement on findings and corrective action
- On disagreement, explain significance and risk of not correcting
- Auditee may request implementation help
  - Explain auditor vs consultant role
  - Assisting may harm independence
- Brief senior auditee management after agreement
- Periodic summary to audit committee
  - Independent route for sensitive findings
### 1.9.2 Audit report objectives
- Six objectives
  - Formally present results to auditee and client
  - Serve as formal closure of the engagement
  - Provide assurance statements and recommendations
  - Serve as reference for future researchers
  - Basis for follow-up audit
  - Promote audit credibility
- Based on stakeholder requirements, standards and protocols
- Unbiased, well-supported responses to audit objectives
- Standards 1401 Reporting and 1402 Follow-up
### 1.9.3 Audit report structure and contents
- Introduction
  - Audit objectives and limitations
  - Scope and period of coverage
  - Nature and extent of procedures
  - Methodology and guidelines statement
- Audit findings in separate sections
  - Grouped by materiality or recipient
- Overall conclusion and opinion on adequacy of controls
  - Actual potential risk from deficiencies
- Reservations or qualifications
- Detailed findings and recommendations
  - Level of detail depends on recipient
  - Minor findings may go by memorandum
- Describe finding, cause and risk
- Separate document for highly confidential matters
- Identify criteria applied (organizational, professional, governmental)
- Issue in a timely manner
- Communicate significant findings early without altering report content
- Auditor decides final content
- Balanced report includes positive comments
- Exercise independence in reporting
- Management response
  - States corrective actions and timing
  - Obtain implementation date commitment
  - Constraints: staff, budgets, other projects
### 1.9.4 Audit documentation
- Written record supporting the auditor's report
- Demonstrates compliance with standards
- Supports basis for conclusions
- Minimum contents
  - Planning and preparation of scope and objectives
  - Description or walk-throughs of audit area
  - Audit program
  - Steps performed and evidence gathered
  - Use of other auditors and experts
  - Findings, conclusions and recommendations
  - Document identification and dates
- Recommended: copy of report, evidence of supervisory review
- Clear, complete, retrievable, comprehensible
- Generally property of auditee
- External access needs senior management and legal counsel approval
- Policies on custody, retention and release
- Work papers: dated, initialed, page-numbered, labeled, filed
- Materiality decides which findings go in the report
  - Significant locally may not be material to headquarters
  - Deprovisioning caught by management review: document, not escalate
### 1.9.5 Follow-up activities
- Assess whether the auditee's corrective action was appropriate 📓
- Follow-up program verifies agreed actions
- Timing depends on criticality and judgment
- Level ranges from inquiry to performing audit steps
- Communicate results to appropriate management
- External auditors follow up if agreed with auditee
### 1.9.6 Types of IS audit reports
- Driven by engagement type and standards requirements
- Separate confidential security report for technical detail
- Engagement types
  - Review
  - Examination (audit): stronger evidence for opinion
  - Agreed-upon procedures: opinion may not be expressed
- Scope narrower for agreed-upon procedures
- Client may set report content and format
