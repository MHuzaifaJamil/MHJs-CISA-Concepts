# 1.7 Audit Evidence Collection Techniques

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Evidence: information used to decide if criteria are met
- Conclusions need sufficient, relevant and competent evidence
### 1.7.0 Evidence
- Sufficient, reliable, relevant and useful
- Reliability hierarchy ⭐
- Types of evidence
  - Auditor's observations
  - Interview notes
  - Results of independent third-party assessors
  - Correspondence, internal documents, partner contracts
  - Results of audit test procedures
- Determinants of reliability
  - Independence of provider: outside sources more reliable
    - Confirmation letters for receivables
    - Signed external contracts if originals seen
  - Qualifications of provider
  - Objectivity: objective evidence more reliable than judgment-based
    - Direct review of media inventory is objective
  - Timing: evidence may not be retrievable later
    - Uncontrolled spreadsheets change
- Appropriate (quality) and sufficient (quantity) per IFAC
- Competent evidence is reliable and relevant
- Focus on overall audit objectives
### 1.7.1 Techniques
- Reviewing IS organization structures
  - Adequate SoD is a key general control
  - Distributed processing and end-user computing change structures
- Reviewing IS policies and procedures
  - Confirm they exist, are understood and are followed
  - Management responsible for formulating and controlling policies
  - Periodic reviews for appropriateness
- Reviewing IS standards
- Reviewing IS documentation
  - Evaluate controls preserving electronic document integrity
  - Feasibility studies and initiating documents
  - External supplier documentation
  - SLAs with IT providers
  - Functional requirements and design specifications
  - Test plans and reports
  - Program and operations documents
  - Program change logs
  - User and operations manuals
  - Security plans and risk assessments
  - BCPs
  - QA reports
  - Security metrics reports
  - CASE and prototyping may automate documentation
- Inquiry and interviewing
  - Organize in advance with clear objectives
  - Fixed outline, interview form or checklist
  - Never accusatory
  - Verify notes with interviewee
- Observation of personnel performing their duties
  - Be unobtrusive and document in detail
  - Interim report if observation not timely
  - Photographs can support evidence
  - Identifies
    - Actual functions
    - Actual processes and procedures
    - Security awareness
    - Reporting relationships and SoD
  - Compare results with logical access rights
  - Drawback: observed staff may change behavior
- Inspection
- Re-performance and recalculation
  - Actual performance of control in real time
  - Better evidence than other techniques
  - Used when inquiry, observation and examination are insufficient
- Walk-through 📓
  - Confirms understanding of controls
  - Helps identify evidence to collect
- Confirmation
- Examination includes testing of controls
