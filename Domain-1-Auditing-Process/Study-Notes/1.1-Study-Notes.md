## 1.1 Opening Principles

### Why standards matter
- **IS audit** (information systems audit)
  - Definition: independent review of systems, controls and processes against criteria.
  - Use: gives assurance that IT supports business goals safely.
  - Audit impact: conclusions are only trusted if the method is credible.
- Credibility of an audit depends on adherence to accepted standards.
- ISACA publishes standards, guidelines and a code of ethics for this purpose.
- **Code of ethics**
  - Definition: written rules for professional and personal conduct.
  - Use: guides members and certification holders in daily decisions.
  - Audit impact: breach damages trust in the auditor and the profession.
- Ethics code covers conduct of members and certification holders, not only auditors.

## 1.1.1 ISACA IS Audit and Assurance Standards

### Basics
- **Standard**
  - Definition: a mandatory requirement for performing and reporting audit work.
  - Use: sets a common quality floor for every engagement.
  - Audit impact: skipping a standard weakens the audit and the auditor's position.
- Standards speak to three audiences.
  - IS auditors: the minimum acceptable performance level.
  - Management and others: the profession's expectations of practitioners.
  - CISA holders: their professional performance requirements.
- Example: a stakeholder asks what quality to expect; standards state the baseline.

### Nature of standards
- Mandatory for ISACA members 📓⭐
- Define the minimum acceptable performance level 📓
- Non-compliance can lead to disciplinary action 📓
- ⚠ Check: "Disciplinary action" comes from your notes, not this manual passage.
- Minimum level is a floor, not a target of excellence.

### Standard categories ➕
- ⚠ Check: Manual does support these three categories; the ➕ mark can be removed.
- **General standards**
  - Definition: guiding principles under which the assurance profession operates.
  - Use: apply to the conduct of all assignments.
  - Audit impact: failures here (independence, due care) undermine the whole audit.
  - Cover ethics, independence, objectivity, due care, knowledge, competency, skill.
- **Performance standards**
  - Definition: rules on how an assignment is actually carried out.
  - Use: direct planning, supervision, scoping and evidence work.
  - Audit impact: gaps here produce unsupported conclusions.
  - Cover planning and supervision, scoping, risk and materiality.
  - Also cover resource mobilization and assignment management.
  - Also cover audit evidence and professional judgment with due care.
- **Reporting standards**
  - Definition: rules on how results are communicated.
  - Use: define report types, communication means and content.
  - Audit impact: unclear reporting hides findings from decision makers.
- Memory aid: general is who, performance is what, reporting is say.

### Standards, guidelines and tools hierarchy 📓
- Standards = mandatory 📓
- Guidelines = advisory, support the standards 📓
- **Tools and techniques**
  - Definition: sample processes an auditor might follow in an engagement.
  - Use: show one way to meet a standard.
  - Audit impact: they never set requirements, so no breach arises from skipping them.
- Practical examples, not obligations 📓
- Example: a sampling worksheet template is a tool; using it is optional.
- Exam traps
  - Correct logic: only standards are mandatory; guidelines and tools are supportive.
  - Distractor "guidelines are mandatory" is wrong: they are advisory.
  - Distractor "standards are optional" is wrong: members must comply.
  - Distractor "mandatory only for certain regulated audits" is wrong: scope is not limited.

### Independence and objectivity
- **Independence**
  - Definition: freedom from conflicts of interest and undue influence.
  - Use: lets the auditor reach unbiased conclusions.
  - Audit impact: lost independence makes findings unreliable.
- **Objectivity**
  - Definition: impartial mental attitude in all engagement matters.
  - Use: keeps judgment free from bias.
  - Audit impact: conflicts of interest can impair it.
- Organizational independence and professional independence ➕
- ⚠ Check: Manual names "Organizational Independence" (1002) but never defines a "professional independence" pair.
- Independence is impaired if the auditor helped design the audited system 📓⭐
- Example: a consultant who built a payroll system should not audit it.
- Conflicts of interest must be disclosed to audit management 📓⭐
- External audit firm independence is the top due-diligence factor 📓
- ⚠ Check: Manual lists independence among outsourcing considerations but never ranks it "top".
- ⚠ Check: Ranking comes from practice questions; track record also matters before appointment.
- Exam traps
  - Correct logic: involvement in design is a threat to independence.
  - Fix is disclosure to audit management, not silent self-review.
  - Distractor "auditor's expertise offsets conflict" is wrong: skill does not restore independence.
  - Distractor "resolve it informally with the auditee" is wrong: disclosure goes to audit management.

### Exam note
- Standard numbering need not be memorized.
- Exam tests applying standards within the audit process.

## 1.1.2 ISACA IS Audit and Assurance Guidelines

- **Guideline**
  - Definition: advice on how to comply with the standards.
  - Use: helps auditors choose practical ways to implement standards.
  - Audit impact: ignoring one is allowed, but the departure needs a reason.
- Advisory, not mandatory 📓⭐
- Show how to apply the standards 📓
- Provide recommended practices 📓
- Auditor duties regarding guidelines
  - Consider guidelines when deciding how to implement standards.
  - Use professional judgment applying them to specific audits.
  - Justify any departure from the standards.
- ⚠ Check: Manual says justify departure "from the standards"; guidelines themselves are not mandatory.
- Example: the auditor uses a different test approach and documents why.
- Exam traps
  - Correct logic: guidelines flex with judgment; standards do not flex.
  - Distractor "guideline departure triggers discipline" is wrong: only standards are mandatory.

## 1.1.3 ISACA Code of Professional Ethics

- Applies to ISACA members and certification holders.
- Duty 1: support adoption of standards and good governance practices.
  - Governance scope includes audit, control, security and risk management.
- Duty 2: perform with objectivity, due diligence and professional care.
- Duty 3: serve stakeholders lawfully, with high conduct, not discrediting the profession.
- **Confidentiality** (duty 4)
  - Definition: keeping information gained in work from improper disclosure.
  - Use: protects clients' sensitive data.
  - Audit impact: misuse harms the client and the auditor's standing.
  - Disclose only if required by legal authority.
  - Never use for personal benefit or release to inappropriate parties.
  - Example: an auditor cannot trade on unpublished merger information.
- **Competency** (duty 5)
  - Definition: having needed skills, knowledge and ability.
  - Use: limits the work an auditor accepts.
  - Audit impact: unqualified work yields unreliable results.
  - Maintain competency through continuing education.
  - Undertake only activities one can reasonably expect to complete competently.
- **Disclosure of material facts** (duty 6)
  - Definition: telling appropriate parties significant facts known to you.
  - Use: keeps reported results accurate.
  - Audit impact: omission can distort results and mislead decisions.
  - Inform appropriate parties of the results of work performed.
- Duty 7: support professional education of stakeholders on governance topics.
- Exam tests understanding and application, not memorization.
- Exam traps
  - Correct logic: confidentiality yields only to legal authority.
  - Distractor "client request to hide facts" is wrong: significant facts must be disclosed.
  - Distractor "accept work to gain experience" is wrong: competency limits what you accept.

## 1.1.5 IS Internal Audit Function

### Basics
- **Internal audit function**
  - Definition: in-house team giving independent assurance on risk and controls.
  - Use: evaluates governance, controls and IT for the organization.
  - Audit impact: only credible if independent of those audited.
- Role set by an audit charter approved by board and audit committee.
- If no such bodies exist, senior management approves.
- Professionals need a clear mandate, which the charter may express.
- IS audit may be part of internal audit.
  - Or an independent group.
  - Or integrated within financial and operational audit.
- Charter may treat IS audit as an audit support function.
- Function reports to audit committee, or highest management level such as the board.
- ⚠ Check: Manual says "audit committee, if one exists" else highest management level.

### Audit charter ⭐
- **Audit charter**
  - Definition: formal document setting the audit function's mandate.
  - Use: authorizes the function and defines its scope.
  - Audit impact: without it, authority and independence are weak.
- Covers purpose, responsibility, authority and accountability.
- Approved at the highest level (board or audit committee).
- Defines independence and reporting lines ➕
- ⚠ Check: Manual does not list this as charter content; reporting line is separate.
- States management responsibility, objectives and delegation of authority.
- Covers consulting-related services the IS audit function may perform.
- Changed only if thoroughly justified.
- Example: the board approves a charter before the team starts any review.
- **Engagement letter**
  - Definition: document for one specific audit exercise and objective.
  - Use: confirms scope and terms for that engagement.
  - Audit impact: avoids misunderstanding about what will be audited.
- Charter is overarching and covers the entire scope of audit activities.
- Engagement letter focuses on one audit with a specific objective.
- Responsibility, authority and accountability may be documented in either document.
- External provider: formal contract or statement of work.
- Exam traps
  - Correct logic: the charter is approved by the highest level, not by auditors.
  - Distractor "audit manager approves the charter" is wrong: auditors cannot approve their own mandate.
  - Distractor "engagement letter replaces the charter" is wrong: different scope levels.

### Management of the IS audit function
- Audit planning and scheduling
- Reporting lines to the audit committee
- Audit committee oversight of audit quality 📓
- ⚠ Check: Manual does not list planning, scheduling or reporting lines here.
- ⚠ Check: Audit committee oversight comes from your notes; manual stresses independence reporting.
- Fulfil audit objectives while preserving independence and competence.
- Add value to senior management in IT management and business objectives.

### IS audit resource management
- **Competency maintenance**
  - Definition: keeping technical skills current as technology changes.
  - Use: lets auditors assess new technologies reliably.
  - Audit impact: outdated skills raise the risk of missed issues.
- Skills matrix and staffing ➕
- ⚠ Check: Manual has no skills matrix; it says weigh skills when assigning.
- Continuous education to address emerging risks 📓
- Supervision and review of auditor work by experienced staff 📓
- ⚠ Check: Supervision is a performance standard topic, not stated in this resource passage.
- Consider skills and knowledge when planning audits and assigning staff.
- Annual staff training plan aligned to technology direction and risk.
- Review the training plan periodically.
- Provide specialized IT resources such as tools, methodology and work programs.
- Related: standard 1006 Proficiency.

### Using the services of other auditors and experts
- **Outsourcing audit work**
  - Definition: assigning part or all of audit work to outside parties.
  - Use: fills skill gaps and capacity shortages.
  - Audit impact: liability is not necessarily transferred with the work.
- Reasons: scarcity of IS auditors and need for specialists.
- Experts: networking, systems integration, digital forensics.
- Subject-matter experts: banking, securities trading, insurance, privacy, law.
- Example: a bank hires a forensics specialist for a fraud review.
- Considerations before outsourcing
  - Legal restrictions on outsourcing audit or security services.
  - Audit charter or contractual stipulations.
  - Impact on audit objectives, audit risk and professional liability.
  - Independence and objectivity of experts.
  - Competence, qualifications and experience.
  - Scope and approach of the outsourced work.
  - Supervisory and audit management controls.
  - Method of communicating results.
  - Compliance with legal, regulatory and professional standards.
- Further considerations by assignment
  - Testimonials, references and background checks.
  - Access to systems, premises and records.
  - Confidentiality restrictions protecting customer information.
  - CAATs and tools used by the provider.
  - Standards and methodologies for work and documentation.
  - Nondisclosure agreements.

### Third-party (external) IS audit firm
- **Due diligence**
  - Definition: checking a provider's fitness before appointing it.
  - Use: confirms track record, certifications and expertise.
  - Audit impact: weak vetting risks poor or biased audit results.
- Due diligence before appointment: track record, certifications, expertise 📓
- Independence of the audit firm 📓
- **Service level agreement (SLA)**
  - Definition: contract stating scope, expectations and responsibilities.
  - Use: sets clear terms with the audit firm.
  - Audit impact: unclear terms cause disputes and coverage gaps.
- SLA: scope, expectations, responsibilities 📓
- ⚠ Check: Manual names a contract, statement of work and engagement letter, not SLA.
- **Non-disclosure agreement (NDA)**
  - Definition: legal promise not to reveal sensitive information.
  - Use: protects data the audit firm will access.
  - Audit impact: breach can expose confidential client information.
- NDA: protects sensitive information 📓
- **Performance monitoring**
  - Definition: ongoing review of the provider's work against objectives.
  - Use: confirms audit objectives are achieved.
  - Audit impact: unmonitored work may lose objectivity or quality.
- Performance monitoring: confirms audit objectives are achieved 📓
- ⚠ Check: Manual says monitor planning, supervision, review, documentation, plus objectivity and independence.
- Exam traps
  - Correct logic: match each control to its purpose.
  - Independence is the key trust factor; cost and location are secondary.
  - SLA clarifies expectations; NDA protects information; monitoring confirms objectives.
  - Distractor "outsourcing reduces auditor accountability" is wrong: liability stays.
  - Distractor "NDA proves independence" is wrong: it only protects confidentiality.

### Reliance on the work of experts
- Related: standard 1204 and guideline 2206 on work of experts.
- ⚠ Check: Manual pairs 1204 Performance and Supervision with guideline 2206.
- Review experts' work papers to confirm planning, supervision, documentation and review.
- Also judge appropriateness and sufficiency of the evidence provided.
- Review reports for scope, audit period, assumptions and management approval.
- Assess usefulness of reports and impact of significant findings on objectives.

### Auditor remains responsible for conclusions
- Professional liability is not necessarily delegated.
- Communicate objectives, scope and methodology via formal engagement letter.
- Establish regular monitoring of the provider's work.
- Monitor the relationship for objectivity and independence throughout.
- Example: an expert's flawed report still reflects on the engaging auditor.
- Exam trap: delegating work does not delegate the responsibility for conclusions.

## Takeaway
- Standards are mandatory; guidelines advise; tools and techniques only illustrate.
- Independence is lost when auditors design or build what they audit.
- Disclose conflicts to audit management and report all significant facts.
- The highest level approves the charter; engagement letters cover single audits.
- Outsourced audit work keeps liability with the engaging auditor.
- Exam tests applying standards and ethics, not memorizing numbers.
