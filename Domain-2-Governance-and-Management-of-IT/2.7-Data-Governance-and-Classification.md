# 2.7 Data Governance and Classification

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Detailed inventory of information assets is the first step
- Review and update inventory and classification regularly
- Classification sets sensitivity and criticality levels
- Defines access controls per level; consistent security requirements
- Enables cost-benefit analysis; avoids overspending on protection
- Information owner decides classification per policy
- Keep classification simple; three to five levels typical
- Classification defines
  - Importance of asset
  - Asset owner
  - Process for granting access
  - Approver of access rights and levels
  - Extent and depth of security controls
- Consider legal, regulatory, contractual, internal requirements
- Separates production data from test data access
- Unlabeled media indicate potential misuse; social engineering risk
#### Classification examples
- Public: freely accessible (marketing material, press releases)
- Internal: limited to authorized employees (plans, org charts)
- Confidential: kept private (financials, customer lists, card data, contracts)
- Restricted: highly sensitive, legal fines possible (proprietary, regulated data)
### 2.7.1 Data inventory and classification
- Full inventory is the most important resource for PIA
- Includes types of personal information collected
- Enterprise-wide and current scope
- Consistent with metadata repository, managed by data governance
- Privacy and data governance manage jointly
- Consider unintentional capture of personal data
### 2.7.2 Legal purpose, consent and legitimate interest
- Cover how personal information is obtained, used, shared, retained, accessed
- GDPR has more processing bases than these three
#### Legal purpose
- Specify purpose in privacy notice, with permissible legal basis
- Align later use with purpose and consents
- Consult authorities on legitimate purpose where needed
- Member states may add requirements; know all countries involved
#### Consent
- Obtain implicit or explicit consent per regulation
- Obtain before collection
- Obtain before use beyond original purpose
- Obtain before transfer to third parties or other jurisdictions
- Cover sheet on electronic consent transmissions
- Privacy engineers record consents, withdrawals and denials
- Support decisions on use or nonuse and use limitation
- GDPR and HIPAA specify consent requirements
#### Legitimate interest
- Basis for lawful processing without consent
- Individuals' rights must not override enterprise's, given reasonable expectations
- Relationship: client, customer, patient, employee
- Examples: tax filings, public health statistics, forensic analysis of hacking
- Assess purpose, necessity and balance elements
- Involve legal counsel, security officer, privacy officer
- GDPR is predominant regulation requiring it
### 2.7.3 Data subject rights
- Rights vary by law; identify and document those that apply
- NIST Privacy Framework functions
  - Control-P: manage data processing with granularity
  - Communicate-P: reliable understanding and dialog about processing
- Control-P categories
  - CT.PO-P: data processing policies, processes, procedures
  - CT.DM-P: data processing management
  - CT.DP-P: disassociated processing
- Communicate-P categories
  - CM.PO-P: communication policies, processes, procedures
  - CM.AW-P: data processing awareness
- Transborder data flow
  - Data transmission between two countries
  - Laws of origin or destination may apply
  - Concerns: legal compliance, security, integrity, privacy
  - Conflicting privacy laws between countries
  - Some countries regulate encryption of transborder data
  - Internet routing may cross borders unexpectedly
