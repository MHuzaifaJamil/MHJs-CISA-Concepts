## 2.11 Introduction (QA program and why it matters)

### Quality
- Definition: how well a product or service meets stated requirements.
- Use: gives a measurable target for IT products and processes.
- Audit impact: auditors need a defined requirement to test conformance against.

### QA program
- Definition: a planned, organized set of actions giving confidence that requirements are met.
- Use: wraps QA policies, procedures and processes into one managed system.
- Audit impact: the manual ties IT process integrity and reliability to QA.
- Audit impact: absent or weak QA lowers confidence in all IT process outputs.
- The IS auditor must understand QA concepts, structures, roles and responsibilities.
- Example: a bank's QA program requires tested, approved changes before every release.

## 2.11.1 Quality assurance

### Basics: QA and QC are different
- The two terms are often used as if they mean the same thing.
- They are not identical; the manual defines each separately.
- Simple memory aid: QA is about process, QC is about product.
- ⚠ Check: outline implies a clean split; manual has QC checking processing accuracy too.

### Quality assurance (QA)
- Definition: planned, systematic actions that build confidence requirements will be met.
- Use: helps IT make sure staff follow prescribed quality processes.
- Audit impact: tests whether procedures exist, are known and are followed.
- Example: QA sets up ISO 9001-compliant procedures to spread quality practices.
- ISO 9001 is a well-known quality management standard; further detail is ➕ unverified here.
- QA develops, promulgates and maintains standards for the IT function.
- QA also trains staff in QA standards and procedures.
- Audit impact: check that standards are current, published and taught.

### QA role in change control
- QA personnel verify system changes are authorized before reaching production.
- They also verify changes are tested and implemented in a controlled manner.
- This follows the enterprise change and release management policy.
- Use: stops unreviewed code from entering the live environment.
- Audit impact: sample changes and trace each to approval and test evidence.
- Example: a hotfix reaches production with no test record, which is a QA failure.

### Source code management tools
- Definition: software that stores code, tracks versions and controls who can change it.
- Use: lets QA oversee developer access, program versions and source code integrity.
- Audit impact: review version history, access lists and unauthorized-change alerts.
- Segregating developer access to production protects the live environment from untested code.
- ⚠ Check: outline says developer segregation; manual says segregated developer access to production.

### Quality control (QC)
- Definition: observation techniques and activities used to meet quality requirements.
- Use: runs tests and reviews to show software is defect-free.
- Use: also confirms the software meets user expectations.
- Timing: QC may occur at various stages but must finish before production.
- Audit impact: absence of pre-production QC evidence is a significant finding.
- Example: QC checks programs and documentation follow standards and naming conventions.
- QC periodically checks accuracy and authenticity of input, processing and output.
- ⚠ Check: the outline drops "authenticity" and "meets user expectations"; the manual includes both.

### QA independence
- Definition: QA reports and operates apart from the people whose work it reviews.
- Use: lets QA judge honestly without pressure from development or operations.
- Audit impact: check QA reporting lines and any pressure on QA results.
- QA may sit inside a larger control entity in some enterprises.
- Small enterprises may lack a separate QA function.
- There, one person may hold more than one role.
- Under no circumstances may a person review their own work.
- Reviewers must not hold a role creating a segregation of duties conflict.
- Example: a DBA must not quality-review changes that affect the database.
- Segregation of duties: splitting tasks so no one person controls a whole process.
- Audit impact: user notes classify segregation of duties as a preventive control.

### Exam traps for 2.11.1
- Correct logic: independence and no self-review outrank staffing convenience.
- Trap: "small firm may let the author review" is wrong; compensate with other reviewers.
- Trap: QA tests the software; wrong, testing and defect checks are QC.
- Trap: QC sets enterprise standards; wrong, QA develops and maintains standards.
- Trap: a DBA reviewing database-impacting changes looks competent but creates a conflict.
- Trap: QC checks may happen after go-live; wrong, they must precede production.

## 2.11.2 Quality management

### Definition and processes
- Definition: how IT department processes are controlled, measured and improved.
- A process here means tasks that yield desired results when done properly.
- Use: makes outcomes consistent rather than dependent on individual effort.
- Audit impact: auditors assess whether processes exist, are measured and are improved.

### Areas of control
- Software development, maintenance and implementation.
- Acquisition of hardware and software.
- Day-to-day operations.
- IT control performance.
- Service management.
- Security.
- HR management.
- General administration.
- The manual says "may include," so this list is illustrative, not closed.
- Audit impact: use the list as a scoping checklist when planning a quality review.

### Documented processes as governance evidence
- The IT department's defined, documented processes are evidence of effective governance.
- Insisting on observance of processes is key to efficiency and effectiveness.
- Audit impact: documentation alone is not enough; test that it is actually followed.
- Example: a documented change procedure bypassed by staff signals weak governance.

### Quality standards
- Definition: published frameworks that guide how to run quality processes.
- Use: help give a predictable, measurable, repeatable and certified environment.
- Audit impact: certification is evidence, but auditors still test actual operation.
- ⚠ Check: manual names only ISO 9001 as an example; other standards ➕ unverified.

### Exam traps for 2.11.2
- Correct logic: documented and followed processes show governance; documents alone do not.
- Trap: quality management covers only software development; wrong, the list is much wider.
- Trap: certification proves controls work; wrong, auditors still test operating effectiveness.

## 2.11.3 Operational excellence

### Definition
- Definition: continuous improvement of how efficiently and effectively an enterprise operates.
- Use: operational excellence teams carry out this improvement.
- Audit impact: auditors judge whether improvement work is tied to measurable results.

### What teams do
- Improve efficiency and effectiveness of enterprise operations.
- Identify and eliminate waste.
- Streamline processes.
- Improve communication and collaboration.
- May use data and analytics to find improvement areas.
- Example: analytics show a slow approval step, so the team removes a redundant sign-off.

### Common responsibilities
- Develop and implement best practices.
- Train and support other employees.
- Conduct research and development.
- Manage knowledge and information.
- Serve as a resource for employees and stakeholders.
- Specific duties vary by industry and focus area.
- The manual links best practices to efficiency, lower cost and customer satisfaction.
- Audit impact: improvement must not weaken controls, especially segregation of duties.

### Exam traps for 2.11.3
- Correct logic: operational excellence targets efficiency and waste, not compliance checking.
- Trap: it replaces QA; wrong, it complements QA and QC.
- Trap: cost cutting justifies removing controls; wrong, control objectives must still be met.

## Case study stub: Accenco (stem summary only)

- Setting: small, fast-growing financial institution reviewed for IT and business alignment.
- Auditor received only a short list of business goals and IT goal slides.
- Risk management committee of senior managers met three times in two years.
- No minutes were kept for those meetings.
- Several IT strategic plan initiatives were absent from the next IT budget.
- No full-time CIO exists.
- An IS manager reports to the CFO, who reports to the board.
- Board monitors IT initiatives; the CFO reports progress frequently.
- Per the SoD matrix, programmers need only DBA approval for direct production data access.
- Programmers give developed code to the program librarian, who migrates it to production.
- Internal audit performs IS audits monthly, as part of business performance review.
- Internal audit reports to the CFO.
- Business managers review and sign off financial results for correctness.

## Takeaway

- QA builds confidence in process; QC tests the product.
- QA must be independent, and nobody may review their own work.
- Quality management means controlled, measured, improved IT processes.
- Documented and followed processes are evidence of effective governance.
- Operational excellence reduces waste without weakening required controls.
- Check manual wording on QC: it must complete before production.
