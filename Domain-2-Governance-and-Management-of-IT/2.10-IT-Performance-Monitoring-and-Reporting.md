# 2.10 IT Performance Monitoring and Reporting

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Stakeholders need assurance investments are aligned and managed
- Measurement may be statutory or contractual
- Outcome measures: business value, competitive advantage, performance metrics
- Link incentives to performance measures
- Share results with employees, customers, stakeholders
- Monitoring process
  - Define KPIs, KRIs, KCIs
  - Timely performance reporting
  - Prompt action on deviations
- Metrics need measurable baseline and success or failure threshold
### 2.10.1 Key Performance Indicators (KPI) 📓
- Measures how well a process enables a goal
- Lead indicator of goal achievement
- Indicates capabilities, practices, skills
- Also called performance drivers (BSCs)
- Example: employee satisfaction with IT services
- Control criteria often based on KPIs
- Changeover KPI examples
  - Percentage of errors
  - Number of trouble reports
  - Duration of system outage
  - Customer satisfaction
### 2.10.2 Key Risk Indicators (KRI)
- Early warning of exposure to risk events
- Example: percentage increase in phishing attempts
- Threshold triggers alerts to assigned monitors
### 2.10.3 Key Control Indicators (KCI)
- Measure how well a control reduces causes, consequences or likelihood of risk
- Example: percentage of IT assets compliant with security policies
#### Developing metrics
- Identify critical data points for business objectives
- Identify specific, quantifiable work outputs
- Establish targets for scoring
- Monitor against metrics and report to those who can adjust
- Effective metric traits
  - Consistently measured
  - Based on accepted best practices
  - Useful for internal and external comparison
  - Meaningful to customers and sponsors
  - Accurate data, expressed as number, percentage or unit
  - Contextually specific
#### Auditor checks metrics cover
- Long-term organizational objectives
- Short-term business and IT plan performance
- Risk and regulatory compliance
- Internal and external user satisfaction
- IT processes, solution and service delivery
- Future-oriented activities
- Continuously monitor performance and capacity of IT resources
### 2.10.4 Performance optimization
- Performance: how well a system works, including satisfaction
- Optimization: improve efficiency while holding service levels at minimal added investment
- Measures not used to assign accountability or meet reporting duties
- Effective governance achieved when
  - Senior leadership sets goals aligned to objectives
  - Process owners set metrics aligned to goals
  - Each management layer monitors performance
- Critical Success Factors (CSF)
  - Executive sponsor is the top CSF for a security program 📓⭐
  - Leadership support
  - Clear goals and objectives
  - Data-driven decision making
  - Employee involvement
  - Continuous improvement
  - Transparency in common terms
- Methodologies and tools
  - PDCA cycle, ITIL, COBIT
  - PDCA steps
    - Plan: set objectives and processes, start small
    - Do: implement and collect data
    - Check: compare results to targets
    - Act: corrective action on root causes
  - PDCA with agile: reassess via sprints
  - Agile iterative and incremental vs waterfall single pass
  - Other EGIT frameworks: COBIT, ISO/IEC 27000, ITIL, O-ISM3, ISO/IEC 38500, ISO/IEC 20000, ISO 31000
- Developing metrics: start with customer-critical processes 📓⭐
### 2.10.5 Approaches and techniques
- Six Sigma
  - Quantitative, data-driven process improvement
  - Focus: defect reduction
  - Defect: anything outside customer specifications
  - Lean Six Sigma: eliminate non-value steps
- Business agility / Agile methodology
  - Mindset of flexibility; deliver earlier in smaller increments
  - Customer collaboration
  - Iterative development (sprints)
  - Self-organizing teams
  - Continuous feedback
  - Adaptability and flexibility
  - Emphasis on simplicity
  - Frameworks: Scrum, Kanban, Extreme Programming (XP)
  - Scrum: time-boxed sprints, product backlog, product owner, scrum master
- IT Balanced Scorecard 📓
  - Assesses IT beyond financial view
  - Four perspectives
    - User orientation
      - Asks how users view IT ➕
    - Business contribution
      - Asks how management views the organization ➕
    - Operational excellence
    - Future orientation
      - Asks whether IT is positioned to meet future needs ➕
  - Layers per perspective: mission, strategies, measures, sources
  - Mission examples: preferred supplier, economic IT services, reasonable business contribution, future opportunities
  - Strategy examples: user partnerships, enhanced service levels, control IT expenses, business value, new capabilities, staff training, R&D support
  - Measures: KPIs, KRIs as early warning, KCIs for control effectiveness
  - Sources: end-user personnel, COO, process owners
  - Aids IT strategy committee in alignment
  - Objectives: board reporting vehicle, stakeholder consensus, show added value, communicate performance, risk, capabilities
- IT portfolio management vs Balanced Scorecard
  - Portfolio: agility in adjusting investments, feedback on whole program investment
  - BSC: operationalizes strategic plan, feedback loop tied to strategic planning
  - BSC goal is not IT value or budget control
- Additional performance measurement approaches
  - Benchmarking: compare performance with peers and competitors
  - Business process reengineering (BPR): radical redesign for dramatic gains, often IT automation
  - Root cause analysis: find origins of errors to design controls
  - Life cycle cost-benefit analysis
    - Life cycle (LC)
    - Life cycle cost (LCC)
    - Benefit analysis (BA)
