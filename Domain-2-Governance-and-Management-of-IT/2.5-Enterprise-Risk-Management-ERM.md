# 2.5 Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- ERM from IT view: identify vulnerabilities and threats, choose countermeasures
- Reduce risk to acceptable level (residual risk) based on asset value
- ERM is management function, not independent audit
- ERM advises leaders on strategy to reach risk appetite
- Risk management: identify, analyze, evaluate, treat, monitor, communicate
#### Risk appetite and tolerance
- Risk appetite: risk enterprise will take for strategic objectives, set by senior leadership
- Risk tolerance: acceptable deviation from appetite
- Appetite strategic and enterprise-level; tolerance tactical, per department or project
- Both inform decisions such as product launch
#### Risk responses
- Avoid: eliminate exposure by not pursuing activity
- Mitigate: lessen probability or impact via controls
- Share or transfer: partnership, insurance, contract
- Accept: acknowledge risk and proceed, with formal monitoring
- Rejecting (ignoring) risk is a red flag for auditor
#### Levels of IT risk management
- Operational: effectiveness, control bypass, resource loss, noncompliance
- Project: complexity, objectives not met
- Strategic: alignment, competitors, technological change
- Levels interact; do not operate separately
### 2.5.1 Developing a risk management program
- First step: create an asset inventory 📓⭐
- Asset classification matches protection to value 📓⭐
  - Prevents over- and under-protection 📓
- Step: establish purpose of program
  - Align with overall strategy
  - Define KPIs, KRIs, metrics to evaluate effectiveness
  - Senior management and board set tone and goals
- Step: assign responsibility
  - Individual or team develops and implements ERM program
  - Integrate risk management at all levels
  - Operational staff and board assist risk committee
### 2.5.2 Risk management life cycle
- Repeatable process to manage IT risk consistently
- Step 1: IT risk identification (threats and vulnerabilities in IT assets) 📓
  - Collect data for identification, analysis, reporting
  - Threat: circumstance or event that can harm an information resource
  - Harm: destruction, disclosure, modification, denial of service
  - Classification by value, or by criticality and sensitivity
  - Typical assets: information and data, hardware, software, documents, personnel, clients and customers
  - Other assets: buildings, inventory, cash, goodwill, reputation
- Step 2: IT risk assessment
  - Assess threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood
  - Threat classes: errors and omissions, malicious damage, fraud, theft, equipment or software failure
  - Vulnerability: characteristic a threat can exploit
  - Vulnerability examples: lack of user knowledge, lack of security functionality, poor awareness, untested technology, unprotected communications
  - Human threat actors: novices (script kiddies), hacktivists, criminals, terrorists, nation-states, riots, political instability
  - Environmental threats: floods, lightning, tornadoes, hurricanes, earthquakes, fires
  - Impact: result of threat exploiting vulnerability
  - Impact factors: severity, duration, velocity
  - Loss types: direct money, legal breach, reputation, endangered staff, breach of confidence, lost opportunity, reduced efficiency, business interruption
  - Impact varies by enterprise nature (retailer vs hospital)
  - Overall risk = probability x magnitude of impact
  - Extended: probability x impact x velocity
- Step 3: risk response and mitigation
  - Avoid, reduce, transfer, accept 📓
  - Accepted risk needs formal documentation and executive approval 📓⭐
  - Auditor first verifies the acceptance documentation 📓⭐
  - Controls are countermeasures or safeguards: people, processes, products
  - Control strength: design strength and effectiveness likelihood
  - Strongest: preventive, automated, formal
  - Weakest: informal, manual
  - Hybrid (IT dependent) controls mix manual and automated
  - Control matrix: preventive, detective, corrective by strength
  - Residual risk: risk remaining after controls
  - Risk above appetite: add controls; below: check excessive control
  - Residual risk acceptance considers
    - Organizational policy
    - Risk appetite
    - Risk identification and measurement
    - Uncertainty in assessment approach
    - Cost and effectiveness of implementation
    - Cost of control versus benefit
  - Control selection by cost-benefit analysis
  - Reduction methods: terminate, minimize probability, minimize impact, transfer via insurance
- Step 4: risk and control monitoring and reporting
  - Monitor risk performance and environmental change
  - Triggers reassessment and control changes
  - Encompasses risk assessment, mitigation, reevaluation
  - Ongoing, iterative process
#### Auditor understanding of IT business risk
- Industry and internationally accepted risk management processes
- Purpose, nature and environment of business
- Dependence on technology
- Business risk of using IT
- Overview of business processes and IT impact
- Risk assessment starts with objectives and critical assets
- Adaptive process; effort focused on most sensitive or critical assets
### 2.5.3 Risk analysis methods
- Qualitative
  - Words or descriptive rankings (high, medium, low)
  - Based on checklists, interviews, subjective ratings
  - Simplest, most frequent; lacks accounting rigor
  - Text analysis can be time-consuming
- Semiquantitative
  - Descriptive ranks mapped to numeric scale (e.g., high 5, medium 3, low 1)
  - Used when quantitative impossible or to reduce subjectivity
  - Common in surveys scored 1 to 5
- Quantitative: SLE, ARO, ALE ➕
  - Numeric (monetary) likelihood and impact values
  - Data: historical records, experience, industry practice, statistics, testing
  - Generally performed during BIA
  - Main problem: valuation of information assets
  - Tech asset value includes replacement cost and value of data processed
  - SOX scoping by transaction volume or value (98 percent POS system example)
