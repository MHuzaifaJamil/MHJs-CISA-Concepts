# 2.8 IT Resource Management

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Limited resources: people and money; opportunity cost
- Auditor understands investment and allocation practices
- ROI includes financial and nonfinancial benefits
- Convert nonfinancial benefits to monetary units where feasible
- IT management plans, builds, implements, operates, monitors per governance direction
### 2.8.1 Value of IT
- IT value depends on strategic alignment 📓⭐
- Value = relationship between cost and benefit
- Larger benefit relative to cost, greater value
- Portfolio management differs from IT financial management: strategic, directive
### 2.8.2 Implementing IT portfolio management
- Investment portfolio analysis supports project prioritization 📓⭐
  - Balances IT project risk against financial reward 📓
- Determines whether best IT projects pursued
- Criteria: financial, strategic, tactical; must be able to change
- First step: standardize terminology
- Initial tasks
  - Ensure management commitment and agreed targets
  - Plan model in line with management process
  - Specify portfolio inclusion criteria
  - Describe roles, tasks and decisions
  - Organize tools, support, instructions
- Implementation methods
  - Analyze risk profile
  - Diversify projects, infrastructure, technologies
  - Align and realign with business objectives
  - Continuous improvement
- Mandatory projects: regulatory compliance, technical debt
- Business case required
- Completed programs stay in portfolio with status changed and results evaluated
### 2.8.3 IT management practices
- IT as service or support function, now integrated in operations
- Practices implement policies and procedures
- Review covers HR, change management, financial management, information security management
### 2.8.4 Human resource management
- HR policies: recruit, hire, train, promote, measure, discipline, succeed, retain, terminate
- Not tested on CISA exam (subjective)
- Recruiting and hiring
  - Hiring controls
    - Background checks (criminal, educational, financial, professional)
    - Confidentiality or nondisclosure agreements
    - Employee bonding (illegal in some countries)
    - Conflict of interest disclosures
    - Codes of professional conduct or ethics
    - Noncompete agreements
  - Residual risk
    - Unsuitable employee for the role
    - Reference checks skipped or fraudulent
    - Temporary staff and contractors add uncontrolled risk
    - Lack of confidentiality awareness
- Employee handbook
  - Given at hire
  - Contents: security policies, conduct, ethics code, expectations, benefits, vacation, overtime, outside employment, evaluations, emergency procedures
  - Disciplinary actions for excessive absence, confidentiality or security breach, noncompliance
  - Published code of conduct
  - Handbook is information source, not a control
- Training
  - New-hire orientation: cybersecurity, confidentiality, data management, compliance, conflict of interest
  - Specialized IT training: security, access controls
  - Ongoing refresher and job-function training
  - Required when new hardware or software implemented
  - Cross-training reduces dependence on one employee
  - Assess risk of one person knowing whole system
- Scheduling and time reporting
  - Efficient use of computing resources
  - Time reporting monitors scheduling and staffing
  - Supports cost allocation, invoicing, chargebacks, KPI measurement, activity analysis
- Terms and conditions of employment
  - Signed by employees, contractors, third-party users
  - Confidentiality or nondisclosure agreement before access
  - Legal responsibilities and rights (copyright, data protection)
  - Responsibility for classifying information and managing assets
  - Handling information from other enterprises
  - Enterprise responsibility for personal information
  - Responsibilities outside premises and hours (remote or hybrid)
  - Actions if requirements disregarded
  - Obligations may continue after employment ends
- Expectations during employment
  - Apply security per policies
  - Responsibilities in approved job descriptions
  - Awareness, education, training for all
  - Formal disciplinary process for security breaches
- Employee performance management
  - Goal setting and performance reviews
  - SMART goals: specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, time-bound
  - Pay, bonuses, promotions based on performance
- Disciplinary actions
  - Performance improvement plan (PIP) common
- Promotion policies
  - Fair, equitable, objective criteria
  - Auditor checks historical promotion data for bias
- Required vacations
  - Someone else performs the job at least yearly
  - May expose fraud if no collusion
  - Mandatory leave mitigates internal fraud
  - Job rotation: reduces fraud and key-staff dependence
- Retention and succession plans
  - Retention: compensation, benefits, mentorship, development
  - Succession: redundancy or trained replacement for critical functions
- Termination policies
  - Written, voluntary and involuntary cases
  - Escort procedures for adverse terminations
  - Return devices, keys, IDs, badges
  - Remove company information from personal devices
  - Revoke logins including application-specific credentials
  - Notify staff and security
  - Final pay routines remove from payroll
  - Termination interview
  - Role changes may need access revoked and reissued
### 2.8.5 Enterprise change management
- Defined, documented process to apply technology improvements
- Involve all impacted levels to avoid resistance
- IT leads or facilitates change
- Obtain senior management commitment first
- Gain support from each functional area
- Communication process to end users: impact and benefits
- Include method for user feedback
- Feedback: requirements validation, user acceptance testing, training
### 2.8.6 Financial management practices
- Technology costs are major budget element
- Short useful life drives replacement costs
- Cost allocation
  - User-pays chargeback by standard formula
  - Charges: staff time, computer time, other costs
  - Gives market-like cost for IPF services
  - Chargeback policy set by board, implemented by CFO, user and IS management
- IS budgets
  - Facilitates forecasting, monitoring, analysis
  - Link to short-term projects and long-term objectives
- Software expenses vs capitalization
  - Classify as operating or capital expense
  - IAS 38 six criteria for capitalizing development costs
  - Must show probable future economic benefits
  - Intangible assets: websites, software
  - Under IFRS, get guidance from chartered accountants
  - On-premises purchase generally capital expense
  - Cloud subscription usually operating expense
  - Manipulating classification can misstate financials
  - Auditor checks expenses tracked against approved budget
### 2.8.7 Information security management
- Lead role protecting information and processing resources
- Protects against unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, destruction
- Goal: confidentiality, integrity, availability
- Key responsibilities
  - Risk assessment and management
  - Security policy development
  - Incident response and management
  - Security awareness and training
  - Security architecture and design
  - Vulnerability management: scans, patches, penetration tests
  - BCP and DRP
    - BIA and scenarios
    - RTO: maximum acceptable downtime
    - RPO: maximum tolerable data loss, in time units
  - Identity and access management
    - Strong authentication, least privilege, monitoring
  - Compliance and regulatory requirements
  - Security incident monitoring and analysis (SIEM)
