# 2.9 IT Vendor Management

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Reliance on third-party vendors increases breach and incident risk
- Many major breaches originated via vendor access
#### Best practices managing vendor risk
- Risk assessments before awarding contracts
- Right-to-audit clauses in all contracts
- Minimum access (least privilege)
- Accurate inventory of vendors and access levels
- Remove access when relationship ends
- Monitor and certify vendors regularly
### 2.9.1 Sourcing practices
- Evaluate IT objectives and staffing model for delivery method
- Sourcing questions
  - Is it a core function?
  - Does it hold critical knowledge not replicable elsewhere?
  - Can another party do it at same or lower price, same quality, no added risk?
  - Does enterprise have experience managing third parties or offshore?
  - Contractual or regulatory restrictions on offshore or foreign nationals?
- IT steering committee reviews and approves sourcing decision
- Outsourcing process steps
  - Define function to outsource
  - Describe service levels and minimum metrics
  - Know desired knowledge, skills, quality of provider
  - Know current in-house cost for bid comparison
  - Conduct due diligence on providers
  - Confirm architectural considerations for contract or regulation
  - Communicate decision and monitoring requirements internally
- Delivery models
  - Insourced: enterprise staff
  - Outsourced: vendor staff
  - Hybrid: mix, joint ventures, supplemental staff
- Location models
  - Onsite
  - Offsite (nearshore): remote, same area
  - Offshore: different geographic region
- Auditor understands vendor service scope
  - Commercial off-the-shelf products, outsourced services, cloud, managed services
- Understand vendor SLAs
- Consider supplier financial viability, licensing scalability, software escrow
- Understand RFP requirements specifications
- Security controls specified in RFP
- Vendor selection essentials
- Escrow agreement; right to audit
- Assurance testing and reporting by trusted third party (e.g., annual SOC report)
### 2.9.2 Outsourcing practices and strategies
- Contracts must allow independent external security review 📓⭐
- Monitor outsourced activity through SLA metrics 📓⭐
- Service delivery transfers; accountability remains with client management
- Transparency and decision ownership stay with client
- Outsourcing is strategic, not merely procurement
- Retain core activities, outsource noncore
- Handing control of IT functions to external party
- Requires revisiting control framework
#### Reasons for outsourcing
- Focus on core activities
- Pressure on profit margins
- Competition demanding cost savings and faster time-to-market
- Flexibility in organization, structure, market size
#### Advantages, disadvantages and risk (figure 2.13)
- Possible advantages
  - Economies of scale from reusable components
  - Vendor focus and time on a project
  - Vendor experience with problems and techniques
  - Better specifications and contracts
  - Less scope creep
- Possible disadvantages and business risk
  - Costs exceeding expectations
  - Loss of internal IT experience
  - Loss of control over IT
  - Vendor failure
  - Limited product access
  - Deficient legal and regulatory compliance
  - Contract terms not met
  - Lack of contractor loyalty
  - Disgruntled customers or employees
  - Uncompetitive service costs
  - Obsolete vendor IT systems
  - Failure to receive anticipated benefits
  - Reputational damage
  - Lengthy, expensive litigation
  - Loss or leakage of information or processes
- Risk reduction options
  - Measurable, shared goals and rewards
  - Software escrow
  - Multiple suppliers or withholding business as incentive
  - Periodic competitive reviews and benchmarking
  - Short-term contracts
  - Cross-functional contract management team
  - Contract provisions for foreseeable contingencies
#### Outsourcing contract provisions
- Service quality expectations (ISO/IEC TR 33015, CMMI, ITIL, ISO)
- Access control and security administration
- Violation reporting and follow-up with SLAs
- Owner notification and investigation cooperation
- Change/version control and testing requirements
- Network control responsibilities
- Performance parameters (e.g., processing times)
- Capacity management criteria
- Contract change provisions
- Dispute escalation and resolution
- Indemnification for provider-caused damages
- Confidentiality agreements (NDAs)
- Right to audit (facilities, records, copies, personnel, computerized files)
- Business continuity and disaster recovery, with testing
- CIA of enterprise data and clear data ownership
- Compliance with laws, including later ones
- Intellectual property ownership and protection
- Warranty and maintenance periods
- Software escrow
- Clear roles and responsibilities
- Vendor follows enterprise policies unless agreed otherwise
- Subcontractors identified; enterprise approves changes
#### Managing the relationship
- Contract describes means, methods, processes, structure, quality control
- Regularly review contract and service levels
- Review outsourcer procedures and quality programs (ISO/IEC TS 33061, CMMI, ITIL)
- Choose supplier carefully; cultural and personnel compatibility
- Decision covers quality, continuity, control, competitive advantage, technical knowledge
- SLAs
  - Required service level and support options
  - Guaranteed uptime or downtime
  - Customer support level
  - Penalties and enforcement
  - Incentives (bonuses, gain-sharing)
  - Instrument of control
- Cross-border legislation awareness
- Legal team reviews and approves outsourcing
- Industry standards and benchmarking
  - Standards from vendor user groups, publications, associations
  - Examples: ISO 9001:2015, CMMI
  - Determine service level of similar arrangements
- Globalization practices and strategies
  - Active oversight of remote or offshore sites
  - Risks and audit concerns
    - Legal, regulatory and tax issues
    - Continuity of operations
    - Personnel policy changes
    - Telecommunication issues
    - Cross-border and cross-cultural issues
    - Planned globalization and expansion
- Outsourcing and third-party audit reports
  - Assurance on provider controls via periodic third-party reports
  - SSAE 18 and AICPA guide: SOC 1, SOC 2, SOC 3
  - Replaced SAS 70
  - ISAE 3402 from IAASB
  - SOC 1: controls relevant to user entities' financial reporting
  - SOC 2: security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy
  - SOC 3: like SOC 2 without detail of control design and tests
  - Other reports: penetration tests, security assessments
  - Auditor skills
    - Management assertions vs services provided
    - Validate independence, objectivity, competence of assessors
    - Handle qualified opinions
    - Use bridge letters when new report delayed
    - Obtain, review and present results to management
### 2.9.3 Cloud governance
- Strategic direction is primary focus
- Governance activities need cloud considerations
  - Goal setting
  - Policy and standards
  - Roles and responsibilities
  - Risk management
- Business processes change
  - Data processing, development, retrieval
  - Storage, archiving, backup
- Business units can bypass IT to get cloud services
- Policies for sourcing, managing, discontinuing cloud services
- Designate relationship manager or service management team
- Third party assigns compliance checking and enforcement
- Sufficient technical skills to monitor information security requirements
- Act on deficiencies: enforce SLAs, reassess vendor viability
- Keep control and visibility over security
- Visibility into change management, vulnerabilities, incident reporting
- Considerations
  - Cloud risk management strategy
  - Strong cybersecurity controls
  - Resilience, outsourcing, vendor lock-in, concentration risk
  - Staff skills
- Ultimate responsibility for information remains with enterprise
### 2.9.4 Governance in outsourcing
- Responsibilities, roles, objectives, interfaces, controls for third-party services
- Client and provider work together on consistent approach
- Objective: continuity of service, profitability, added value for both parties
- Governance balances risk, demand, provision, cost
- Extended responsibilities
  - Contractual viability via continuous review
  - Explicit governance schedule in contract
  - Manage relationship through SLAs and operating level agreements (OLAs)
  - Manage stakeholders and expectations
  - Clear decision, escalation, dispute, demand, delivery roles
  - Allocate resources by prioritized need
  - Evaluate performance, cost, user satisfaction, effectiveness
  - Ongoing communication
- Preplanned governance built into contract; evolves
- Business alignment driven by service recipients
- SLAs and OLAs measured by performance and user satisfaction
#### Right-to-audit understanding
- How auditing of provider is allowed by contract
- Visibility into provider internal controls (CIA, preventive, detective, corrective)
- Documented SLAs for problem and incident management communicated to affected parties
### 2.9.5 Capacity and growth planning
- Essential given IT's strategic importance
- Reflect long- and short-range business plans
- Part of budgeting process
- Capacity reflects infrastructure and staff changes
- Lack of qualified staff delays projects or misses service levels
- May drive outsourcing for growth
### 2.9.6 Third-party service delivery management
- Service delivery management system maintains security and service levels
- Check agreement implementation, monitor compliance, manage changes
- Monitoring and review of third-party services
  - Services, reports, records monitored; regular audits
  - Monitor service performance levels
  - Review service reports and hold progress meetings
  - Share and review security incident information
  - Review third-party audit trails and records of events, problems, failures
  - Resolve identified problems
- Managing changes to third-party services
  - Consider criticality and reassess risk
  - Enterprise changes: service enhancements, new applications, policy updates, new controls
  - Provider changes: network enhancements, new technologies, new products or versions, new development tools, facility location, vendor or subcontractor change
- Service improvement and user satisfaction
  - SLAs set the baseline
  - Improvement examples: fewer help desk calls, fewer errors, better availability
  - Penalties and rewards
  - Monitor satisfaction by interviews and surveys at SLA intervals
