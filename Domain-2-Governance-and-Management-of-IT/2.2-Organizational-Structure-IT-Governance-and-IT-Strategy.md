# 2.2 Organizational Structure, IT Governance and IT Strategy

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Corporate governance: system by which enterprises are directed and controlled
- Board of directors responsible for enterprise governance
- Corporate governance relationships: management, board, shareholders, stakeholders
- Aims: trust, transparency, accountability
- Governments use governance frameworks to reduce inaccurate financial reporting
- COBIT 2019 figure 2.1: components of a governance system
### 2.2.1 Enterprise Governance of Information and Technology (EGIT)
#### Concept
- IT governance: evaluate stakeholder needs, set direction 📓
- Stakeholder involvement drives effective governance 📓
- Board of directors is ultimately responsible 📓⭐
- Senior management, CIO and steering committee support the board 📓
- EGIT: all stakeholders input into IT decisions (board, management, internal customers, finance)
- EGIT is stewardship of IT resources for stakeholders
- Purpose: IT aligns with and supports enterprise objectives
- Purpose: realize promised benefits, exploit opportunities
- Purpose: use IT resources responsibly, manage IT risk
- Two core issues
  - IT delivers value (driven by strategic alignment)
  - IT risk is managed (driven by embedded accountability)
- Strategic alignment is a critical success factor
- Key element: business-IT alignment leads to business value
#### EGIT overall processes
- IT resource management: updated inventory of IT resources, risk process
- Performance measurement: resources perform, deviations flag risk early
- Compliance management: legal, regulatory, contractual requirements
#### Governance vs management (COBIT view)
- Governance: evaluate needs, set direction, monitor performance and compliance
- Management: plans, builds, runs, monitors per governance direction
#### COBIT 2019 📓
- Goals cascade 📓
  1. Stakeholder drivers and needs 📓
  2. Enterprise goals 📓
  3. Alignment goals 📓
  4. Governance and management objectives 📓
- Governance vs management objectives ➕
- Domains: EDM, APO, BAI, DSS, MEA ➕
  - EDM: Evaluate, Direct and Monitor (governance) ➕
  - APO: Align, Plan and Organize ➕
  - BAI: Build, Acquire and Implement ➕
  - DSS: Deliver, Service and Support ➕
  - MEA: Monitor, Evaluate and Assess ➕
- Design factors ➕
- Performance management and capability levels ➕
- Governance system components ➕
  - Processes ➕
  - Organizational structures ➕
  - Principles, policies and frameworks ➕
  - Information ➕
  - Culture, ethics and behavior ➕
  - People, skills and competencies ➕
  - Services, infrastructure and applications ➕
- Governance system purpose: satisfy stakeholder needs, generate value from IT
- Value balances benefits, risk and resources
#### Other governance frameworks
- ISO/IEC 38500 ➕
  - Governance of IT principles for governing bodies: effective, efficient, acceptable IT use
- ISO/IEC 27000 series: information security program best practices
- ITIL: operational IT service management, business value delivery
- O-ISM3: process-based information security maturity model
- ISO/IEC 20000: service management specification aligned with ITIL
- ISO 31000:2018: risk management guidelines
### 2.2.2 Good practices for EGIT
- Strategic alignment ensures effective, valuable governance 📓⭐
- Documented accountability ensures proper execution 📓
- Measure effectiveness through stakeholder involvement 📓
- Value delivery, risk management, resource optimization, performance measurement ➕
#### Factors driving EGIT importance
- Boards demand better return from IT investment
- Rising IT expenditure
- Regulatory control requirements: SOX, Basel, GDPR, sector rules
- Service provider selection, outsourcing and acquisition (e.g., cloud)
- Adoption of control frameworks and good practices
- Cost optimization via standardized approaches
- Growing maturity of accepted frameworks
- Benchmarking against standards and peers
#### Evaluate, direct, monitor processes
- Conformance and performance
- System of internal controls
- Compliance with external requirements
### 2.2.3 Audit's role in EGIT
- Audit helps successful EGIT implementation
- Audit provides leading-practice recommendations to senior management
- Audit monitors compliance with EGIT initiatives
- Independent, balanced view of EGIT metrics
- EGIT reporting is at highest enterprise level, crosses boundaries
- Audit charter approved by audit committee
#### Terms of reference should state
- Scope of work, functional areas and issues
- Reporting line to highest level for EGIT issues
- Right of access to information, including third-party providers
- Consider auditor status and skills; hire independent third party if insufficient
#### Aspects IS auditor assesses
- Alignment of enterprise governance and EGIT
- Alignment of IT with mission, vision, values, objectives, strategies
- Achievement of performance objectives (effectiveness, efficiency)
- Legal, environmental, information quality, fiduciary, security, privacy requirements
- Control environment
- Inherent risk in IS environment
- IT investment and expenditure
#### Three Lines Model
- First line, second line, third line
  - Model developed by the IIA
  - First line: enterprise management, owns processes, controls and metrics
  - Second line: risk management, independently evaluates known and emerging risk
  - Second line may advise but not overrule management decisions
  - Third line: independent audit, tests and assures
  - Third line sets own audit programs and scope
  - Third line has separate reporting line to audit committee
  - Governing body: integrity, leadership, transparency
- IS audit assures enterprise acts to mitigate risk blocking objectives
### 2.2.4 Information security governance
- Effective information security governance
  - Subset of corporate governance, gives security strategic direction
  - Ensures security risk managed and resources used responsibly
  - Value drivers: confidentiality, integrity, availability, continuity, asset protection
  - Why significant: networking, rapid change, IT dependence, sophisticated threats, extended enterprise
- Executive sponsorship gives authority and funding 📓⭐
#### Governance framework elements
- Security strategy linked to business objectives
- Governing policies for strategy, controls, regulation
- Standards for each policy
- Security organization free of conflicts of interest
- Institutionalized monitoring processes
#### Accountability and policy approval
- Board and CEO accountable for information security governance
- CEO accountable to board, responsible through executive management
- Policy approvers drawn from varied operations and staff functions
- Policy approval based on consensus where possible
#### Information security program
- Protection commensurate with asset value or compromise risk
- Protects information wherever created, processed, stored, disposed
- Scope extends to outsourcing, cloud, onshoring and offshoring
#### Outcomes of effective governance
- Strategic alignment
- Risk management
- Compliance
- Value delivery
#### Enablers
- Performance measurement
  - SMART objectives
  - Defined, agreed metrics aligned with strategy
  - Measurement process gives feedback on shortcomings
  - Independent assurance via external assessments and audits
- Resource management
  - Capture knowledge
  - Document security processes and practices
  - Security architecture to use infrastructure efficiently
- Process integration
  - Avoid siloed security activities
#### What executives strive for
- High-quality information for decisions
- Business value from IT investments
- Operational excellence
- IT risk at acceptable level
- Optimized IT cost
- Compliance with laws, contracts, policies
### 2.2.5 Information systems strategy
- IT plans must align with the business plan 📓⭐
- IT strategy must support business objectives 📓
- Top management involvement improves alignment 📓⭐
- Lack of top management support leads to misalignment 📓
- Boards cannot leave IS strategy only to functional management
- Threats: resource abuse, cybercrime, fraud, errors and omissions
- IS strategic processes give assurance existing and emerging goals are met
### 2.2.6 Strategic planning
- Long-term organizational direction 📓
- IT needs both long- and short-term plans 📓⭐
- Strategic vs tactical vs operational planning 📓
  - Approved suppliers support long-term strategy 📓
  - Software testing methodology is operational 📓
  - Short-term plan for a new system is tactical 📓
- Business plan compatibility review 📓⭐
- Short-range (tactical) plan review ties business objectives to IS goals 📓
- IT Balanced Scorecard maps IT metrics to business objectives 📓⭐
- Enterprise architecture also supports alignment 📓
#### Strategic planning practice
- Under top management responsibility
- Identify cost-effective IT solutions and action plans
- Key players: IT management, IT steering committee, strategy committee
- Traditional cycle: every three to five years
- Agile strategy: continuous planning, shorter horizons
- Volatile environments need more frequent reviews
- Monitor customer, technology, regulatory, geopolitical shifts
#### Requirements for new and revised systems
- Consider strategic intentions, objectives, initiatives, needed IT capabilities
- Review existing portfolio: functional fit, cost, risk
- Assess IT capacity: infrastructure and support processes
  - Project management, development, maintenance, security administration, help desk
- Consider ROI on existing IT and legacy decommissioning
- Balance maintenance cost against new initiative cost
#### Audit considerations
- Check strategic planning process or framework
- How business operational, tactical, development plans feed IT strategy
- Contents, updating, communication, monitoring of plans
- How CIO or senior IT management creates the business strategy
- Lack of IT involvement in business strategy means misalignment risk
### 2.2.7 Business intelligence
- Data warehouse, data mart, data lake ➕
- ETL, OLAP ➕
- BI supports strategic planning with data and insights
#### BI benefits
- Identify trends and patterns
- Understand customer behavior
- Assess performance
- Make better decisions
#### Typical BI measurement areas
- Process cost, efficiency, quality
- Customer satisfaction
- Customer profitability and predictors
- Staff and unit KPI achievement
- Risk management: unusual transactions, incident and loss statistics
#### Drivers of BI interest
- Size and complexity of modern enterprises
- Pursuit of competitive advantage (ERP, cloud, supply chain)
- Legal requirements (e.g., report all accounts and suspicious patterns)
#### Data architecture
- Enterprise data flow architecture (EDFA)
- Logical data architecture
- Seek most granular (atomic) data, since storage is cheap
#### EDFA layers
- Presentation/desktop access: spreadsheets, queries, BSCs, dashboards
  - Includes direct queries
  - Includes ad hoc queries ➕
- Data source: operational, external, nonoperational data
- Core data warehouse: normalized relational data for reporting
- Data mart: subset per business unit, relational or OLAP cube
- Data staging and quality: copy, transform, quality control, load only reliable data
- Data access: connects storage with sources, hides source organization
- Data preparation: assembles data for marts, precalculates OLAP, data mining
- Indexing layer: shown between data preparation and warehouse in figure 2.3 ➕
- Metadata repository: data about data, business context, transformation and validation rules
- Warehouse management: schedules build and maintenance, security administration
- Application messaging: transports data and control messages between layers
- Internet/intranet: browser UI, TCP/IP networking
#### Data analysis models
- Context diagrams: major processes and external parties
- Activity or swim-lane diagrams: deconstruct processes
- Entity relationship diagrams: data entities and relations
- Involve business operatives to avoid copying suboptimal data configurations
### 2.2.8 Organizational structure
- Key component of governance; identifies decision-making entities
- Structures vary by size, industry, location
#### IT governing committees
- IT steering committee
  - Level: management
  - Decides overall IT spending and cost allocation
  - Aligns and approves enterprise IT architecture
  - Approves project plans, budgets, priorities, milestones
  - Acquires and assigns resources
  - Monitors projects for value, time and budget
  - Monitors resource and priority conflicts between divisions, IT and projects
  - Recommends changes to strategic plans
  - Oversees day-to-day IT service delivery and projects
  - Ensures projects keep meeting business requirements, reevaluating the business case
  - Communicates strategic goals to project teams
  - Major contributor to management IT governance responsibilities
  - Focus: implementation
  - Members: sponsoring executive, business executives, CIO, advisors (IT, audit, legal, finance)
- IT strategy committee
  - Level: board
  - Advises board on IT strategy, alignment, resources, costs, risk and return
  - Advises on IT relevance, progress on major projects, IT risk exposure
  - Members: board members and non-board specialists
  - Focus: current and future strategic IT issues
  - Delegated by board to input and prepare strategy approval
  - Contains IT risk and optimizes IT costs, including external sourcing
  - Gives management direction on IT strategy
  - Drives and catalyzes board IT strategy
- Other committees: IT executive, IT governance, IT investment, IT management
#### Steering committee detail
- Board member who understands IT risk ideally chairs it
- Charter defines duties; members have decision authority
- General review board for major IS projects, not routine operations
- Review long- and short-range IT plans
- Approve major hardware and software acquisitions within board limits
- Approve and monitor projects, budgets, standards, procedures
- Approve sourcing strategies: insourcing, outsourcing, offshoring
- Review resource adequacy and allocation
- Decide centralization vs decentralization
- Support enterprise security program
- Report to board on IS activities
#### Roles and responsibilities of senior management and boards
- Board: ultimate IT governance responsibility 📓
- Senior management: reviews information security policy 📓
- Senior business management owns risk acceptance level 📓
- Governance needs board or senior management approving policy, monitoring, reviewing metrics
##### Board of directors
- Know information assets and criticality
- Receive high-level risk assessment and BIA results
- Approve key assets to protect for due care
- Tone at the top; define and enforce penalties from board down
- Board accountable and liable for enterprise
##### Senior management
- Leadership and ongoing support for security governance
- Integrate with business process owners
##### Information security standards committee (ISSC)
- Representatives of C-level, IT, application owners, business owners, operations, HR, audit, legal
- Deliberates control suitability, secure configuration of OSs and databases
- Auditor ensures auditability via audit trails and logs
- Legal advises on liability and conflicts with law
##### Chief information security officer (CISO)
- Drives program: implementable, auditable policies, standards, procedures
- Function exists even without the title (CIO, CTO, CFO, CEO may perform)
- Legal responsibility ultimately rests with senior management and board
- Position increasingly elevated to senior management
#### Matrix of outcomes and responsibilities
- RACI chart ➕
- Outcomes: strategic alignment, risk management, value delivery, resource management, performance measurement, process assurance
- Levels: board, executive management, steering committee, CISO, audit executives
- Matrix informational, not specifically tested
#### IT organizational structure and responsibilities
- IT structure aligns with business requirements 📓⭐
- Organizational chart shows roles, reporting lines, SoD 📓⭐
- IT roles: CIO, CTO, CISO, DBA, security administrator, network administrator, programmer, QA, librarian, help desk ➕
- IT headed by IT manager/director or CIO
- Functions: applications, data, technical support, user support, operations, risk management
- CISA exam does not test specific job responsibilities
#### Data ownership
- Classifies data elements and assigns responsibility for confidentiality, completeness, accuracy
- Establishes accountability by assigning protection to named employees
- Auditor checks ownership assigned and owner aware
- Auditor samples job descriptions against security policy
- Auditor reviews data classification appropriateness
- Data owners
  - Managers and directors using data to run business
  - Identify and classify data by risk
  - Authorize access
  - Update access rules on personnel changes
  - Regularly review access rules
  - Secure data across life cycle, origination to disposal
- Data custodians
  - Store and safeguard data
  - IS staff such as systems analysts, operators
- Data users
  - Internal and external; access authorized by owners
  - Restricted and monitored by security administrator
  - Watch for unauthorized people, follow guidelines
- Documented authorizations
  - Data access authorized in writing
  - Auditor samples authorizations
  - Where owners exist, only owners give written authority
- Security administrator
  - Provides physical and logical security for programs, data, equipment
  - Operates under security policy
- New IT users sign security obligations
  - Read and follow policies
  - Keep IDs and passwords secret
  - Create quality passwords
  - Lock screens when idle
  - Report suspected violations
  - Maintain physical security
  - Conform to laws and regulations
  - Use IT resources only for authorized business
#### IT roles and responsibilities
- Organizational chart defines hierarchy and authority
- Job descriptions, RACI charts, swimlane diagrams clarify roles
- Auditor observes whether formal descriptions match actual practice
- Systems development manager: manages programmers and analysts
- Project management
  - Plans and executes IS projects
  - May report to project management office
  - Reports progress to steering committee
- Help desk (service desk)
  - Responds to user technical questions and problems
  - Record problems reported, solved, escalated, then analyze
  - Acquire hardware and software for end users
  - Assist and train end users
  - Monitor technical developments
  - Determine problem source in production and start corrective action
  - Initiate efficiency changes
- End user: operates business application services
- End-user support manager: liaison between IT and end users
- Data management: data architecture, data as corporate asset
- Quality assurance (QA) manager: facilitates quality activities in IT
- Information security management
  - Separate from IT, headed by CISO
  - CISO may report to CIO or dotted line
  - Conflict: CIO values cost and continuity, CISO values protection
- Infrastructure operations and maintenance
  - Operations manager runs data center staff
  - Staff: operators, librarians, schedulers, data control
  - Control group collects, converts, controls input, balances and distributes output
  - Control group in separate area, authorized staff only
- Media management
  - Records, issues, receives, safeguards removable media files
  - Tools for inventory, movement, version control, configuration management
- Data entry
  - Batch or online; user departments often enter directly
  - Controls for validated, accurate, complete, authorized data
- Supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA)
  - Centralized monitoring of large sites (plants, power)
  - Site control automated by RTUs or PLCs
  - Operators use HMI to supervise or override
  - Data feeds history log for trending
  - Internet migration eases integration but exposes critical infrastructure to attack
- Systems administration
  - Maintains multiuser systems: LAN, WLAN, WAN, PAN, SAN, intranets, extranets, mainframes
  - Adds workstations and peripherals
  - Sets up user accounts
  - Installs systemwide software
  - Anti-virus procedures
  - Allocates storage
  - Mainframe shops may call it systems programmer
- Security administration
  - Begins with management commitment and written policy
  - Ideally full-time employee, reports to infrastructure director
  - Maintains access rules
  - Controls issuing of user IDs and passwords
  - Monitors violations, takes corrective action
  - Reviews security policy periodically
  - Runs security awareness program
  - Tests security architecture
  - Works with compliance, risk, audit
- Database administration (DBA)
  - Custodian of data; defines and maintains data structures
  - Specifies and changes physical data definition
  - Selects and tests optimization tools
  - Educates programmers on database structures
  - Implements definition, access, update, concurrency controls
  - Monitors usage, collects performance statistics, tunes
  - Defines backup and recovery procedures
  - Can override controls and access all data
  - Compensating controls: SoD, management approval, supervisor log review, detective controls on tools
- Systems analyst: designs systems from user needs, writes requirements and specifications
- Security architect
  - Evaluates security technologies
  - Designs network, access control, identity management security
  - Sets security policies and requirements
  - Deliverables differ from systems analyst
- System security engineer (ISO/IEC 21827)
  - Covers project life cycles
  - Covers whole enterprise
  - Interacts with other disciplines
  - Interacts with other enterprises (acquisition, certification, evaluation)
- Applications development and maintenance
  - Staff work in test-only environment
  - Another group moves changes to production
- Infrastructure development and maintenance
  - Maintains systems software and OS
  - Broad access needs tamper-resistant electronic logs
  - Access only to libraries of software they maintain
  - Control domain admin and superuser accounts
#### Vendor and outsourcer management
- Dedicated staff for multiple vendors and outsourcers
- Prime contact for vendor within IT
- Direct outsourcer and escalate issues
- Monitor and report service levels
- Review contract changes and obtain IT approval
#### Network management
- Dispersed IPFs: LANs, WANs, wireless networks
- Administrators handle routers, switches, firewalls, segmentation, performance, remote access
- Reports to IPF director or end-user manager, at least dotted line to IPF director
- Ensures links work, backups occur, purchases authorized
- LAN administrator should have no application programming duties
#### Separation of duties within IT
- Segregation of duties prevents unauthorized actions 📓⭐
- Access control policy review checks incompatible duties 📓⭐
- SoD controls
- Compensating controls for lack of SoD 📓
  - Log developer access to production and independently review 📓⭐
- Duties to segregate
  - Custody of assets
  - Authorization
  - Recording transactions
- Without SoD
  - Asset misappropriation
  - Misstated financials
  - Inaccurate documentation
  - Undetected improper fund use or data change
  - Undetected unauthorized program or data changes
- Auditor documents job relationships and assesses SoD adequacy
- SoD control matrix is a guideline, not an industry standard
- Automated tools can test access against SoD matrix; tailor predefined matrices
- Remote logging so privileged users cannot access own logs
- Compensating controls consider enterprise size and structure
#### SoD control mechanisms
- Transaction authorization by user department; periodic checks
- Ownership of assets assigned in writing to data owner
- Access to data
  - Physical, system and application security layers
  - Decisions based on policy, SoD and least privilege
  - Sensitivity levels: top secret, secret, confidential, unclassified
  - Labels indicate sensitivity
  - Mandatory vs discretionary policy-based controls
- Authorization forms
  - Formal management-approved access requests
  - Signature authorization logs for large or remote sites
  - Periodic privilege reviews
- User authorization tables
  - Define who can update, modify, delete, view data
  - Levels: system, transaction, field
  - Secure tables with passwords or encryption
  - Control log reviewed; exceptions investigated
#### Compensating controls for lack of SoD (detail)
- Audit trails: show who, when, type, fields, files updated
- Reconciliation by user department or data control group
- Exception reporting with evidence of supervisor handling
- Transaction logs: manual or automated
- Supervisory reviews: observation, inquiry or remote
- Independent reviews for small organizations
- Evaluate system-generated reports before relying on them
### 2.2.9 Auditing the IT governance structure and implementation
- Reviewing documentation
  - IT strategies, plans, budgets
  - Security policy documentation
  - Organization and functional charts
  - Job descriptions
  - IT steering committee reports
  - System development and program change procedures
  - Operations procedures
  - HR manuals
  - QA procedures
  - Check created as authorized and current
- Unclear roles: recommend defining accountability 📓⭐
- Governance effectiveness measured by stakeholder involvement 📓
#### Indicators of potential problems
- Excessive costs
- Budget overruns
- Late projects
- High staff turnover
- Inexperienced staff
- Frequent hardware/software errors
- Excessive backlog of user requests
- Slow computer response time
- Aborted or suspended development projects
- Unsupported or unauthorized purchases
- Frequent upgrades
- Extensive exception reports
- Exception reports not followed up
- Lack of succession plans
- Reliance on one or two key personnel
- Lack of adequate training
