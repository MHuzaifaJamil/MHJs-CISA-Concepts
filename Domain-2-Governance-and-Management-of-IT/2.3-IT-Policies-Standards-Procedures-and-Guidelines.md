# 2.3 IT Policies, Standards, Procedures and Guidelines

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Policies and standards: tools of governance and management respectively
- Procedures and guidelines: operations responsibility
- Policy hierarchy example (access): policy, standard (least privilege), procedure (role-based AP access), guideline (auto revoke after five days)
### 2.3.1 Policies
- Information security policy approved and reviewed by senior management 📓⭐
- Characteristics of a good policy
  - High-level statement of management intent, expectations, direction
  - Constitution of governance; aligned with strategic objectives
  - Static for long periods in mature enterprises
  - Lower-level policies by divisions consistent with high-level policies
  - States approver and review date
  - Updated for technology, risk, regulatory and process change
  - Supports business objectives, reinforced by IS controls
- Bottom-up development uses operational input and risk analysis 📓⭐
- Policy success needs employee comprehension 📓⭐
- Missing policies are systemic risk, not single failures 📓
- Employee policy manual: behavior standards, legal grounds for discipline 📓
#### Audit use of policies
- Policies in audit scope; test for compliance
- May rely on other assurance function's testing
- Policies are benchmark for evaluating compliance
- Report policies hindering business objectives
- Consider third-party and OSP applicability and conflicts
#### Information security policy (ISP)
- Rules protecting information and related technology
- Foundation of security infrastructure; guides behavior
- Balance control with productivity; control cost not above benefit
- Approved by senior management; communicated to employees, providers, partners
- Auditor uses ISP as reference framework
- ISO/IEC 27001 and 27002 as content benchmarks
- Elements of ISP document
  - Definition of information security
  - Scope and objectives
  - Management intent and expectations
  - Framework for control objectives and risk assessment
  - Compliance with legislative, regulatory, contractual requirements
  - Education, training and awareness requirements
  - Business continuity and disaster recovery
  - Consequences of violations
  - General and specific responsibilities, incident reporting
  - References to supplemental documentation
- Sign-off at hire and regularly (e.g., annually)
- Detailed internal version, summary external version
#### Policy set concerns
- High-level information security policy: CIA statements
- Data classification policy
- Acceptable use policy
- End-user computing policy (incl. personal devices)
- Access control policies
- Incident response policy
- Remote access / work-from-home policy
#### Review of the ISP
- Owner with management responsibility
- Review at planned intervals (at least annually) or on significant change
- Management review inputs
  - Feedback from interested parties
  - Independent review results
  - Preventive, detective, corrective action status
  - Previous review results
  - Process performance and compliance
  - Changes in environment, business, resources, legal, technical
  - Third parties, outsourcing, offshoring
  - Threat and vulnerability trends
  - Reported incidents
  - Regulator recommendations
- Management review outputs
  - Better alignment with business objectives
  - Better security management approach
  - Better risk assessment and management
  - Better control objectives and controls
  - Better resource and responsibility allocation
- Keep record with version control, revision and distribution dates
- Obtain management approval of revised policy
#### Auditor policy review checklist
- Basis or framework of policy
- Appropriateness and completeness
- Contents
- Exceptions and why (e.g., legacy apps)
- Approval process
- Implementation process
- Implementation effectiveness
- Awareness and training
- Periodic review and update process
### 2.3.2 Standards (mandatory specifications)
- Mandatory requirement, code of practice or specification from recognized standards body
- Criteria to judge whether procedures, processes, systems meet policy
- Policies are constitution, standards are laws
- Set boundaries: processes, people, technologies
- Set security baselines and acceptable risk appetite
- Updated for latest thinking and technology
- Professional standards: ISACA, IIA
### 2.3.3 Procedures (step-by-step)
- Documented, defined steps achieving policy objectives
- Derived from parent policy, within standards' boundaries
- Written clearly and concisely
- Formulated by process owners
- More dynamic than policies; frequent review
- Auditor identifies controls, evaluates design, tests operation
- Practice not matching procedure causes inconsistency
- Awareness is critical; use reliable deployment and automation
- Go through change management for updates
- Embed procedures in information systems
### 2.3.4 Guidelines (advisory) 📓
- Created by process owners to help execute procedures
- Clarify policies and standards
- Dependencies, suggestions, examples
- Narrative, background information, tools
