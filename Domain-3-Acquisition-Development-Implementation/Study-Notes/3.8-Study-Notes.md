## 3.8 Postimplementation Review (basics)

### Project closure versus review
- Term: Project closure.
  - Definition: Formal end of a project, done at completion.
  - Use: Gives accurate results, improves future projects, releases resources in order.
  - Audit impact: Missing closure means lessons and open issues are lost.
- Closure decides whether objectives were met or formally excused.
- Closure also records lessons learned, both mistakes and good practices.
- Term: Postimplementation review (PIR).
  - Definition: Look-back check of a live system after go-live.
  - Use: Confirms benefits, shortcomings and built-in controls are real.
  - Audit impact: Main evidence that controls work as designed.
- PIR happens weeks or months after completion, not at closure.
- Reason for delay: benefits and shortcomings only appear in real use.
- PIR is part of benefits realization.
- PIR estimates overall project success and business impact.
- PIR determines whether appropriate controls were built into the system. 📓⭐
- Validate control design and technical integrity is the audit priority. 📓⭐
- Example: A payroll system goes live; three months later, PIR finds duplicate payments.
- Exam traps
  - Closure and PIR are different events; PIR comes later with real-use data.
  - Best PIR answers stress control design and integrity, not just user happiness.

### Review scope
- PIR covers technical details and the process followed.
- Adequacy: does the system meet user requirements and business objectives?
- Adequacy: were controls defined and actually implemented?
- Projected cost versus benefits, or ROI measurements, are reviewed.
- Recommendations address inadequacies and deficiencies found.
- A plan to implement those recommendations is required.
- Development process: chosen methodologies, standards and techniques were followed.
- Development process: appropriate project management techniques were used.
- Residual risk of operating the system must be within acceptable levels.
- Term: Second joint review.
  - Definition: Repeat review by development team and users.
  - Use: Catches lessons not visible at first review.
  - Audit impact: Needs enough production time to judge effectiveness and value.
- Exam traps
  - A single early review cannot show true ROI; allow enough business cycles.
  - Acceptable risk is judged against appetite, not zero risk.

### Project closeout steps (figure 3.28)
- Step 1: assign outstanding issues to named individuals, with budget if applicable.
- Step 2: assign custody of contracts.
- Step 2: archive documentation or pass it to those needing it.
- Step 3: hold PIR with project team, development team, users, stakeholders.
- Step 3 aim: identify lessons learned for future projects.
- Step 3 content criteria: deliverable targets and extra objectives fulfilled.
- Step 3 content criteria: project incentives attained, schedule and costs adhered to.
- Step 3 process criteria: team dynamics and internal communication.
- Step 3 process criteria: relationships with external stakeholders.
- Step 4: document risks found, including risks of proper deliverable use.
- Step 4: update the risk register.
- Step 5: second PIR once true benefits and costs are realized.
- Step 5 result measures overall project success and business impact.
- ⚠ Check: Manual's step 3 list is "include the following"; outline matches.
- Exam traps
  - Order matters: issues and contracts first, then PIR, risks, then second PIR.
  - Outstanding issues need an owner and budget, not just a note.

### Effective review requirements
- Identify information to review during feasibility and design phases.
- Collect that information at every project stage.
- Checkpoints measure process effectiveness and accuracy of estimates.
- Example: Project manager checks software estimate accuracy at each milestone.
- Set business measurements before the project starts.
- Measure the same items again after implementation.
- Without a baseline, benefits cannot be proven.
- Allow enough business cycles to realize actual ROI.
- Exam traps
  - Baselines set after go-live are the wrong answer; they must be set up front.

### Measurements of critical success factors (figure 3.29)
- Term: Critical success factor (CSF) measure.
  - Definition: Business metric compared before and after implementation.
  - Use: Shows whether the system delivered value.
  - Audit impact: Gives objective evidence beyond opinions.
- Productivity: transactions per month and per user.
- ⚠ Check: Manual also lists dollars spent per user; outline omits it.
- Quality: number of discrepancies and disputes.
- Quality: occurrences of fraud or misuse detection.
- Economic value: total processing time reduction.
- Economic value: monetary value of administration costs.
- Customer service: turnaround time for customer question handling.
- Customer service: frequency of useful communication to users.
- Example: Disputes fall from 200 to 80 per month after the new billing system.

### Who performs the review
- Joint project team and end users perform an internal review.
- Their focus: critique the project process.
- Independent group, internal or external audit, can also perform it.
- Independent group focus: measure the business value delivered.
- Exam traps
  - Value measurement needs independence; the team reviewing itself is biased.

## 3.8.1 IS auditor's role in postimplementation review

### Independence and documentation
- Auditor must be independent of system development.
- An auditor who consulted on development must not perform the review.
- Reason: reviewing own advice is a self-review threat.
- Auditor focuses on control aspects of development and implementation.
- Document all audit involvement in work papers.
- Work papers support findings and recommendations.
- Reuse the documentation during maintenance and changes.
- Reuse purpose: validate, verify and test impact of changes.
- Periodically confirm the system still meets objectives cost-effectively.
- Periodic review also confirms control integrity still exists.
- Audit impact: Lost independence invalidates the review conclusions.
- Exam traps
  - Auditor advising on requirements may still attend, but cannot do the PIR.
  - Project team reviews critique process; auditor reviews concentrate on controls.

### Auditor functions
- Check objectives and requirements were achieved.
- Evidence: user usage, trouble tickets, work orders, satisfaction.
- Confirm feasibility-study cost benefits are measured, analyzed, reported accurately.
- Review program change requests for design or requirement problems.
- Many change requests suggest flaws in design, programming or requirements interpretation.
- Review that built-in controls operate as designed.
- Term: Enterprise asset management (EAM).
  - Definition: Module tracking and managing organizational assets and their upkeep.
  - Use: Testing key operations if included in the system.
  - Audit impact: Gives a ready way to test key operations.
- Review operators' error logs for resource or operating problems.
- Error logs may reveal poor planning or testing before implementation.
- Review input and output control balances and reports.
- Purpose: verify the system processes data accurately.
- Example: Output totals fail to reconcile with input batch totals.
- Exam traps
  - Trouble tickets and change requests are root-cause clues, not just workload.
  - Reviewing logs and balances beats relying on management assurance.

## Case study (ORM): stem summary
- Wonderwheels is a national outdoor-sports retailer expanding beyond its home country.
- Management asked an internal IS auditor for a high-level cardholder-protection readiness review.
- It is not a point-by-point standard compliance audit; that comes later.
- Initial facts: wireless POS registers use WEP encryption.
- Store POS servers send data to headquarters over strongly encrypted frame relay.
- Weekly sales data goes to a third party on magnetic media.
- The ERP is not GDPR compliant.
- A late database upgrade used phased customization and piloting on real data.
- The database went unpatched over two years; vendor support dropped.
- Internal audit was excluded from key compliance decisions.
- Transactions hang, data corrupt, and fixed errors keep recurring.
- ⚠ Check: Stem summary only; answers and questions are not covered here.

## Takeaway
- PIR comes weeks or months after go-live; closure comes earlier.
- Focus on control design, technical integrity and realized business benefits.
- Set baselines before the project; measure again after implementation.
- Reviewer must be independent; consulting auditors cannot perform the PIR.
- Change requests, error logs and balances reveal design and control problems.
- Second joint review needs enough production time.
