### 3.8 Closure versus postimplementation review

```mermaid
timeline
    title Project end and review
    section At completion
      Project closure : Objectives met or excused : Lessons learned : Resources released
    section Weeks or months later
      First PIR : Benefits and shortcomings visible : Controls validated
    section After enough production time
      Second joint review : True benefits and costs realized
```

| Aspect | Closure | PIR |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | At completion | Weeks or months after go-live |
| Purpose | Results, lessons, release resources | Confirm benefits and controls are real |
| Evidence | Objectives met or excused | Real-use data |

<details>
<summary><b>Definitions</b></summary>

- Project closure: formal end of a project at completion.
- PIR: look-back check of a live system after go-live.
- PIR is part of benefits realization.
- Audit priority: validate control design and technical integrity. 📓⭐

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Example</b></summary>

- Payroll goes live; three months later PIR finds duplicate payments.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Exam traps</b></summary>

- Closure and PIR are different events; PIR comes later.
- Best PIR answers stress control design and integrity, not user happiness.

</details>

### Review scope

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Postimplementation review"] --> B["Adequacy"]
    A --> C["Cost vs benefits or ROI"]
    A --> D["Recommendations<br/>and plan to implement"]
    A --> E["Development process"]
    A --> F["Residual risk"]
    B --> B1["Meets user requirements<br/>and business objectives"]
    B --> B2["Controls defined<br/>and implemented"]
    E --> E1["Methodologies and standards followed"]
    E --> E2["Project management techniques used"]
    F --> F1["Within acceptable levels"]
```

<details>
<summary><b>Definitions</b></summary>

- Second joint review: repeat review by development team and users.
- It needs enough production time to judge value.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Exam traps</b></summary>

- A single early review cannot show true ROI.
- Acceptable risk is judged against appetite, not zero risk.

</details>

### Project closeout steps (figure 3.28)

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    S1["Step 1: assign outstanding issues<br/>to individuals with budget"] --> S2["Step 2: assign custody of contracts<br/>archive or pass on documentation"]
    S2 --> S3["Step 3: postimplementation review<br/>team, users, stakeholders"]
    S3 --> S4["Step 4: document risks<br/>update risk register"]
    S4 --> S5["Step 5: second PIR after<br/>benefits and costs realized"]
    S3 -.-> C1["Content criteria:<br/>targets, incentives, schedule, costs"]
    S3 -.-> C2["Process criteria:<br/>team dynamics, communication, stakeholders"]
```

<details>
<summary><b>Exam traps</b></summary>

- Order: issues and contracts, PIR, risks, second PIR.
- Outstanding issues need an owner and budget, not just a note.
- Step 5 measures overall success and business impact.

</details>

### Effective review requirements

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A["Feasibility and design:<br/>identify info to review"] --> B["Collect at each stage<br/>checkpoints"]
    B --> C["Set business measurements<br/>before project"]
    C --> D["Implement"]
    D --> E["Measure same items again"]
    E --> F["Allow enough business cycles<br/>to realize ROI"]
```

<details>
<summary><b>Example</b></summary>

- Project manager checks estimate accuracy at each milestone.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Exam traps</b></summary>

- Baselines set after go-live are wrong; set them up front.
- Without a baseline, benefits cannot be proven.

</details>

### Measurements of critical success factors (figure 3.29)

| Category | Measures |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Transactions per month, transactions per user |
| Quality | Discrepancies, disputes, fraud or misuse detections |
| Economic value | Processing time reduction, administration cost value |
| Customer service | Question turnaround time, useful communication frequency |

<details>
<summary><b>Example</b></summary>

- Disputes fall from 200 to 80 per month after the new billing system.

</details>

### 3.8.1 IS auditor's role in postimplementation review

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A{"Did the auditor consult<br/>on development?"} -->|"yes"| B["Cannot perform the PIR<br/>self-review threat"]
    A -->|"no"| C["Independent: may perform PIR"]
    C --> D["Focus on controls of<br/>development and implementation"]
    D --> E["Document involvement<br/>in work papers"]
    E --> F["Reuse during maintenance<br/>and changes"]
    F --> G["Periodic check: objectives met<br/>cost-effectively"]
```

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A["Auditor functions"] --> B["Objectives achieved:<br/>usage, tickets, work orders, satisfaction"]
    A --> C["Feasibility cost benefits<br/>measured and reported"]
    A --> D["Program change requests:<br/>design or requirement flaws"]
    A --> E["Controls operate as designed<br/>EAM module if included"]
    A --> F["Operators error logs:<br/>poor planning or testing"]
    A --> G["Input and output<br/>control balances and reports"]
```

<details>
<summary><b>Definitions</b></summary>

- EAM: module tracking organizational assets and their upkeep.
- Many change requests suggest design, programming or requirements flaws.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Example</b></summary>

- Output totals fail to reconcile with input batch totals.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Exam traps</b></summary>

- A consulting auditor may attend but cannot do the PIR.
- Trouble tickets and change requests are root-cause clues.
- Logs and balances beat management assurance.
- Lost independence invalidates review conclusions.

</details>
