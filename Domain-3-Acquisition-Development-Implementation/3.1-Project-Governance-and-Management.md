# 3.1 Project Governance and Management

*PART A: Information Systems Acquisition and Development*

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Portfolio / program structure shows common objectives, risk, resource links
- Governance decisions driven through business case; periodic benefits review
- Governance structure involves project and functional line organization
- Hard factors: deliverables, quality, costs, deadlines
- Soft factors: team dynamics, conflict resolution, leadership, culture, communication
- Environmental factors: politics, stakeholder expectations, ethical and social issues
- Third-party transformation teams must align solution to business, not one-size-fits-all
- Grouped roles in small enterprises: auditor ensures SoD not compromised
- CISA not tested on a particular PM standard; know structures, policies, controls
### 3.1.1 Project management practices
- Application of knowledge, skills, tools, techniques to meet objective, budget, deadline
- Begins with project charter; ends with project completion
- Component processes: initiating, planning, executing, controlling, monitoring, closing
- Apply business process engineering design issues to the PM process itself
- Most IS projects divide into explicit phases (e.g., SDLC)
### 3.1.2 Project management structure
- Steering committee, sponsor, project manager, team
#### Organizational structure types
- Functional-structured: project manager has no formal authority, only advises
- Project-structured: project manager has formal authority over budget, schedule, team
- Matrix-structured: authority shared between project manager and department heads
- Sample project organization chart (figure 3.1)
  - Senior management, steering committee, project sponsor and user management at top
  - Programmers, key users, and software, hardware and network support shown
  - Support groups carry footnote: defined as system development management ➕

  - Chart also lists technical infrastructure ➕
#### Initiation and governance points
- IT steering committee prioritizes major project requests and picks project manager
- Project manager need not be IT staff; needs full operational control
- IS auditor on team as control expert in advisory role, not auditing
- Heavy auditor involvement may make auditor ineligible to audit application later
- IS projects may start in any part of enterprise, including IT
### 3.1.3 Project management roles and responsibilities
#### Project steering committee
- Provides overall direction; represents major stakeholders
- Ultimately responsible for deliverables, costs, schedules
- Senior representative from each significantly affected business area
- Project manager is a member
- Members empowered to decide on designs affecting their departments
- Includes project sponsor, who chairs
- Reviews progress regularly; emergency meetings when required
- Takes corrective action on escalated issues
#### Senior management
- Demonstrates commitment so needed people are involved
- Approves necessary resources
#### Project sponsor
- Provides funding
- Defines critical success factors and metrics with project manager
- Assumes ownership of data and application
- Typically senior manager of primary business unit supported
#### User management
- Assumes ownership of project and resulting system
- Allocates qualified representatives to team
- Participates in process redesign, requirements, test cases, acceptance testing, training
- Reviews and approves deliverables
#### User project team
- Completes assigned tasks; works to local standards
- Acts as subject matter experts for developers
- Advises project manager of plan deviations
#### Project manager
- Day-to-day management and leadership
- Keeps activities in line with overall direction
- Ensures appropriate departmental representation
- Ensures adherence to local standards and stakeholder quality expectations
- Resolves interdepartmental conflicts
- Monitors and controls costs and timetable
- Facilitates scope definition, manages budget, controls via schedule
- Line responsibility for dedicated project personnel
#### Quality assurance (QA)
- Reviews results and deliverables in and at end of each phase
- Confirms compliance with requirements
- Review points depend on SDLC used, system structure and magnitude, deviation impact
- Measures staff adherence to enterprise SDLC; advises on deviations
- Proposes process improvements or greater control points
#### Systems development management
- Provides technical support for hardware and software environments
- Assures compatibility with enterprise computing environment and IT direction
- Assumes operating support and maintenance after installation
#### Systems development project team
- Completes tasks; involves users in development
- Works to local standards
- Advises project manager of necessary plan deviations
#### Security officer (or security team)
- Ensures controls protect data per classification and security policy
- Consults throughout life cycle on security measures
- Reviews security test plans and reports before implementation
- Evaluates security documents for accreditation
- Monitors security effectiveness during operation
#### Information system security engineer
- Applies engineering principles to find and contain vulnerabilities
- Designs per defense in breadth and security in depth
- Ensures data privacy considerations uphold data subject rights
### 3.1.4 Project management techniques ⭐
- Gantt chart, PERT, CPM, critical path, timebox
- Provide quantitative and qualitative size estimating, scheduling, allocation, productivity measurement
#### Three intertwined elements
- Deliverables, duration, budget
- Demanding deliverables correlate with long duration and high budget
- Budget = resources required x fees or costs
- Resources estimated at start using size-estimation techniques
- R x D = TR (resources x duration = total resources, constant)
- Few resources: long project; many resources: short but unmanageable
#### Man-month dilemma
- LR and LD are practical limits of the curve
- Area of rectangle at any point proportional to budget
### 3.1.5 Portfolio / program management
- Project portfolio: all projects in enterprise at a point in time
- Program: projects and tasks linked by strategies, objectives, budgets, schedules
- Programs: more complex, longer, higher budget, higher risk, more strategic
- Example IS program: ERP implementation with infrastructure, BPR, training projects
- Example non-IS program: mergers and acquisitions
#### Program management objectives
- Manage scope, financials, schedules, objectives, deliverables
- Manage context, environment, communication, culture, organization
#### Program roles and governance
- Program owner, program manager, program team
- Program owner distinct from project owner
- Program-owner meetings and program-team meetings
- Program and project methodologies run in parallel, not combined
- Written assignment from program sponsor formally starts program
- Programs closely coupled; portfolio projects need not be
- Structures: expert pools, PMO, project portfolio groups
- Integrative tools: PM guidelines, standard project plans
#### Portfolio versus program example
- Individual activities are projects
- Related activities form a program
- All underway activities form the portfolio
### 3.1.6 Project Management Office (PMO)
- Project portfolio database
- PMO governs PM processes, not project content
- Permanent, adequately staffed structure
- Maintains and develops procedures and standards
- Objective: improve project and program management quality, secure success
- Auditor distinguishes auditing content versus procedural aspects
#### Project portfolio management objectives
- Optimize portfolio results, not individual projects
- Prioritize and schedule projects
- Coordinate internal and external resources
- Transfer knowledge across projects
#### Project portfolio database
- Mandatory; holds owner, schedules, objectives, type, status, cost
- Portfolio bar chart
- Profit-versus-risk matrix
- Project portfolio progress graph
### 3.1.7 Project benefits realization
- Ensures IT-enabled investments deliver promised measurable business value
- Capabilities delivered on time and within budget
- IT assets keep contributing to business value
- Plan looks beyond project cycle to total benefits and costs over system life
#### Key elements
- Describe benefits management
- Assign measure and target
- Establish tracking and measuring regimen
- Document assumptions
- Establish key responsibilities
- Validate benefits predicted
- Plan the benefit to be realized
#### Related points
- Four phases of benefits realization at project level
- Compromise among cost, quality, delivery time, reliability, dependability
- Failure to meet ROI objectives suggests weak SDLC and PM practices
- Continuous process; needs business sponsorship
- Allow time for technical problems to resolve before measuring benefits
- ROI example: benefit over cost, compared with enterprise cost of funds, for go/no-go
### 3.1.8 Project initiation
- Information gathered into terms of reference or project charter
- Charter states objective, stakeholders, project manager, sponsor
- Approval of project initiation document (PID) or project request document (PRD) authorizes start
#### Initiation methods
- One-on-one meetings
- Kick-off meetings
- Project start workshops: buy-in and common overview
- Combination of methods
- Document significant project events as project artifacts
### 3.1.9 Project objectives
- Objectives are action statements supporting project goals
- SMART: specific, measurable, attainable, realistic, timely
#### Breakdown structures
- Object breakdown structure (OBS): solution components hierarchy
- OBS helps with intangible results; avoids missing deliverables
- Work breakdown structure (WBS): tasks to build OBS elements
- WBS: manageable work units, communications tool, cost and resource baseline
- WBS is process-oriented and phased
- WBS detail basis for negotiating objectives
- Figure 3.5 example: Services Online objective with WBS Sales Application ➕
  - Example work packages: WP1 web page, WP2 sales interface ➕
#### Work packages (WPs)
- Distinct owner; main, additional, out-of-scope objectives
- Include dependencies and performance evaluation
- Top WBS level is final deliverable
- Sub-deliverables hold WPs assigned to a unit
- WPs not over 10 days
- WPs independent and not duplicated
- Task lists feed project schedule
- Schedule shows dates, percent complete, dependencies, names, stage boundaries
### 3.1.10 Project planning
- Determine scope, agreed with stakeholders
- Determine tasks, sequence, duration, priority
- Determine IT and non-IT resources
- Determine budget and source of funding
#### Information system development project cost estimation
- Analogous estimating: from prior projects; quickest
- Parametric estimating: statistical data; more accurate than analogous
- Bottom-up estimating: each activity costed; most accurate, most time-consuming
- Actual costs: extrapolate from past costs of same system
- Software size estimation
  - Methods determining relative physical size of software
  - Guides resource allocation, time and cost, effort comparison
  - Single-point estimates (e.g., source lines of code, SLOC) fail on complex systems
  - Multiple-point estimates developed to overcome limit
  - Modern tech: diagrams, objects, spreadsheet cells, queries, GUI widgets
- Function Point Analysis (FPA) ⭐
  - Multiple-point technique for large business applications
  - Indirect measure of software size
  - Counts inputs, outputs, files, interfaces, queries
  - Five counts: user inputs, outputs, inquiries, files, external interfaces
  - Each entry rated simple, average or complex
  - Weights: inputs 3/4/6, outputs 4/5/7, inquiries 3/4/6
  - Weights: files 7/10/15, external interfaces 5/7/10
  - Complexity adjustment: reliability, criticality, complexity, reusability, changeability, portability
  - FP metrics: productivity = FP/person-month, quality = defects/FP, cost = $/FP
  - Works for business apps; weaker for OS, process control, communications
  - COCOMO suits other software types
  - Exam does not test FPA calculation specifics
- Cost budgets
  - Personnel hours by type (analyst, programmer, clerical)
  - Machine hours (computer time, duplication, office, communication equipment)
  - External costs: third-party software, licences, contractors, training, certification, space
  - Budgeting totals human and machine effort; scheduling sequences tasks
  - Two-step costing: sum effort per phase, multiply by hourly rate
  - Revisit budget and schedule at milestones; analyze variances
  - Report variances to management timely
- Software cost estimation
  - Result of software size estimation; helps scope project
  - Cost drivers: source code language, execution time constraints
  - Cost drivers: main storage and data storage constraints
  - Cost drivers: computer access, target machine, security environment, staff experience
#### Scheduling and establishing the time frame
- Earliest start date: logical sequence, parallel tasks where possible
- Latest expected finish: hours, availability, holidays, recruitment time
- Gantt charts
  - Show start and end of activities on timeline
  - Show concurrent versus sequential activities
  - Show resource assignment and percent allocation
  - Compare early or late completion to baseline
  - Track milestones, such as phase end or key deliverable
- Critical path method (CPM)
  - Longest path through project; shortest possible completion time
  - Usually one critical path in nonmanipulated schedules
  - Critical activities have zero slack time
  - Noncritical activities have slack time
  - Forward and backward pass determine earliest completion times
  - Activities are branches; nodes join them in network
  - Resource leveling smooths resource peaks and valleys
  - Leveling uses heuristic methods, not algorithmic ones
- Program Evaluation Review Technique (PERT)
  - CPM-type technique using three duration estimates
  - Optimistic, most likely, pessimistic estimates
  - Formula: (optimistic + pessimistic + 4 x most likely) / 6
  - Assumes beta distribution; allows probability and confidence levels
  - Used where durations uncertain (e.g., pharmaceutical research, complex software)
  - Events are milestones; activities are arrows
  - First identify all activities, events and sequence
  - Critical path is only one in network; route to accelerate or delay project
- Timebox management
  - Fixed short time period with predetermined resources
  - Supports prototyping and RAD-type approaches
  - Prevents cost overruns and delivery delays
  - Still needs quality process
  - System test and UAT normally performed together
### 3.1.11 Project execution
- Program manager with PMO starts execution of planned tasks
- Monitor internal team and contractor/vendor production and quality metrics
- Key success factor: oversight of integrated team across requirements to transition to production
### 3.1.12 Project controlling and monitoring
- Controlling covers scope, resource usage, risk
- New requirements documented and allocated resources if approved
- Stakeholder satisfaction: accurate requirements, documentation, baselining, steering committee
- Metrics needed to monitor development effort
- First step: identify resources (skills, tools, facilities)
- Management of scope changes
  - Scope documented in WBS as part of baseline
  - Formal change request to project manager
  - Only stakeholders may submit change requests
  - Archive all change requests in project file
  - Project manager judges impact on scope, schedule, budget
  - Change advisory board evaluates for sponsor and recommends
  - Project manager updates plan if accepted
  - Sponsor formally confirms updated plan
- Management of resource usage
  - Resource usage is how budget is spent
  - Also monitor productivity
  - Earned value analysis (EVA) compares metrics at intervals
  - EVA metrics: budget to date, actual spending to date
  - EVA metrics: estimate to complete, estimate at completion
  - Estimate to complete exceeding remaining plan means overrun
- Management of risk
  - Risk: uncertain event or condition affecting project
  - Category 1: impacts business benefits; sponsor mitigates
  - Category 2: impacts project itself; project manager mitigates
### 3.1.13 Project closing
- Hand over system to users or support staff; assign outstanding issues
- Sponsor must be satisfied system is acceptable
- Closure questions: who issues notice, when, team release, open actions and funding
- Hand off documentation and duties; identify lessons learned
- Post project review is formal; postimplementation review is later
- Value metrics: total cost of ownership (TCO) and ROI
### 3.1.14 IS auditor's role in project management
- Take active role in life cycle where appropriate
- Understand system to identify vulnerabilities and control points
- Advise project team and senior management of deficiencies
#### Typical tasks
- Meet with key team members to learn components, objectives, user requirements
- Discuss control selection to rank major risks and exposures
- Discuss authoritative sources to identify mitigating controls
- Evaluate controls and advise on design and implementation
- Periodically review documentation and deliverables; confirm methodology followed
- Review application audit trails for security, edit, processing controls
- Identify and test production library security
- Review test plans for verification of requirements
- Analyze test results to evaluate maintenance process
- Review maintenance standards and test procedures applied as described
- Participate in postimplementation reviews
#### Change management system tracking
- History of work orders: date, programmer, changes, close date
- History of programmer logons and logoffs
- History of program deletions
- Adequacy of SoD and QA activities
