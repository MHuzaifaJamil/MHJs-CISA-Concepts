## 5.5.0 Basics: What DLP Is

- **Data loss prevention (DLP)**
  - Definition: tools and processes that stop loss or misuse of sensitive data.
  - Use: stops unauthorized users, especially outsiders, from obtaining or leaking data.
  - Audit impact: auditor checks that DLP matches policy and actually enforces it.
  - Also called data leakage prevention.
- DLP classifies regulated, confidential and business-critical data.
- DLP identifies violations of the organization's DLP policies.
- DLP enforces remediation through alerts and protective actions.
- Goal: stop accidental or malicious sharing that creates organizational risk.
- Consequences of data loss
  - Reduced productivity, because staff who need the data sit idle.
  - Higher costs to replace or regain control of data.
  - Legal exposure from loss of sensitive data.
  - Lost customer trust, lowering revenue and profit.
  - Risk of business closure if recovery funds cannot be raised.
- Example: a clerk emails a customer list outside; DLP detects and blocks it.

## 5.5.1 Types of DLP

- **Network-based DLP**
  - Definition: scans all outgoing data for restricted content or destinations.
  - Use: detects and stops restricted files leaving the organization in real time.
  - Audit impact: confirm coverage of all egress points and alert routing.
  - Uses deep packet inspection (DPI), which reads packet payload, not just headers.
  - Sends an alert, such as email, to the security administrator.
  - Depending on rules, may also quarantine or encrypt the data.
  - Example: blocks a spreadsheet of card numbers sent to a personal webmail.
- **Endpoint-based DLP**
  - Definition: scans files stored on a system and files sent to external devices.
  - Use: blocks copying to flash drives and sending sensitive data to printers.
  - Audit impact: verify agents are installed on all endpoints and cannot be disabled.
  - Admins configure scans for keywords; matching files trigger a blocked copy or print.
  - Works through an agent, a software program that is centrally managed.
- **Cloud DLP**
  - Definition: cloud-delivered DLP that monitors and protects data stored in the cloud.
  - Use: identifies sensitive data using a library of predefined data types.
  - Audit impact: check it covers sanctioned cloud services and remote workers.
  - Driven by growth in remote working.
  - Example: removes a sensitive attachment from an email.
- Exam traps
  - Network DLP sees data leaving the network, not files copied to USB.
  - Copy-to-USB and print control point to endpoint DLP, not network DLP.
  - Header-only inspection is a firewall function; DLP's distinction is payload inspection.

## 5.5.2 Data Loss Risk

- **Data loss risk**
  - Definition: chance sensitive data is corrupted, destroyed, exposed or stolen.
  - Use: framing risk assessment and choosing which DLP controls to prioritize.
  - Audit impact: auditor maps each cause category to a compensating control.
- Consequences: corruption, destruction, confidential exposure, and intellectual property (IP) theft.
- Technology causes
  - Loss or theft of devices.
  - Unencrypted data storage.
  - Software vulnerabilities and missing patches or updates.
  - Unsecure remote access tools.
  - Insecure communication platforms and insecure links to third parties.
  - Inappropriate access rights to applications holding sensitive data.
  - Poor system programming or design.
  - Production data processed in the development environment.
  - Manual also lists inherited data, unnecessary software and vendor product faults.
- Process causes
  - Poor data governance oversight.
  - No data retention policy.
  - No risk assessments.
  - Keeping or transmitting unencrypted data.
  - Not enforcing least privilege (POLP) and need to know.
  - Poor incident response practices.
  - Software setting misconfigurations.
  - Failure to close expired accounts.
  - Manual also lists unassessed data sensitivity and poor data monitoring systems.
- People causes
  - Negligence and lack of training or awareness.
  - Accidental deletion of files.
  - Emailing sensitive data without encryption.
  - Sharing work devices and passwords.
  - Insider threats: data theft, sabotage, fraud, often from discontent.
  - Exposure through blackmail via social engineering.
  - Copying data to remote systems to support work.
- Threat actors: malware propagation, software corruption, sabotage.
- Natural disasters: fires, earthquakes, tornados, liquid spills, power outages, temperature extremes.
- DLP use cases
  - Compliance: identify, classify and tag data; monitor activity for evidence.
  - IP protection: classify structured and unstructured IP against exfiltration.
  - Data visibility: track data across endpoints, networks and cloud.
  - Pervasiveness: span cloud and complex supply chain environments.
  - Frequent breaches: protects against nation states, criminals and malicious insiders.
  - Value of stolen data: stolen data is sold on the dark web.
- Exam traps
  - Most loss causes are people and process failures, not purely technical ones.
  - Best first response to a cause is the control targeting it, not new tools.

## 5.5.3 DLP Solutions and Data States

- **Data states**
  - Definition: the three conditions of information: at rest, in transit, in use.
  - Use: each state is covered by a different DLP technology.
  - Audit impact: auditor confirms all three states are covered, not just one.
  - Manual's heading text calls the second state "data in motion".
- **Data at rest**
  - Definition: data stored on systems and data stores.
  - Use: DLP identifies and logs where specific information types are stored.
  - Crawlers are applications that log onto each end system remotely.
  - Crawlers search data stores using rules from the DLP management console.
  - Example: finding credit card or social security numbers on a file server.
- **Data in transit**
  - Definition: data moving across the network.
  - Use: network appliances or embedded technology selectively capture and analyze traffic.
  - Files travel as packets; DLP passively monitors and captures the right streams.
  - It reassembles packets into files and applies the same analysis as at rest.
  - DPI is the core of this ability.
  - On detection, it alerts and may block in real or near real time.
  - It may also quarantine or encrypt the data.
- **Data in use**
  - Definition: data being handled by users at workstations.
  - Use: monitors copying to flash drives, printing, and cut and paste.
  - An agent does this, ideally controlled by central DLP management.
- Exam traps
  - Crawlers suit data at rest; agents suit data in use; appliances suit transit.
  - Encrypting stored data does not replace monitoring of data in use.

## 5.5.4 DLP Controls

- Auditors perform regular audits to assure control efficiency and effectiveness.
- Auditors also advise on improving controls and reducing data leakage.
- **Governance controls**
  - Craft DLP policy, procedures and guidelines.
  - Classify data and assign sensitivity levels.
  - Perform data loss risk assessment regularly.
  - Update data risk profiles regularly to spot new threats.
  - Standardize endpoints to simplify deployment.
  - Document all data loss incidents.
  - Periodically audit compliance with data protection requirements.
- **People controls**
  - Do not leave sensitive data exposed and unattended.
  - Prohibit copying sensitive data to removable media.
  - Provide read-only access to sensitive information.
  - Include data protection clauses in employment contracts.
  - Require data owner authorization before data export.
  - Train and develop employees in data protection.
- **DLP solution deployment controls**
  - Deploy using a risk-based approach.
  - Fine-tune policies to organizational requirements.
  - Test the implementation.
  - Encrypt data in transit and at rest.
- **IT controls**
  - Closely monitor all sensitive data channels.
  - Prohibit unauthorized devices on the network.
  - Block files containing personally identifiable information (PII).
  - Configure removable storage as read-only.
  - Use multilayered authorization and access controls.
  - Encrypt backups containing sensitive information.
  - Implement firewall and antivirus technologies.
- **Procurement controls**
  - Check solution compatibility with the organization's data formats.
  - Choose a product giving real-time reports of policy violations.
- Exam traps
  - Policy and classification come before technology; tools cannot protect unclassified data.
  - Preventive controls beat detective ones when the question asks to reduce loss.

## 5.5.5 DLP Content Analysis Methods

- **Content analysis**
  - Definition: how DLP inspects data to decide whether a policy is violated.
  - Use: triggers policy violations that start alerts or blocking.
  - Audit impact: method choice drives false positive and false negative rates.
- **Regular expression matching**
  - Definition: matches set patterns, such as 16-digit card numbers.
  - Use: serves as the first filter for sensitive data.
  - Weakness: without checksum validation, false positives are high.
- **Structured fingerprinting**
  - Definition: algorithms map data to short unique strings called fingerprints.
  - Use: protects structured data such as databases and directory services.
  - Database fingerprinting is also called exact data matching (EDM).
  - Applied to forms, it detects social security or card numbers in transit.
- **Exact file matching (EFM)**
  - Definition: compares file hashes against exact fingerprints; content is not analyzed.
  - Use: detects whether file content has changed.
  - Strength: low false positives.
  - Weakness: ineffective for files with different versions.
- **Indexed document matching (IDM)**
  - Definition: fingerprinting applied to unstructured data.
  - Use: protects Office documents, PDFs, JPEGs and multimedia files.
  - Detects derived content, such as text copied into another file.
- **Lexicon matching**
  - Definition: also called conceptual analysis; uses dictionary terms and rule-based matches.
  - Use: analyzes unstructured data for sensitive ideas.
  - Weakness: needs customization to the DLP solution in place.
- **Statistical analysis**
  - Definition: uses machine learning and Bayesian analysis.
  - Use: finds obscure sensitive information other methods miss.
  - Needs huge data volumes; yields false positives and false negatives.
- **Categorization**
  - Definition: applies prebuilt categories with rules for highly sensitive data.
  - Use: judges whether data is highly sensitive or breaches compliance.
  - Example: a prebuilt credit card information category.
- ⚠ Check: outline omits EFM's weakness; it fails on modified file versions.
- Exam traps
  - Exact-match methods give few false positives but miss edited copies.
  - Derived or reworded unstructured content points to IDM, not EFM.
  - Structured database records point to EDM, not IDM.
  - Pattern-only matching overflags; checksum validation reduces false positives.

## 5.5.6 DLP Deployment Best Practices

- **DLP policy creation**
  - Develop a policy covering loss prevention and component actions.
  - Customize preconfigured policies that map to common regulations.
- **Define business and security requirements**
  - Do this before deployment.
  - Compliance and cybersecurity standards shape the deployment.
- **Employee involvement**
  - Every employee is responsible for data security standards.
  - IS staff should join deployment to answer queries and fix errors.
- **Senior management support**
  - Leaders from all departments must back the rollout.
  - They then supervise their staff in applying best practices.
- **Deployment mode**
  - Passive (monitoring) mode: leaks are analyzed but not blocked.
  - Active mode: blocks leakage traffic and informs the DLP administrator.
- Incorporate strong encryption into DLP solutions.
- Address insider threats; most threats are launched internally.
- Data classification
  - Start with the most valuable or sensitive data.
  - Then cascade to less sensitive data.
- Risk assessment
  - Data on user devices or with third parties carries different risk.
  - Data is at highest risk when in use on endpoints.
  - Program must account for mobility of data.
- Control documentation
  - Early controls are simple; they grow more complex as the program matures.
  - Document environment changes and procedures to prevent employee mistakes.
- Training and development reduces accidental insider data loss.
- Workflow management
  - Route incidents to the appropriate parties for resolution.
  - Directory service integration lets the console map network addresses.
  - Backup and restore preserve policies and configuration settings.
  - Reporting may use external reporting tools.
- Audit infrastructure: audit the whole DLP solution and supporting infrastructure regularly.
- Exam traps
  - Start in passive mode, tune, then move to active blocking.
  - Begin with the most sensitive data, not all data at once.
  - Management support and policy precede tool selection.

## 5.5.7 DLP Risk, Limitations and Considerations

- **Improperly tuned network DLP modules**
  - Tune and test before enabling content blocking.
  - Monitor-only mode allows tuning and warns users of non-compliant activity.
  - Involve business and IT stakeholders in planning and monitoring.
  - Provide an access path if critical content is blocked off-hours.
  - Audit impact: blocked business processes with no after-hours support is a finding.
- **Excessive reporting and false positives**
  - Similar to an improperly configured IDS.
  - False positives overwhelm staff and hide valid hits.
  - Avoid heavy use of template patterns or black-box solutions.
  - Customize rules to the organization's data patterns.
  - Roll out in phases, highest-risk areas first.
  - Too many patterns or detection points too soon overwhelms resources.
- **Encryption limits inspection**
  - Encrypted content cannot be read by DLP.
  - Manual refers readers to section 5.6 Data Encryption.
- **Graphics limitation**
  - DLP cannot intelligently interpret graphics files.
  - Scanned data and design documents in image form create a gap.
  - DLP can still identify file types, source and destination.
  - Traffic analysis may flag uncharacteristic movement of such files.
  - Mitigate by blocking, manual inspection, or strong IP-handling policies.
- Exam traps
  - Immediate blocking at go-live is wrong; tuning in monitor mode comes first.
  - More rules do not mean better protection; they raise false positives.
  - DLP does not remove the need for encryption and access control.

## Takeaway

- DLP classifies data, detects policy violations, and enforces remediation.
- Network, endpoint and cloud DLP cover different channels; match tool to channel.
- Use crawlers at rest, appliances in transit, agents in use.
- Exact-match methods are precise but brittle; statistical methods are broad but noisy.
- Deploy passive first, tune, then block; start with most sensitive data.
- Known blind spots: encrypted content and graphics files.
