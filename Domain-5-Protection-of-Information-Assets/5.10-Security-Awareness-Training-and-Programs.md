# 5.10 Security Awareness Training and Programs

*PART B: Security Event Management*

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- People are the weakest link; programs reduce attacks
### 5.10.1 Information security learning continuum
- Awareness
  - Changes behavior; passive learning; starts at hire
  - Answers "what"; short term; self-paced e-learning, videos
  - Testing: short quizzes, true/false, multiple choice
- Training
  - Teaches job skills; more formal; functional areas
  - Answers "how"; intermediate term; instructor-led, hands-on
  - Testing: application-level problem solving
- Education
  - For security professionals; colleges, universities
  - Answers "why"; long term; seminars, research
  - Testing: essays, architecture exercises
### 5.10.2 Benefits of a security awareness, training and education program
- Supports individual accountability
- Serves as preventive control
- Serves as detective control
- Lowers security risk
- Foundation for other security controls
### 5.10.3 Approach
- Training for all employees and third parties; before access for new hires
- Questions: audience, message, intended result, method, culture
- Mechanisms for raising awareness
  - Computer-based training
  - Email reminders and security tips
  - Written policies and updates
  - Nondisclosure statements
  - Newsletters, web pages, videos, posters, login reminders
  - Visible enforcement
  - Simulated security incidents
  - Rewarding reporters of suspicious events
  - Periodic reviews, job descriptions, performance reviews
- Three steps: design, develop material, implement
### 5.10.4 Conditions for a successful program
- Security culture
- Policy incorporating awareness, training, education
- Inform everyone of roles and responsibilities
- Monitoring and review processes
- Organization-wide focus
- Tone at the top
- Simple rules of behavior
- Basis for penalties and sanctions
- Rollout plan after needs assessment
### 5.10.5 Conducting a needs assessment
- Roles analyzed
  - Executive management
  - Information security personnel
  - System owners
  - System administrators and IS support
  - Operational staff and system users: riskiest group
- Information sources for needs assessment
  - Interviews with key personnel
  - Organizational security surveys
  - Review of existing training material
  - Metrics, for example percent trained in social engineering
  - Security plans and role reviews
  - System access databases
  - Internal audit findings
  - Discussions with management and system owners
  - Analysis of security events, incidents, attacks
  - Industry trends
### 5.10.6 Implementing an awareness and training program
- Define program scope
- Select training staff
- Identify target audience
- Motivate management and trainees
- Administer the program
- Maintain the program
- Evaluate the program
  - Student questionnaires
  - Observation of employees
  - Testing employees
  - Monitoring incidents before and after
  - Focus groups
  - Interviews with selected trainees
- Regular awareness training is the best defense against phone-based social engineering 📓⭐
