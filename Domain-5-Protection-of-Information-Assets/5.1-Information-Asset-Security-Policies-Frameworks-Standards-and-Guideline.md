# 5.1 Information Asset Security Policies, Frameworks, Standards and Guidelines

*PART A: Information Asset Security and Control*

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

### 5.1.1 Policies, procedures and guidelines
- Information security policy: high-level rules set by senior management and board
- Auditor reviews policy date of last update for currency
- Key elements of a policy
  - Scope: where information is located, who can access it
  - Policy statement: approach, laws and regulations, information types handled
  - Objectives: SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound)
  - Objective: confidentiality
  - Objective: integrity
  - Objective: availability
  - Objective: security guidance
  - Objective: monitoring
  - Objective: governance
  - Objective: compliance
  - Objective: privacy
- Characteristics of an information security policy
  - Executive support
  - Business-driven
  - Simple and user-friendly
  - Integrated, with issue-specific policies
  - Accessible (for example, on intranet)
  - Strategic, long-term focus
  - Professional
  - Penalties for noncompliance
  - Flexible
  - Regularly updated; revisions record date and responsible department
- Types of policies
  - Organizational (master) information security policy
  - System-specific policy (SysSP): single system, often enforced by access controls
  - Issue-specific policy (ISSP): addresses specific threats
- Issue-specific policy examples
  - Acceptable use policy
  - Change control policy
  - Access control policy
  - Incident response policy
  - IAM policy
  - Remote access policy
  - BYOD policy
- Information security procedures
  - Step-by-step tasks to achieve an objective
  - Lowest level in the documentation chain, closest to systems and users
  - Cover OS install, account setup, privileges, audit, data destruction, incident reporting
- Information security guidelines
  - Less prescriptive than procedures; recommendations
  - Open to interpretation, need not be strictly followed
  - Assist implementation of standards
### 5.1.2 Information security frameworks and standards
- Standard: set of mandatory activities or rules supporting policies
- Advantages of standards
  - Repeatable, consistent security management
  - Common understanding of terms and concepts
  - Product functionality and compatibility
- Framework: processes defining policies and procedures for a security program
- Framework choice depends on location, industry, compliance expectations
- ISO/IEC 27001 and 27002 ➕
  - ISO/IEC 27001: information security management system (ISMS) specification
  - Covers organizational, people, physical and technological controls
- NIST ➕
  - NIST CSF: standards, guidelines and best practices for cybersecurity risk; any sector
- COBIT 📓
  - IT governance framework; minimizes IT risk and ensures efficient resource use
- Other frameworks and standards in ORM
  - Zachman Framework: classifies enterprise architectures
  - TOGAF: aligns business and architecture objectives
  - SABSA: risk-driven security enterprise architecture, lifecycle model
  - ITIL: IT service management standardization
  - CIS Critical Security Controls (CSC): technical controls, no risk management
  - PCI DSS: cardholder data security, applies to outsourced payment operations
  - CSA STAR: assurance framework for cloud service providers
  - Cloud Control Matrix (CCM): 197 control objectives in 17 domains
- Reasons to use a framework
  - Improved security management
  - Effective risk management
  - Effective incident response
  - Increased market reach
  - Increased security reliability
- Auditing frameworks
  - Review logical access, security testing and investigation techniques
  - Also review risk management, risk assessment results, control design, incident management
### 5.1.3 Information security baselines
- Baseline: minimum security level a system, network or device must meet
- Example baseline: minimum C2 trusted computer system evaluation standard
- Baseline topics (Figure 5.3)
  - Environment/inventory: maintain inventory with OS and owner
  - Malware: antivirus with automatic updates, at least weekly
  - Passwords: strong passwords; departmental accounts avoid sharing
  - Patching: automatic OS and software patching
  - Minimizing services: remove unnecessary services
  - Addressing vulnerabilities: remove default and unused accounts
  - Backups: offsite, enable recovery from user mistakes and hardware failure
- Baseline evaluation checklist topics (Figure 5.4)
  - Environment/inventory, malware, passwords, patching
  - Minimizing services, vulnerabilities/auditing, backup and recovery/BCP
  - IT staff, privacy
  - MFA enabled for cloud-based systems
  - Patches tested before applying; machine backed up first
  - Immutable backups and broker service
- Access standards
  - Auditor checks they separate duties, prevent fraud or error, meet policy
  - Defined at generic level, for specific machines, for specific applications
  - Example: all passwords at least eight characters
