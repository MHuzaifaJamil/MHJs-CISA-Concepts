# 5.2 Physical and Environmental Controls

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Facility management often designs these controls, not IT or security manager
- Environmental controls: temperature, humidity, power
### 5.2.1 Environmental exposures and controls
- Equipment issues and exposures
  - Causes: lightning, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, hurricanes, tornados
  - Power failure categories
    - Total failure (blackout)
    - Severely reduced voltage (brownout)
    - Sags, spikes and surges
    - Electromagnetic interference (EMI)
  - Short-term interruptions: surge protectors
  - Intermediate-term interruptions (seconds to 30 minutes): UPS
  - Long-term interruptions (hours to days): alternative generators (diesel, gasoline, propane)
  - Water damage and flooding: broken pipes, even on upper floors
  - Other causes: terrorist threats, vandalism, electrical shock, equipment failure
  - Questions: power within spec, air conditioning, humidity, ventilation adequate
  - Questions: antistatic protection, dust-free, food and tobacco prohibited
  - Questions: backup media protected, equipment stored safely, maintenance scheduled
- Controls for environmental exposures
  - Evaluate co-located, outsourced and cloud datacenter exposures
  - Alarm control panels
    - Separate from burglar systems
    - Accessible to fire department at all times
    - Weatherproof box, controlled room, dedicated power circuit
    - Control or disable separate zones
  - Water detectors
    - Under raised floors and near drain holes
    - Audible alarm; location marked on floor
  - Smoke detectors
    - Above and below ceiling tiles, below raised floor
    - Linked to monitored station
    - Supplement, not replace, fire suppression
  - Handheld fire extinguishers: strategic locations, inspected at least annually
  - Manual fire alarms: near exit doors, linked to guard station
  - Fire suppression systems
    - Activate automatically on high heat; inspected annually
    - Trigger fire doors, fire department, duct closure, power shutdown
    - Segmented so one zone does not activate all
    - Total flooding method: agent fills enclosed 3D space
    - Local application method: agent applied directly onto fire
    - Water-based sprinkler: water always in pipes, leak risk
    - Dry-pipe sprinkler: water flows only after alarm activates pump
    - FM-200 (HFC-227ea): often preferred
    - Novec 1230: clean agent, FM-200 alternative
    - Auditor may review documentation of tests instead of testing
  - Strategic location of computer room
    - Avoid basement and top floor; middle floors best
    - Avoid adjacent water or gas pipes, paper storage, airports, chemical plants
  - Regular inspection by fire department annually
  - Emergency power-off switch
    - Two switches: inside room and outside near room
    - Labeled, shielded against accidental activation, video surveillance
  - Fireproof walls, floors and ceilings: true floor to true ceiling, two-hour fire rating
  - Electrical surge protectors and voltage regulators, often built into UPS
  - UPS/generator
    - Cleanses power and bridges outage for orderly shutdown
    - Battery or gasoline-powered generator
  - Power leads from two substations: redundant power lines
  - Wiring in fire-resistant electrical panels and conduit
  - Inhibited activities: food, drink, tobacco prohibited in IPF
  - Fire-resistant office materials; nonflammable cleaning fluids
  - Documented and tested emergency evacuation plans
    - Human safety first; do not leave IPF insecure
    - Controlled shutdown procedure
  - Humidity and temperature control
### 5.2.2 Physical access exposures and controls
- Physical access exposures
  - Unauthorized entry
  - Vandalism, damage or theft of equipment or documents
  - Copying or viewing sensitive or copyrighted information
  - Alteration of sensitive equipment and information
  - Public disclosure of sensitive information
  - Abuse of data processing resources
  - Blackmail
  - Embezzlement
  - Wiretapping/eavesdropping
  - Possible perpetrators
    - Disgruntled, on-strike, threatened or terminated employees
    - Employees with addiction or financial or emotional problems
    - Former employees
    - Competitors, thieves, organized crime, hackers
    - Accidental non-malicious actors
  - Most likely source: uninformed or accidental person; greatest impact: malicious
  - Facilities to protect
    - Computer rooms, tape libraries, storage rooms
    - Offsite backup storage, communications closets
    - Operator consoles, portable equipment, printers, LANs
    - Power sources, disposal sites
  - Safeguards extend to access points, third parties and service providers
- Physical access controls
  - Hidden cameras are detective; visible cameras and signs deter 📓
  - Bolting door locks: metal key stamped "do not duplicate"
  - Combination (cipher) locks: change combination on staff transfer or termination
  - Electronic door locks: card, token or biometric
    - Card assignable to individual
    - Restrict by door or hour
    - Hard to duplicate; easy to deactivate
    - Silent or audible alarms on unauthorized attempt
  - Biometric door locks
  - Manual logging: visitor log, ID verification
  - Electronic logging: unsuccessful attempts highlighted
  - Identification badges: visitor badges different color
  - Video cameras: monitored, retained, resolution sufficient to identify
  - Security guards: bonded if supplied by external agency
  - Controlled visitor access: escorted
  - Lighting around premises
  - Human safety takes precedence
  - Bonded service personnel limit financial exposure, not physical risk
  - Mantrap/airlock (access control vestibule)
    - Two doors, one person at a time
    - Reduces piggybacking
    - Full-height turnstile achieves similar effect
  - Deadman doors for delivery and dispatch areas
  - Computer workstation locks
  - Controlled single entry point with receptionist
  - Alarm system on inactive entry points, motion detectors, reverse-flow doors
  - Secured document distribution carts
  - Computer room not visible or identifiable: no windows or signs
- Auditing physical access
  - Tour computer site and offsite storage
  - Contractual right to audit third-party sites
  - Review documents: evacuation procedures, inspection tags, suppression tests, key lock logs
  - Related facilities: operator consoles, printer rooms, UPS/generator, media storage
  - Look above ceiling panels and below raised floor
  - Check walls extend to real ceiling
  - Entry paths: entry and emergency doors, glass, movable walls
  - Entry paths: suspended ceilings, raised floors, ventilation, behind curtains
### 5.2.3 Industrial Control Systems (ICS) security
- ICS types
  - SCADA systems
  - Programmable logic controllers (PLCs)
  - Remote terminal units (RTUs)
- ICS risk
  - Poor security posture
  - Improper input validation
  - Improper authentication processes
  - Poor security configuration and maintenance
  - Embedded software with Internet-connected controllers
  - Network design weaknesses: no zones, no port security
  - Technological depreciation cycle: limited processing for crypto
  - Incompatibility with information security standards
  - Weak information security protocols
  - Dependencies on other ICT systems
  - Presence of third parties on site
  - Compliance risk
- ICS security best practices
  - Conduct ICS asset inventory and classification
  - Monitor ICS network baselines
  - Segment ICS networks with ICS-protocol-aware firewalls
  - Implement principle of least privilege (POLP)
  - Deploy IDS/IPS
  - Secure remote access: encryption
  - Secure physical access: guards, badges
  - Implement redundancy
  - Harden systems and networks: block unused ports, patch
  - Develop incident response plan (IRP)
  - Supply chain security: assess vendors for vulnerabilities and malicious code
