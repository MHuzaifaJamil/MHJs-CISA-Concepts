## 4.7.0 Basics: general controls

- General controls: controls over hardware, software, telecommunications, networks, applications and data.
  - Definition: Controls covering all computer resources, not one application.
  - Use: Protect the business because it relies on computer processing.
  - Audit impact: Weak general controls undermine reliance on application controls.
- Problem and incident management are operational general controls.
  - Audit impact: Auditor tests whether failures are detected, logged, escalated and resolved.

## 4.7.1 Problem management

- Incident: an unplanned event that disrupts or degrades a service.
  - Definition: A single disruption needing service restoration.
  - Use: Triggers the incident handling process.
  - Audit impact: Unlogged incidents hide recurring weaknesses.
- Problem: the underlying cause behind one major or several similar incidents.
  - Definition: Root issue that produces repeated incidents.
  - Use: Investigated in depth to remove the cause.
  - Audit impact: Unmanaged problems cause recurring outages.
- Problem management: investigation of major or similar incidents to find root cause.
  - Definition: Proactive analysis of why incidents happen.
  - Use: Reduce the number and severity of incidents.
  - Audit impact: Check that recurring incidents trigger problem records.
  - Example: Weekly server crashes lead to finding one faulty memory driver.
- Root cause analysis (RCA): finding the original reason, not the symptom.
  - Definition: Structured search for the underlying cause.
  - Use: Choose a fix that prevents recurrence.
  - Audit impact: Auditors run RCA before reporting findings to management. 📓
- Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram: cause-and-effect chart sorting causes into categories.
  - Definition: Fish-skeleton diagram with causes branching off the problem.
  - Use: Visualize all possible causes of one problem.
- Brainstorming: group idea generation about possible causes.
  - Use: Surface causes the team might otherwise miss.
- 5 Whys: ask "why" repeatedly to trace cause and effect.
  - Definition: Iterative questioning, each answer feeds the next question.
  - Use: Drill from symptom down to root cause.
  - Example: Site down, disk full, logs unrotated, no job, no owner.
  - ⚠ Check: "Five" is a guideline; stop when the root cause is reached.
- Known error: a problem whose root cause is identified.
  - Definition: Understood fault, fix may not yet exist.
  - Use: Allows a workaround to be developed.
- Workaround: temporary way to avoid or reduce a known error's impact.
  - Use: Restore service quickly if the incident recurs.
  - Audit impact: A workaround is not a permanent fix; track the permanent fix.
- Known error database (KEDB): repository of known errors and their workarounds.
  - Definition: Searchable store of documented known errors.
  - Use: Help desk resolves repeat incidents immediately.
  - Audit impact: Stale or missing KEDB entries slow resolution and repeat effort.
- Problem management versus incident management:
  - Problem management reduces number and severity of incidents.
  - Incident management restores normal service quickly, minimizing business impact.
  - Problem management is proactive; incident management is reactive.
- Exam traps
  - Question about permanent prevention: choose problem management, not incident handling.
  - Question about fastest restoration: choose incident management, not root cause analysis.
  - A workaround alone is wrong when recurrence prevention is asked.

## 4.7.2 Process of incident handling

- Incident management: critical IT service management (ITSM) process.
  - Definition: Process to respond to nonstandard events affecting IT services.
  - Use: Restore normal service as defined by the service level agreement (SLA).
  - Audit impact: Verify the process is documented, followed and monitored.
- Incident life cycle stages, in order:
  - Initiation
  - Classification
  - Assignment to specialists
  - Resolution
  - Closure
- Prioritization uses impact and urgency.
  - Definition: Impact is business harm; urgency is how soon it must be fixed.
  - Use: Decide which incident is handled first.
  - Example: A server crash outranks a senior executive's printer problem.
  - Audit impact: IS management must define documented priority parameters.
- Escalation: passing unresolved incidents to higher levels or specialists.
  - Use: Prevent incidents lingering past acceptable times.
  - Audit impact: Escalation must follow criteria set by IS management.
- SLA: agreement defining acceptable service and incident statistics.
  - Use: Benchmark resolution performance.
  - Audit impact: Compare actual incident statistics against SLA ranges.
- Exam traps
  - Priority is impact plus urgency, not who reported the incident.
  - Incident handling is reactive; do not pick it for root cause removal.

## 4.7.3 Detection, documentation, control, resolution and reporting of abnormal conditions

- Abnormal condition log: automated or manual record of possible errors.
  - Definition: Central log of conditions that may indicate an error.
  - Use: Detect, track and resolve errors systematically.
  - Audit impact: No log means no evidence errors were handled.
- Typical errors logged:
  - Application errors
  - System errors
  - Network errors
  - Telecommunication errors
  - Operator errors
  - Hardware errors
- Error log entry should contain:
  - Error date, error code, description and source.
  - Escalation date and time.
  - Department or center responsible for resolution.
  - Status code: open, closed pending future date, or irresolvable currently.
  - Narrative of resolution status.
  - Initials of the log maintainer and of the closer.
- Access control over the log:
  - Adding entries should not be restricted, for control purposes.
  - Updating entries is limited to authorized people.
  - Updates must be traceable.
  - ⚠ Check: Outline says "anyone may add"; manual says adding should not be restricted.
- Separation of duties: the person closing differs from the log maintainer or initiator.
  - Audit impact: Same person opening and closing allows concealed errors.
- Risk of unresolved problems:
  - Primary risk: interruption of business operations.
  - Also possible corruption of production data.
  - Indefinite unresolved status is never acceptable.
- Escalation procedures should document:
  - Names and contact details of specialists per problem type.
  - Problem types needing urgent resolution.
  - Problems that can wait until normal working hours.
- Resolution should be communicated to systems, programming, operations and user staff.
- Auditor tests
  - Problems assigned to individuals or groups most capable of resolving them.
  - Problems resolved on time.
  - Responsible departments appear in problem management documentation.
- Exam traps
  - Restricting log additions is wrong; restrict updates and closure instead.
  - Closer being the log maintainer is a separation of duties failure.

## 4.7.4 Support / help desk

- Technical support: specialist knowledge of production systems.
  - Use: Assist change, development and problem resolution.
  - Use: Inform management of technologies that may benefit operations.
  - Audit impact: Support procedures must follow organizational strategy and policy.
- Help desk (service desk): first, single, central point of contact for users.
  - Definition: Unit that receives user calls and tickets.
  - Use: Serve the user and follow the incident management process.
  - Audit impact: Verify all hardware and software incidents are fully documented.
- Triage: sorting tickets at intake, then escalating by complexity and expertise.
  - Audit impact: Escalation follows priorities set by management.
- Typical support functions:
  - Determine incident source and take corrective action.
  - Initiate problem reports as required.
  - Hold detailed network, system and application knowledge.
  - Provide second- and third-tier support.
  - Maintain vendor software documentation, releases and fixes.
  - Signal abnormal call patterns to IS operations.
  - Answer inquiries about specific systems.
  - Provide technical support for telecommunications processing.
  - Document in-house utilities and systems.
- Abnormal call patterns act as an early warning of problems.
  - Example: Sudden login-failure calls reveal an authentication outage.
- Exam traps
  - Help desk is the single contact; users should not call specialists directly.
  - Documentation of every incident matters even when resolved at first contact.

## 4.7.5 Network management tools

- Response time reports: time from user command to host answer.
  - Use: Track slow response; investigate bottlenecks in I/O, bandwidth, CPU.
- Downtime reports: track availability of telecommunication lines and circuits.
  - Use: Show interruptions from power failure, overload, operator error.
  - Remedies include added lines, dependable links, backup power, better access controls.
  - Remedies include software-defined networking (SDN) to bundle connectivity.
- Help desk reports: history of problems and their resolutions.
  - Use: Spot recurring problems and trends.
- Online monitors: check transmission accuracy and errors.
  - Echo checking: received data bounced back to sender for verification.
  - Status checking: ensures messages are not lost or sent twice.
- Network monitors: real-time display of network nodes and statuses.
- Protocol (network) analyzer: diagnostic tool attached to a network link. 📓⭐
  - Definition: Captures and records real-time packet data on a link.
  - Use: Produces network use reports and diagnoses traffic problems.
  - Typically hardware-based, operating at data link or network level.
  - Output: protocols in use, packet types, traffic volume, errors, bandwidth percentage.
  - Audit impact: Can expose sensitive cleartext traffic; restrict and control its use.
  - ⚠ Check: "Audit impact" on cleartext exposure is my inference, not manual text. ➕
- Simple Network Management Protocol (SNMP): TCP/IP protocol that monitors and controls network devices.
  - Definition: Console polls agents running on each device.
  - Use: Collect performance and security statistics; manage configurations.
  - Every router, switch, hub, PC and server needs an agent.
  - Audit impact: Messages carry sensitive data; manual advises SNMPv2 or v3.
  - ⚠ Check: Manual groups v2 and v3; SNMPv3 is the encrypted version. ➕
- Internet Control Message Protocol (ICMP): error-reporting protocol in TCP/IP.
  - Definition: Devices send error messages, such as host unreachable.
  - Use: Quickly locate source and cause of network errors.
  - Does not exchange data between systems, unlike SNMP.
- Exam traps
  - Capture and record packets: protocol analyzer, not online monitor or reports. ⭐
  - Reports summarize history; only the analyzer inspects live packet content.
  - SNMP manages devices; ICMP reports errors; do not swap them.

## 4.7.6 Problem management reporting reviews

- Best indicator of incident program effectiveness: incident resolution mean time. 📓⭐
  - Definition: Average time to resolve incidents.
  - Use: Measures process efficiency directly.
  - Distractors: incident count, time between incidents, and alert time do not measure resolution.
- Areas the auditor reviews:
  - Interviews with IS operations personnel.
  - Performance records.
  - Outstanding error log entries.
  - Help desk call logs.
  - Operations documentation and IT department procedures.
- Questions the auditor asks:
  - Are procedures documented for logging, analyzing, resolving and escalating problems?
  - Are reasons for processing delays valid?
  - Are recurring problems identified and prevented?
  - Are recurring problems hidden from IS management?
  - Are statistics on online processing performance adequate?
  - ⚠ Check: Last two outline items paraphrase the manual's wording; confirm against figure 4.16.
- Exam traps
  - Efficiency metric means time-to-resolve, not frequency of incidents.
  - Auditor reviews records and logs first, not just management assurances.

## Takeaway

- Problem management removes causes; incident management restores service fast.
- Known errors with workarounds sit in the KEDB.
- Prioritize incidents by impact and urgency, escalate per management criteria.
- Anyone may add to the error log; only authorized, different person closes.
- Protocol analyzer captures packets; resolution mean time best measures incident effectiveness.
