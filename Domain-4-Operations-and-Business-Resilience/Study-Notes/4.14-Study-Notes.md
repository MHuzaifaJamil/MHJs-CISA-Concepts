## 4.14 Overview: Laws, Regulations and Planning

- Definition: Data backup is a spare copy of data kept for recovery.
- Use: It lets the business restore data after loss, corruption, or disaster.
- Audit impact: Missing or untested backups mean recovery may fail entirely.
- Definition: Restoration is copying backed-up data back into working systems.
- Use: It returns systems to a usable state within RTO and RPO.
- Audit impact: Auditors test restoration, because a backup never restored is unproven.
- Laws and regulations affect how data is handled, stored, and retained.
- Audit impact: Verify retention periods and storage locations match applicable legal requirements.
- Example: A regulator requires seven-year record retention, so short rotation cycles would breach it. ➕
- ⚠ Check: The seven-year figure is illustrative only; actual periods vary by law.

## 4.14.1 Data Storage Resiliency and DR Methods

### RAID

- Definition: RAID (redundant array of independent disks) combines several disks into logical units.
- Use: Gives redundancy, better performance, or both.
- Audit impact: RAID protects against disk failure, not deletion, corruption, or site loss.
- Storage arrays hide the complexity of building logical volumes from disks.
- Audit impact: Staff must still understand RAID level trade-offs despite array automation.
- Exam traps: RAID alone is not a backup; it does not replace offsite copies.

### Array Replication Modes

- Definition: Replication copies data from one storage system to another site.
- Use: Protects against site failure and supports application recovery.
- Definition: Synchronous replication confirms a local write only after the remote write completes.
- Use: Near-zero data loss, but needs strong bandwidth and low latency.
- Audit impact: Synchronous mode suits very low RPO; distance and latency limit it.
- Definition: Asynchronous replication copies data on a schedule.
- Use: Tolerates slower links and longer distances.
- Audit impact: Asynchronous mode risks losing data written since the last replication.
- Definition: Adaptive replication switches between modes depending on network load.
- Audit impact: Effective RPO varies, so confirm which mode is active during peaks.
- Exam traps: Lowest RPO points to synchronous; cost-sensitive long distance points to asynchronous.

### Array-Based and Host-Based Replication

- Definition: Array-based (hardware) replication is done by the storage array itself.
- Use: It is transparent to the application and operating system.
- Audit impact: No application changes needed, so coverage depends on array configuration.
- Definition: Host-based replication is done by software on the server.
- Use: Serves servers without disk arrays, replicating local volumes remotely.
- Audit impact: Consumes server resources; verify agents are installed and running.

### Remote Storage and E-Vaulting

- Definition: Remote storage keeps physical backup media at an off-site location. 📓
- Use: Media survives a disaster that destroys the primary site.
- Audit impact: Check distance, access control, and transport security.
- Definition: E-vaulting electronically transmits backup data to a remote site. 📓
- Use: Removes physical transport and speeds up off-site protection.
- Audit impact: Verify transmission encryption and that remote data is restorable.
- Example: Nightly backups sent over an encrypted link to a vault provider.

## 4.14.2 Backup and Restoration

### Planning and Secondary Storage

- Plan backups from the BIA, RPO, and RTO.
- Aim to minimize data loss within budget and business objectives.
- Definition: RPO is the maximum tolerable data loss, measured in time.
- Audit impact: Backup frequency must be at least as often as the RPO.
- Definition: RTO is the maximum tolerable time to restore service.
- Audit impact: Restore speed and method must fit within the RTO.
- Secondary storage includes mirrored disks (local or remote) and network storage.
- Removable media are recorded at one site and stored in offsite libraries.
- Number and location of libraries depend on use availability and interruption risk.
- Catalog maintenance is manual (offsite librarian) or automatic (vaulting solutions).
- Integrated backup solutions are gradually replacing manual media inventories.
- Audit impact: Manual catalogs become error-prone as data volumes grow.

### Immutable Backup

- Definition: Immutable backup cannot be altered, deleted, or encrypted, even by administrators.
- Use: Protects backups from ransomware and malicious insiders.
- Audit impact: Confirm immutability settings and retention locks are actually enabled.
- Cloud providers can offer backup storage that is made immutable.
- Exam traps: Encryption protects confidentiality; immutability protects integrity and availability of backups.

### Offsite Library Controls

- Limit physical access to authorized personnel only.
- Encrypt backup media, especially in transit.
- Build to withstand fire, heat, and water.
- Locate away from the data center, outside the same disaster zone.
- Keep an inventory of media and files with retention times.
- Record all media movements in and out of the library.
- Keep a catalog of file versions and locations, protected from disclosure.
- Apply retention periods per enterprise policy.
- Audit impact: Sample the inventory against physical media to test accuracy.

### Cloud Backup

- Definition: Cloud backup is an offsite library in a third-party provider data center.
- Use: Scalable offsite copy without owning storage infrastructure.
- Cloud still has a physical location where backup servers hold the data.
- Disconnected infrastructure resists ransomware that targets connected backups.
- Encrypt data in transit and in the cloud; limit keys to need-to-know.
- Verify backup and restore times meet RTO and RPO.
- Real restores may need large network transfers; small samples understate time.
- Audit impact: Test a full-scale restore, not only a sample file.
- Exam traps: Cloud backup does not remove the customer's responsibility for restore testing.

### Security and Control of Offsite Facilities

- Offsite facility must be as secure as the originating site.
- Use locked doors, no windows, and surveillance.
- Facility should not be easily identifiable from outside.
- Monitor humidity, temperature, and air quality.
- Provide UPS, raised floors, smoke and water detectors, and tested fire suppression.
- Paper records must not create a fire hazard.

### Media and Documentation Backup

- Duplicate data and documentation offsite as a recovery prerequisite.
- Transport physical media and mirrored backups securely.
- Audit impact: Software without its documentation may be unrecoverable by a recovery team.

### Types of Backup Devices and Media

- Selection factors: standardization, capacity, speed, and price.
- Removable media: CDs, DVDs, removable hard drives, and solid-state drives.
- Tape formats: DDS, DAT, DLT, AIT, and LTO.
- Disk-based options: VTLs, disk snapshots, host-based or array-based replication.
- Definition: A VTL is disk storage that emulates a tape library.
- Use: Fast backup and restore with tape-compatible software; can replicate offsite.
- Definition: A snapshot is a momentary copy of a volume or file system.
- Use: Full copy or changed blocks only; can mount on another server.
- Audit impact: Snapshots on the same array do not protect against array failure.
- Disk-based backup suits virtualized servers, since whole VMs can be replicated.
- Offsite copies need the same security as the originals.

### Periodic Backup Procedures

- Backup per RPO; data folders need more frequent backup than configuration or software files.
- Online systems use nightly or hourly backups, or replication.
- Automated backup and media management reduces operator error.
- Agents on servers collect data and ship it to backup servers.
- Disk-array replication is hidden from servers and runs over a SAN or LAN.
- Audit impact: Review job logs for failed or skipped backups.

### Frequency of Rotation

- Keep a point-in-time copy plus changes since.
- Frequency and retention generations are set per application.
- Anticipate failure at any processing step.
- Legacy batch systems keep master files at intervals, with matching transaction files.
- A DBMS needs a specialized backup agent for its make and version.
- Arrange vendor software licenses for the alternate site beforehand.
- Custom software needs object and source libraries and current patches at all locations.
- Keep compatible backup hardware offsite, with support agreements for long-term retention.
- Preserve and update documentation offsite.
- Adequate backup is a prerequisite to recovery.

### Types of Media and Documentation Rotated

- Software: operating systems, languages, compilers, utilities, and applications.
- Paper: operational guides, user manuals, records, data files, and databases.
- Offsite classification: operating procedures such as run books and job stream control.
- System and program documentation: flow charts, source listings, logic, and user manuals.
- Special procedures: exception, variation, and emergency processing.
- Input and output source documents: copies, microfiche, and microfilm.
- A copy of the business continuity plan is also stored offsite.
- Store sensitive data in fire-resistant magnetic media containers.
- Policy defines what is stored and for how long; software manages rotation.
- Missing backup policy is a major risk. 📓
- ⚠ Check: A notes question has two answers: no policy ("HDA") versus frequency.
- Principle: Without a policy, no frequency, scope, or testing requirement exists to enforce.
- ⚠ Check: That answer is unresolved; confirm against the QAE source.
- Exam traps: Prefer the root cause (no policy) over symptoms like frequency.

## 4.14.3 Backup Schemes

- Three main schemes: full, incremental, and differential, often combined.

### Full Backup

- Definition: Full backup copies all files and folders to media. 📓
- Use: Baseline for incremental and differential schemes. 📓
- Output is one backup set, a single repository for restore.
- Cost: needs more time and media capacity than other schemes.
- Audit impact: Restore is simplest and least error-prone.

### Incremental Backup

- Definition: Copies only changes since the last full or incremental backup. 📓⭐
- Use: Main goal is faster backups using fewer resources. 📓
- Restore needs the full backup plus all incremental sets, in sequence. 📓
- Restore is slower and more complex than full or differential.
- Less media is needed, a real but secondary benefit. 📓
- Data integrity is required for all backup types, so it is not distinctive. 📓
- Audit impact: One missing or corrupt incremental set breaks the restore chain.
- Exam traps: Primary objective is speed and low resource use, not simpler restoration.
- Exam traps: Integrity applies to every scheme, so it is never the distinguishing goal.
- Exam traps: Storage saving is true, but secondary to the speed objective.
- ⚠ Check: The user's notes contain an incremental-backup question with two disagreeing answers.
- ⚠ Check: Notes' concept block supports "faster backups, fewer resources"; the storage-space option conflicts.
- ⚠ Check: Notes show no answer key; confirm the marked one.
- Resolution: Manual says incremental is faster and needs less media, yet restore takes longer.
- Resolution: "Primary" objective favors speed and resource reduction over storage saving.
- Resolution: Storage saving is a consequence of copying less data.

### Differential Backup

- Definition: Copies all changes since the last full backup, so it is cumulative.
- Use: Faster and smaller than full; restore needs last full plus last differential.
- Restore is faster than incremental.
- Backup is slower and uses more media than incremental, because data accumulates.
- Audit impact: Fewer sets in the chain lowers restore risk compared to incremental.

### Worked Restore-Sequence Example

- Assumptions: Full backup Sunday, then one backup each night Monday to Saturday.
- Assumptions: Full is 100 GB; 10 GB of different files changes daily.
- Assumptions: Data is lost on Thursday morning; restore to Wednesday night's state.
- Full-only scheme: restore needs 1 set, the latest full backup.
- Incremental scheme: restore needs Sunday full plus Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday incrementals.
- Incremental count: 1 full + 3 incrementals = 4 sets, applied in date order.
- Differential scheme: restore needs Sunday full plus Wednesday differential only.
- Differential count: 1 full + 1 differential = 2 sets.
- Wednesday differential holds three days: 10 + 10 + 10 = 30 GB.
- Restored: incremental 100+10+10+10 = 130 GB; differential 100+30 = 130 GB.
- Loss after Saturday: incremental needs 1+6 = 7 sets; differential needs 2.
- Weekly media, Monday to Saturday: incremental 6 x 10 = 60 GB.
- Differential sizes 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 GB total 210 GB.
- Formula: differential media = daily change x (1+2+...+n) = 10 x 21.
- Verification: Both schemes restore 130 GB by Wednesday; set counts 4 versus 2 recount correctly.
- Verification: Differential total 10 x (6 x 7 / 2) = 210 GB matches the 10+20+30+40+50+60 sum.
- Verification: Inverse check: 210 GB versus 60 GB gives 3.5 times more media for differential.
- Trade-off: Incremental favors backup speed and media; differential favors restore speed and fewer sets.
- Exam traps: Differential is not "since last backup"; it is "since last full backup."
- Exam traps: Incremental is "since last backup of any kind," not since full.
- Exam traps: Restore needs full plus all incrementals, or full plus latest differential.
- ⚠ Check: Figures are illustrative assumptions; real change rates overlap and shrink differential growth.

### Method of Rotation: Grandfather-Father-Son

- Definition: Rotation scheme that cycles media in daily, weekly, and monthly tiers.
- Use: Keeps multiple generations while reusing media economically.
- Daily backups (son) rotate through the week.
- Last backup of the week becomes the weekly (father).
- Last weekly of the month becomes the monthly (grandfather).
- Last monthly of the year becomes the yearly backup.
- Monthly and annual media are retained, not rotated.
- Offsite media are not returned until replacements are safely offsite.
- Variations exist for quarterly backups and extra redundancy.
- Audit impact: Verify rotation follows policy and retention generations are actually kept.

### Record Keeping for Offsite Storage

- Media records: data set name, volume serial, creation date, period, bin number.
- Critical documentation records: name, location, system, and date of last update.
- Automated media management records this information.
- Keep receipt and shipment logs for media moved between facilities.
- Audit impact: Reconcile logs to physical media to detect lost or unauthorized media.

### 3-2-1 Backup Strategy

- Definition: Keep one primary backup and two copies of the data.
- Use: Use two different media types.
- Keep at least one copy offsite.
- Use: Reduces impact of single points of failure like media errors or theft.
- Audit impact: The offsite element may be unavailable from some cloud providers. ➕
- ⚠ Check: Manual says primary plus two copies; common usage says three total.
- Example: Production disk, local VTL copy, and encrypted cloud copy.

## Takeaway

- Incremental favors fast backup; restore needs full plus every incremental set.
- Differential restore needs only the last full plus the latest differential.
- Backup policy, RPO, and RTO drive frequency, media, and replication choices.
- Immutable, offsite, encrypted backups protect against ransomware and site disasters.
- Test restores at realistic scale; untested backups are unproven.
- Rotation records and offsite controls must be reconciled to physical media.
