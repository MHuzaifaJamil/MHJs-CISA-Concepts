## 4.15 Business Continuity Plan (overview)

### Basics
- Definition: BCP is a plan that keeps the business running during any disturbance. 📓
- Use: Enables the organization to continue operations when disruption arises.
- Audit impact: Planning needs rigorous effort and committed resources; auditor checks both.
- Definition: DRP is the plan to recover an inoperable facility and relocate operations. 📓
- Use: Restores IT systems and facilities after a disaster.
- Audit impact: Auditor confirms DRP exists and is consistent with the BCP.
- Definition: Restoration plan returns operations to normal in a restored or new facility.
- Use: Closes the recovery cycle after temporary operations.
- Audit impact: Often forgotten; verify the plan covers the return to normal.
- Definition: Strategically important processes are key processes driving growth and business goals.
- Use: They are the first thing identified when building or updating a BCP.
- Audit impact: If critical processes are wrong, the whole plan is misdirected. ⭐

### Executive policy and ownership
- Definition: Formal executive policy states the overall recovery target.
- Use: It empowers people who develop, test and maintain the plans.
- Audit impact: No policy means no authority; this is a top-level gap.
- Senior managers are primarily responsible for the BCP. ⭐
- Reason: They safeguard assets and the organization's viability.
- Audit impact: Delegating ownership wholly to IT is a finding.

### Risk assessment for the BCP
- Definition: Risk assessment on key processes judges threats against those processes.
- Use: Risk is proportional to impact and probability of the threat.
- Audit impact: Plans not tied to risk assessment waste resources.
- Assessment outputs, in order:
  - Identify resources supporting key processes: people, data, infrastructure, third parties.
  - Identify vulnerabilities and threats.
  - Estimate threat probabilities.
  - Assess efficiency and effectiveness of existing countermeasures.
- Example: A payment process depends on one data center and one telecom carrier.

### Plan scope and structure
- BCP gives reduced but sufficient functionality while recovery occurs. ⭐
- Exam trap: BCP does not promise full normal service immediately.
- BCP considers critical operations needed for survival.
- BCP also considers human and material resources supporting those operations.
- BCP includes continuity procedures, the DRP and the restoration plan.
- Multiple plans are allowed, but they must be consistent.
- A single integrated plan is preferred.
  - Reason: Better coordination among plan components.
  - Reason: Committed resources are used most effectively.
- Solutions differ by scenario and by contractual requirements.
- Example: Online transaction processing needs a different solution than back-office processing.
- Exam traps:
  - Integrated plan is desirable, not mandatory; consistency is the mandatory part.
  - Same process at another location may still need a different solution.

## 4.15.1 IT business continuity planning

### Alignment and dependencies
- Definition: IT BCP applies enterprise BCP approach when IT processing is threatened.
- Use: Most key business processes depend on systems, infrastructure and data.
- Audit impact: IT plan must align with corporate strategy and corporate BCP.
- Application value depends on its role in supporting organizational strategy.
- Component value is derived from the application using it.
- Example: A network link is critical only because a critical application uses it.
- Alternate facilities must be ready and compatible with the original facility.
- Alternate facility plans for their use must be current.
- If the IT plan is separate, it must support the corporate BCP.

### Reducing likelihood of disruption
- Data center location should avoid a flood plain.
- Data center location should avoid earthquake fault lines.
- Data center should not be near explosives or toxic materials.
- Definition: Loop or mesh topology gives multiple network paths between nodes.
- Use: Alternative processing facilities are built into the network.
- Audit impact: Single points of failure in topology are findings.
- Countermeasures include:
  - Duplicate processing facility
  - Redundant data networks
  - Resilient hardware
  - Backup and recovery systems
  - Data replication

### Risk assessment output and BIA
- Risk assessment output is a dependency map.
- Map links processes, applications, IS and IT components, with threats and vulnerabilities.
- Applications are grouped by criticality.
- Risk can be estimated qualitatively with rating values for impact and probability.
- Risk can be calculated quantitatively with monetary loss and probability.
- Exam trap: Candidates are not tested on calculating risk, only on understanding it.
- Definition: BIA measures business losses from a disruption.
- Use: Gives maximum downtime and tolerable data loss per application.
- Use: Quantifies losses as they grow, guiding technology and facility choice.
- Audit impact: Recovery choices without a BIA lack justification.
- Risk assessment and BIA results feed the IS business continuity strategy.
- Strategy states main technology, principles and an implementation roadmap.
- As infrastructure changes, countermeasures change, so the plan must be retested.
- Exam trap: A plan is never finished; stale plans are a finding. ⭐

### BCP versus IT DRP
- BCP covers business actions during disaster.
- Example: Where staff report, how orders are taken, which vendors to call.
- IT DRP is a subcomponent restoring systems, communications, applications and data.
- DRP may sit inside the BCP or be a separate document.
- Exam trap: DRP alone is not a BCP; people and processes also matter. ⭐

### Cost and recovery grouping
- Not every system needs a recovery strategy.
- Cost of a recovery option should never exceed its benefit. ⭐
- BIA also groups systems by recovery time.
- Recovery time groups guide the choice of technological controls.
- Exam trap: Choosing the fastest recovery for everything wastes money.

### Security during disaster
- Physical and IS controls may fail during a disaster.
- Implement an ISMS to protect integrity, confidentiality and availability in all conditions.
- ISMS means information security management system.

### Third-party and cloud
- Cloud provider disruptions may not be recoverable by the customer.
- Customer may have to wait for the vendor's own BCP.
- Audit impact: Review contracts, vendor BCP evidence and exit options.

## 4.15.2 Disasters and other disruptive events

### Nature and causes
- Definition: Disaster makes critical information resources inoperative for some time.
- Duration ranges from minutes to several months.
- Disasters require recovery efforts to restore operational status.
- Natural causes: earthquakes, floods, tornados, severe storms, fire.
- Utility loss: electricity, telecommunications, natural gas, other delivery services.
- Human causes: terrorist attacks, hacker attacks, human error.
- Other causes of disruption:
  - System malfunctions
  - Accidental file deletions
  - Untested application releases
  - Loss of backup
  - Denial-of-service attacks, intrusions and viruses
- Recovery may require restoring hardware, software or data files.

### Early warning and fallback
- Many disruptions start as minor incidents.
- The help desk acts as an early warning system.
- Definition: Creeping disaster is a slow degradation that often goes undetected.
- Example: Database performance slowly degrades until the database halts.
- Audit impact: Look for help desk trend analysis to catch these.
- Strategy includes worst-case scenarios with short and long-term fallbacks.
- Short term: use an alternate processing facility.
- Long term: identify and equip a new permanent facility.

### Pandemic planning
- Definition: Pandemic is a widely spreading infectious disease outbreak, possibly worldwide.
- Differs from traditional BCP: scale and duration are hard to determine.
- Audit impact: Auditor should evaluate preparedness for pandemics or outbreaks.

### Damage to image, reputation or brand
- Rumors may or may not relate to a serious incident.
- Loss of trust is among the worst consequences of a crisis.
- Public relations activities help contain damage.
- Banks, healthcare, airlines and similar high-impact industries need elaborate protocols.
- Appoint and prepare a trained spokesperson beforehand.
- Best choice: senior legal counsel or a public relations officer. ⭐
- Only the spokesperson makes public statements, regardless of rank.
- Keep a pre-drafted generic announcement with blanks for circumstances.
- Do not deviate from it because of time pressure.
- Announcement states an investigation started; it does not state causes.
- Do not assume liability or blame the system or process.
- Senior executives with shared responsibilities should not travel together.
- Reason: Organization keeps a senior manager after a common disaster.
- Exam traps:
  - CEO speaking publicly is wrong; rank does not grant authority to speak.
  - Naming the root cause immediately creates liability risk.

### Unanticipated events
- Definition: Black swan is a surprise, major-effect event, rationalized after the fact.
- Black swan events cannot be expected or planned for.
- Management should still think about contingency planning for critical processes.
- Criticality of process, industry or activity drives that effort.

## 4.15.4 Business continuity policy

### Definition and purpose
- Definition: Policy is a top-management-approved document defining BCP extent and scope.
- Use: Defines whether BC is a project or ongoing program.
- Policy can have public and internal sections.
- Purposes of the policy:
  - Tell internal stakeholders the company commits resources and expects all areas to follow.
  - Reassure external stakeholders such as shareholders and regulators.
  - Empower those responsible for business continuity.
  - State principles: risk assessment, BIA, redundancy targets.
- Policy is later reflected in the IT business continuity strategy.
- Audit impact: Verify top-management approval and defined scope.

### Proactive stance and control types
- Policy should be proactive: detect, prevent, and mitigate consequences.
- BCP or IT DRP is the organization's most critical corrective control. ⭐
- It depends on incident management and backup and recovery.
- Controls that reduce likelihood (figure 4.37):
  - Infrastructure monitoring
  - Capacity management
  - Incident management (help desk)
  - Risk management
- Controls that mitigate consequences:
  - Backup and recovery
  - BCP or IT DRP
  - Special clauses in vendor or supplier contracts
  - Spare processing site
  - UPS or power generator
- Exam trap: Backups and BCP are corrective, not preventive; monitoring is preventive.
- Incident management group needs staffing, support and crisis training.
- BCP should be well designed, documented, drill tested, funded and audited.

## 4.15.5 BCP incident management

### Principles
- Definition: Incident is any unexpected event, even one causing no damage.
- Incidents are dynamic, so management must be dynamic, proactive and documented.
- Classification can change while the incident is resolved.

### Classification
- Negligible: no perceptible or significant damage.
  - Example: Brief OS crash with full recovery, or momentary outage covered by UPS.
- Minor: no material or financial impact of relative importance; localized.
  - Example: Internet outage in a single office of a large firm.
- Major: material negative impact on processes, other systems, departments or clients.
  - Example: Ransomware disabling servers and workstations.
- Crisis: major incident seriously disrupting operations and possibly third parties.
- Crisis severity is generally proportional to time from start to resolution.
- Document and revisit minor, major and crisis incidents until resolved.
- A major incident can shrink briefly, then expand to a crisis.
- Negligible incidents are analyzed statistically for systemic or avoidable causes.
- Exam trap: Negligible incidents are not ignored; trend analysis finds root causes.

### Severity criteria
- Severity is driven largely by estimated downtime versus SLA.
- SLAs may state maximum downtime and recovery estimates.
- Service covers commitments to external customers and internal departments.
- Other criteria: impact on data or platforms, degree of functional impact.
- Figure 4.38 gives example data criterion: database loss of integrity.
- Platform examples: viruses, worms, hardware failure. ➕
- Suggested actions by level (figure garbled in scan):
  - Crisis: follow the business continuity plan. ➕
  - Major: prepare for the business continuity plan. ➕
  - Minor: correct, clean, restore or replace. ➕
- ⚠ Check: Figure 4.38 is unreadable in the manual text; actions need verification.

### Fail-safe and escalation
- Fail-safe approach: give any non-negligible incident provisional level 3.
- Level 3 is the most severe level in the example figure.
- Reevaluate level regularly as the incident evolves.
- Incident response (firecall) team performs the reevaluation.
- Security officer is notified of relevant incidents when triggered.
- Escalation protocol is pre-established, often in the IT DRP.
- Escalation steps include:
  - Calling the spokesperson
  - Alerting top management
  - Involving regulators
- Exam trap: Over-classifying first and downgrading later is safer than the reverse.

## 4.15.6 Development of business continuity plans

### Inputs
- Inputs: BIA, criticality analysis, and management-selected recovery strategy.
- Plan covers all issues in interrupting processes, including disaster recovery.

### Factors in developing or reviewing the plan
- Predisaster readiness, including incident response management.
- Evacuation procedures.
- Procedures for declaring a disaster, with rating and escalation.
- Circumstances for declaring a disaster.
- Not all interruptions are disasters, but small incidents can grow.
- Example: An uncontained virus brings down the whole IT facility.
- Clear responsibilities, and named persons responsible for each function.
- Contact information.
- Step-by-step recovery processes.
- Resources required for recovery and continued operation.

### Writing and storage
- Write plans in simple language everyone understands.
- Structure plans by team so each finds its tasks easily.
- Keep copies offsite.
- Audit impact: Complex or unavailable plans fail under stress.

## 4.15.7 Other issues in plan development

### Involvement
- Management and user involvement is vital to BCP success.
- Users identify critical systems, critical recovery times and needed resources.
- Three personnel divisions must be involved:
  - Support services: detect first signs of an incident.
  - Business operations: may suffer from the incident.
  - Information processing support: run the recovery.
- Exam trap: IT alone cannot define critical systems; business users must.

### Scope
- Consider the entire organization, not only IS processing.
- If no uniform BCP exists, extend IS plan to all dependent units.
- Plan should include a staff list for short, medium and long term.
- Staff list needs redundant contact information, with backups per contact.
- Include building facilities: desks, chairs, telephones.
- Include resources to resume operations, not only IT resources.

## 4.15.8 Components of a business continuity plan

### Plan documents
- A BCP should include:
  - Continuity of operations plan
  - DRP
  - Business resumption plan
- A BCP may also include:
  - Continuity of support plan or IT contingency plan
  - Crisis communications plan
  - Incident response plan
  - Transportation plan
  - Occupant emergency plan
  - Evacuation and emergency relocation plan

### NIST SP 800-34 plan types
- BCP: sustains business operations while recovering from significant disruption.
- COOP: sustains mission essential functions at an alternate site for up to 30 days.
- Crisis communications plan: internal and external communication; controls rumors.
- CIP plan: protects critical infrastructure components; supports COOP.
- Cyber incident response plan: mitigates cyberattacks by isolating systems and cleaning up.
- DRP: activated after major system disruption with long-term effects.
- ISCP: recovers a single information system.
- OEP: minimizes loss of life, injury and property damage.
- OEP is initiated immediately after an event, before COOP or DRP.
- Exam traps:
  - Life safety comes first; OEP precedes recovery plans.
  - ISCP is single-system; DRP coordinates several ISCPs.

### Items to agree before planning
- Governing policies
- Goals and products for each phase
- Alternate facilities
- Critical information resources
- Persons responsible
- Available resources, including humans
- Prioritized scheduling

### Plan copies
- Keep copies at the recovery facility.
- Keep copies at the media storage facility.
- Keep copies at homes of key decision-making personnel.
- An electronic version often sits on a mirrored website.

### Call tree
- Definition: Call tree is a prioritized notification directory of key personnel.
- Use: Starts and carries out recovery efforts; often automated.
- Audit impact: Auditor calls a sample of contacts to test accuracy. ⭐
- Many team leaders may be unavailable in a widespread disaster.
- Call tree should be highly redundant, on hard copy, possibly intranet, and updated.
- Directory contents:
  - Prioritized contacts, who gets called first
  - Primary and emergency numbers and addresses
  - Equipment and software vendors
  - Suppliers of equipment, supplies or services
  - Recovery facility contacts, including hot site and network rerouting
  - Offsite media storage contacts and authorized retrievers
  - Insurance agents
  - Contract personnel services
  - Legal, regulatory and government agencies
  - Procedure to count how many people were reached

### Backup of required supplies
- Keep detailed hard copy procedures for staff unfamiliar with operations.
- Store special forms offsite, such as check stock, invoices and order forms.
- Provide data entry hardware and software at the hot site.
- Provide cryptographic equipment and keys, such as RSA tokens and USB keys.

### Insurance
- ⚠ Check: Manual says insurance specifics are not tested; know required content only.
- IT equipment and facilities coverage: often replaces with "like kind and quality".
- Media reconstruction: covers reproducing damaged media, including programming and backup costs.
- Extra expense: covers extra costs of continuing operations after damage.
- Example: Processing on sixth floor; fire destroys lower floors, interrupting operations.
- Business interruption: covers lost profit from IT malfunction.
- Valuable papers and records: covers actual cash value of papers not defined as media.
- Errors and omissions: legal liability protection for professional mistakes causing client loss.
- Fidelity coverage: covers dishonest or fraudulent employee acts; common in financial institutions.
- Media transportation: covers loss or damage of media in transit.
- If policy does not require copying, obtain a carrier letter on coverage.
- Insurance covers financial loss at historical performance, not current performance.
- Auditor ensures valuation of equipment, infrastructure and data is current.
- Insurance does not compensate reputation, image or goodwill. ⭐
- Exam trap: Insurance supplements recovery; it never replaces continuity planning.

## 4.15.9 Plan testing

### Principles
- Most tests fall short of full-scale; still perform full or partial tests.
- Tests show how well the plan works and what needs improvement.
- Schedule tests to minimize disruption; weekends are generally good.
- Key recovery team members must be involved with enough time.
- Simulate actual prime-time processing conditions, even off hours.

### Test objectives
- Verify BCP completeness and precision.
- Evaluate personnel performance.
- Appraise training and awareness of all employees, not only the team.
- Evaluate coordination with external vendors and suppliers.
- Measure backup site ability and capacity.
- Assess vital records retrieval capability.
- Evaluate state and quantity of relocated equipment and supplies.
- Measure overall operational and IT processing performance.

### Test types
- Desk-based evaluation (tabletop or paper test): walk-through by major plan players. ⭐
- Use: Players decide what might happen in a given disruption.
- It usually precedes the preparedness test.
- Preparedness test: localized version of a full test, using actual resources.
- Use: Cost-effective, incremental evidence and improvement; performed regularly on plan aspects.
- Full operational test: one step away from real disruption. ⭐
- Test the plan on paper and locally before shutting down operations.
- Order of progression: paper test, preparedness test, full operational test.
- Exam traps:
  - Start with the tabletop; a full test first risks real outage.
  - Paper tests give least assurance of actual recoverability.
- Assessing test results and value is an important auditor responsibility.

### Test execution phases
- Pretest: set the stage, such as placing tables and installing backup phones.
- Pretest actions would not exist in a real, unannounced emergency.
- Test: real operational activities are executed and observers evaluate staff.
- Activities include data entry, calls, processing, orders, and moving people and supplies.
- Posttest: cleanup after the test.
  - Return resources to proper places.
  - Disconnect equipment and return personnel.
  - Delete all company data from third-party systems.
  - Formally evaluate the plan and implement improvements.
- Audit impact: Confirm company data was removed from vendor systems after tests.

### Documentation and results analysis
- Each team keeps a diary form recording observations, problems and resolutions.
- Documentation helps in a real disaster.
- Insurers and authorities may request it.
- It supports analysis of plan strengths and weaknesses.
- Results must be gauged quantitatively, not by observation alone.
- Time: elapsed time for tasks, equipment delivery, personnel assembly, site arrival.
- Amount: work performed at backup site by clerical and IS staff.
- Count: vital records, supplies and equipment received, versus required; systems recovered.
- Accuracy: data entry and processing accuracy versus normal conditions.
- Example: 45 of 50 required vital records arrived, so count result is 90%.
- Verification: 45 divided by 50 equals 0.90; 0.90 times 50 equals 45.
- Testing focus: gaps, assumptions, timelines, strategies, personnel and plan accuracy.
- Test at least annually, and after revisions or key personnel or technology changes.
- ⚠ Check: Manual says tests at least once yearly; "annual full test" is unsupported.
- Information security must be validated during the test.

### Plan maintenance
- Triggers for review and update:
  - Process or department changes
  - New resources or applications
  - Changes in business strategy
  - Software or hardware environment changes
  - New events or changed event likelihood
  - Personnel or contact detail changes
- Include BCP in the SDLC. ⭐
- BCP coordinator is responsible for maintenance. ⭐
- Coordinator duties:
  - Schedule periodic reviews and tell staff their roles and deadlines.
  - Call for unscheduled revisions after significant changes.
  - Update the plan within a set number of days of review.
  - Coordinate scheduled and unscheduled tests; at least one scheduled test yearly.
  - Integrate test fixes within a set number of days.
  - Schedule training within 30 days of each revision and test.
  - Keep records of maintenance, testing, training and reviews.
  - Update notification directory at least quarterly; shorter is recommended.
- Manual's "30 days" for plan updates is given as an example ("e.g.").
- Software tools can track maintenance tasks.
- Exam trap: Changes trigger retesting; waiting for the annual cycle is wrong.

## 4.15.10 BCM good practices

### Sources of good practice
- Business Continuity Institute (BCI): good practices for BC management.
- Disaster Recovery Institute International (DRII): professional practices for BC professionals.
- US FEMA: guidance for business and industry on emergency management.
- ISACA COBIT: guidance on relevant IT controls.
- US NIST: measurement science, standards and technology.
- US FFIEC: interagency body overseeing US financial institutions.
- US HHS: describes HIPAA requirements for managing health information.
- ISO 22301:2019: requirements for a business continuity management system (BCMS).
- Use: Organizations adopt these to improve capability and meet regulation.
- Audit impact: Auditor benchmarks the BCP against applicable standards and regulations.

### Development process
- Plan should be based on the long-range IT plan.
- Plan should align with overall business continuity strategy.
- Steps, in order:
  - 1. Conduct a risk assessment.
  - 2. Identify and prioritize critical systems and resources.
  - 3. Identify and prioritize threats and vulnerabilities.
  - 4. Prepare BIAs for loss of critical processes.
  - 5. Choose controls to recover IT components.
  - 6. Develop detailed DRP for IS facilities.
  - 7. Develop detailed BCP for critical business functions.
  - 8. Test the plans.
  - 9. Maintain the plans.
- Exam trap: Risk assessment and BIA come before writing any plan.

## 4.15.11 Auditing business continuity

### Auditor tasks
- Evaluate strategy and its link to business objectives.
- Review BIA findings for current priorities and controls.
- Evaluate BCP adequacy against standards, regulations, RTO, RPO and BIA metrics.
- Verify effectiveness by reviewing prior IT and end-user test results.
- Evaluate cloud-based mechanisms.
- Evaluate offsite storage adequacy.
- Verify secure transport of backup media.
- Evaluate personnel emergency response: procedures, training and drills.
- Ensure a plan maintenance process exists and is effective.
- Check manuals are simple and stakeholders understand their roles.
- Third-party audit rights may be limited by contracts.
- Audit impact: Check contract audit clauses before relying on vendor assurance.

### Reviewing the document
- Obtain policy, strategy, BCP and latest BIA with RTO and RPO.
- Sample distributed manuals for currency.
- Confirm plan supports strategy and invocation procedures are effective.
- Confirm update procedure and maintenance responsibility are documented.

### Reviewing applications covered
- Critical applications, including PC applications, are identified and prioritized.
- Review tolerance level for all applications.
- Secondary site has correct and compatible system software versions.

### Reviewing business continuity teams
- Review member lists and backup facility agreements.
- Call a sample of contacts; verify numbers and manual copies.
- Interview members about assigned responsibilities.

### Plan testing review
- Evaluate test documentation procedures.
- Review backup procedures for each DRP area.
- Emergency procedures are complete, current and understandable.
- Recovery transactions are identifiable separately from normal transactions.
- Teams have written procedures; user recovery procedures are documented.
- Plan covers movement to and from the recovery site.
- Items to rebuild the IPF are stored offsite.
- Examples: blueprints, hardware inventory, wiring diagrams.

### Questions to consider
- Who administers and updates the plan, and where is it stored?
- Which systems and equipment are not covered, and why?
- What assumptions are stated?
- Where are rendezvous points for the emergency management team?
- Are disasters of varying degrees and telecommunication backups addressed?
- Is there a backup facility, and relocation to a new IPF?
- How are master file and media management data merged into predisaster files?
- How is manually processed data loaded into the automated system?
- Are backup procedures, responsibilities, methods, frequency and media defined?
- Is personnel trained on backup equipment and procedures?
- Are offsite storage and recovery documentation adequate?
- Is there a testing and training schedule, and SLA disaster requirements?

### Evaluating prior test results
- BCP coordinator keeps test history; corrections are incorporated.
- Review for problem trends and their resolutions.
- Audit impact: Repeated unresolved problems show a weak maintenance process.

### Evaluating offsite storage
- Media and documentation must be present, synchronized and current.
- Coverage includes data files, application and system software, documentation.
- Coverage also includes supplies, special forms and a BCP copy.
- Detailed inventory test checks dataset names, volume serials, accounting periods, bin locations.
- Compare documentation currency with production.
- Review transport method for ISMS weaknesses.
- Offsite facility security checks:
  - Limit access to authorized users.
  - Raised flooring, humidity and temperature controls.
  - Specialized circuitry and UPS.
  - Water detection, smoke detectors, fire extinguishing.
  - Current inspection and calibration tags.
- Exam trap: Auditor verifies offsite content directly, not only by trusting inventory lists.

### Interviewing key personnel
- Confirm they understand their responsibilities.
- Confirm detailed task documentation is current.

### Reviewing the alternative processing contract
- Check vendor references; verify promises in writing.
- Contract is clear and understandable; a lawyer reviews terms.
- Confirm rules for sites shared with other subscribers.
- Insurance ties in with disaster expenses.
- Contract permits regular hot site tests.
- Evaluate communications requirements for the backup site.
- A lawyer reviews that source code escrow is enforceable.
- Determine limitation of recourse if the vendor breaches.

### Reviewing insurance coverage
- Coverage must reflect actual cost of recovery.
- Review media damage, business interruption, equipment replacement and continuity processing.
- Risk areas come from BIA, customer contracts, SLAs and regulatory impacts.
- Know critical provisions for insurance policies.

## Takeaway
- BCP keeps the business alive; DRP restores IT; senior managers own it.
- Start with key processes, risk assessment and BIA before writing plans.
- Test progressively: tabletop, preparedness, then full; retest after every change.
- Keep call trees, offsite copies and storage current; auditors sample them.
- Cost of recovery must never exceed benefit; insurance never covers reputation.
- Only a trained spokesperson speaks publicly; life safety comes first.
