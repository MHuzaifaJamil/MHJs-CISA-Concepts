# 4.2 IT Asset Management: Study Notes

[← Domain 4](../README.md)

Scope: Study notes for outline file 4.2-IT-Asset-Management.md (CRM 28th Ed. section 4.2).

## 4.2.1 What Is an Asset

- Asset
  - Definition: Anything of tangible or intangible value worth protecting.
  - Use: Lets the organization decide what needs protection and how much.
  - Audit impact: Unidentified assets receive no controls, so they become blind spots.
- Asset examples: people, information, infrastructure, finances, and reputation.
- Example: A customer database and the server hosting it are both assets.
- Protecting an asset requires an inventory showing identity, location, and assigned owner.
- 📓 Risk programs start with a comprehensive asset inventory.

## 4.2.2 First Steps

- IT asset management
  - Definition: Tracking and controlling all hardware and software through their life.
  - Use: Supports protection, licensing, cost control, and recovery planning.
  - Audit impact: Auditors test completeness and accuracy of the asset population.
- First step: identify all IT assets.
- Second step: build an inventory of those assets.
- Asset management applies to both software and hardware.
- Exam traps
  - Asset identification and inventory come before classification or control selection.
  - Protecting only "critical" assets is wrong; all assets need suitable protection.

## 4.2.3 Inventory Record Fields

- Inventory record
  - Definition: One entry describing a single information asset and its attributes.
  - Use: Gives one place to see ownership, value, and handling rules.
  - Audit impact: Missing fields signal weak accountability and weak recovery planning.
- Owner
  - Definition: Person or entity responsible for the asset.
  - Audit impact: No owner means no one decides classification or access.
- Designated custodian
  - Definition: Individual or group responsible for maintenance and care.
  - Use: Applies the controls the owner requires, such as backups.
  - Audit impact: Confirm owner and custodian are distinct and documented.
- Specific identification
  - Definition: Unique identifiers for the asset, such as tag or serial number.
  - Audit impact: Enables tracing an asset from record to physical item.
- Relative value
  - Definition: The asset's worth to the organization.
  - Audit impact: Drives how much protection spending is justified.
- Loss implications and recovery priority
  - Definition: Impact if the asset is lost, and its restoration order.
  - Audit impact: Should align with business impact analysis recovery priorities.
- Location
  - Definition: Where the asset sits, physically or virtually.
  - Audit impact: Needed for physical checks and cloud or data-residency review.
- Security/risk classification
  - Definition: The asset's assigned sensitivity and risk level.
  - Audit impact: Classification sets required safeguards and handling rules.
- Asset group
  - Definition: The wider information system the asset belongs to.
  - Audit impact: Shows dependencies, so one failure's wider impact is visible.
- Life cycle management stage
  - Definition: Current phase of the asset, from acquisition to retirement.
  - Audit impact: Old-stage assets may lack support or patches.
- Compliance requirements
  - Definition: Legal or regulatory duties tied to the asset.
  - Audit impact: Auditors verify those duties are actually met.
- Access controls
  - Definition: Details of who is authorized to access the asset.
  - Audit impact: Compare recorded access against actual access during testing.
- Disposal date
  - Definition: Date the asset was returned or removed from circulation.
  - Audit impact: Confirms retired assets were sanitized and not left in use.
- ⚠ Check: Manual defines disposal date as actual removal date, not planned.
- Exam traps
  - Owner is accountable for the asset; custodian only maintains it day to day.
  - Relative value and recovery priority are separate fields; do not merge them.

## 4.2.4 Building the Initial Inventory

- Purchasing system
  - Definition: Procurement records of what the organization bought.
  - Audit impact: Misses donated, free, or unofficially bought items.
- Contracts
  - Definition: Agreements covering leased, licensed, or outsourced assets.
  - Audit impact: Reveals assets the organization does not physically hold.
- Installed software review and discovery tools
  - Definition: Tools that scan company devices for installed software.
  - Use: Finds software on laptops and mobile devices.
  - Audit impact: Best at finding unrecorded or unapproved software.
- Using several sources together improves completeness.
- Example: Purchasing shows 500 laptops, but scanning finds 520 devices on network.
- Exam traps
  - No single source is complete; combining sources is the stronger approach.

## 4.2.5 Maintaining the Inventory

- Inventory maintenance
  - Definition: Keeping records current as assets change.
  - Audit impact: A stale inventory gives false assurance about coverage.
- Add new assets when acquired and distributed.
- Periodically confirm each asset remains in the owner's possession.
- Remove assets once returned or destroyed.
- Physical tagging of hardware
  - Definition: Attaching a visible unique label to each hardware item.
  - Use: Allows quick physical verification against records.
  - Audit impact: Sample from floor to records, and records to floor.
- Software tracking
  - Definition: Network scanning plus annual confirmation licenses are still needed.
  - Use: Finds installed software and removes unused licenses.
  - Audit impact: Compare scan results with license entitlements for compliance gaps.
- Remote wipe or disable
  - Definition: Remotely erasing or locking a device.
  - Use: Used on departure intent, or when an asset is lost or stolen.
  - Audit impact: Need depends on asset nature and data risk classification.
- Example: A stolen laptop with confidential data is wiped remotely within hours.
- Exam traps
  - Wipe is risk-based, not mandatory for every asset in every case.
  - Tagging suits hardware; software is tracked by scanning and license review.

## 4.2.6 Software Audits and Installation Control

- Software audit
  - Definition: Review comparing installed software with approved software.
  - Use: Flags unapproved software use.
  - Audit impact: Findings feed shadow IT and licensing compliance reviews.
- Restricting installation to IT administrators
  - Definition: Preventive control removing install rights from ordinary users.
  - Use: Stops unapproved software before it arrives.
  - Audit impact: Preventive control is stronger than detective audit alone.
- The two controls complement each other: prevent, then detect.
- Exam traps
  - Audit alone is detective; pairing with admin-only installation adds prevention.

## Takeaway

- Identify assets first, then inventory, then classify and protect.
- Every record needs an owner and a custodian.
- Combine purchasing, contracts, and discovery tools for completeness.
- Keep inventory current: add, confirm possession, and remove retired assets.
- Remote wipe is risk-based; tag hardware, scan software.
- Pair software audits with admin-only installation rights.
