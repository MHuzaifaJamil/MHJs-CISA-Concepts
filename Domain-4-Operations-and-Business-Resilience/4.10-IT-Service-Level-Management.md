# 4.10 IT Service Level Management

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- ITSM: IT managed as discrete, interdependent processes serving the business
- Focus on business deliverables; fine-tune services, reduce cost over time
- SLAs narrow gap between customer expectations and services offered
- Operational level agreements (OLAs): internal agreements supporting service delivery
- Help desk flow: classify as incident, look up KEDB, escalate to problem management
- Change management invoked for any required change
  - Cost-benefit and feasibility reviewed; risk studied; fallback plan
  - Invokes configuration management; versions at system, program or module level
- Frameworks
  - ITIL: detailed ITSM framework on business value delivery
  - ISO 20000: international ITSM standard
### 4.10.1 Service level agreements (SLA) 📓
- Agreement between IT organization (internal or external) and customer (business)
- Describes services in nontechnical terms from customer viewpoint
- Service-level management
  - Define, agree, document, manage cost-justified levels
  - Service catalog, service review meetings
  - Service improvement plans (SIPs) for missed SLAs
- Penalties: fines or reduced billing if SLA missed
- SLA characteristics: accuracy, completeness, timeliness, security
- Monitoring tools
  - Exception reports
    - Causes of excess: poor requirements understanding, design/testing, instructions
    - Causes of excess: operations support, operator training, task sequencing, configuration, capacity
  - System and application logs
    - Tests: only approved programs and authorized personnel access sensitive data
    - Tests: utilities altering data used only for authorized purposes
    - Tests: approved programs run only when scheduled; correct data file generation used
  - Operator problem reports: review operator responses; training needs
  - Operator work schedules: staffing, cross-training, emergency coverage
- SLAs tied to chargeback; outsourcing SLA needed for third parties
- Measures: hardware/software performance, financial, human resources, risk management
- Measures should include risk, security, control, efficiency, effectiveness
### 4.10.2 Monitoring of service levels
- Regular monitoring by appropriate management; review customer impact
- Example: 99.9 percent ATM availability allows about 8.8 hours downtime yearly (ORM text says 3.5 days, which fits 99 percent, not 99.9)
- Outsourced services: responsibility outsourced, accountability remains
- Assurance techniques
  - Questionnaires
  - Onsite visits
  - Independent assurance reports: SSAE 18 SOC 1, AT-101 (SOC 2, SOC 3)
### 4.10.3 Service levels and enterprise architecture
- EA aligns service delivery across channels sharing one backend database
- Channels: mobile apps, Internet, service outlets, third parties, kiosks
- Unacceptable recovery time leads to fault-tolerant, high-availability architecture
