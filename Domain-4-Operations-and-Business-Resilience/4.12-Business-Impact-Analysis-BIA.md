# 4.12 Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

*PART B: Business Resilience*

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Critical step in business continuity strategy and BCP
- Evaluates critical processes (and supporting IT), timeframes, priorities, resources, interdependencies
- Double-check risk assessment input; BIA uncovers hidden components
- Consider contractual commitments with outsourced IT
- Needs senior management sponsorship; criticality approved by senior management
- Include all resource types (applications, data, networks, system software, facilities)
  - Hidden components: gateways, transport servers
- Impact bands (high, medium, low); impact estimated in time (hours, days, weeks)
  - Same approach for data loss and financial impact
- Collect time frames for vital resource supply
- BIA approaches
  - Questionnaire to key users in IT and end-user areas
  - Interviews of groups of key users
  - Joint workshop of IT and process owners to decide downtime and vital resources
- Analyze past transaction volume to support interviews
- Three BIA questions
  - What are the different business processes
    - Criticality indicators: health and safety, income loss, legal requirements, many users
    - Criticality varies by time and mode of operation
  - Which critical information resources relate to critical processes
    - Examples: receiving payments, producing goods, paying employees, advertising, dispatching, compliance
  - What is the critical recovery period before unacceptable losses
    - Banks shorter than manufacturing; day and week matter
- Two independent cost factors
  - Downtime cost: rises with time, then plateaus when business cannot function
    - Components: idle resources, lost sales, financial costs, delays, indirect (market share, image, goodwill)
  - Recovery (corrective measure) cost: falls as target recovery time lengthens
    - Components: BCP preparation and testing, offsite premises, insurance, alternate site arrangements
    - Fixed-base cost per strategy; variable costs after disaster (warm site daily fee, overtime, logistics)
  - Total cost is U curve; choose minimum
  - Cost calculations not tested on CISA exam
### 4.12.0 Resilience lifecycle (your notes) 📓
- Risk Assessment (RA): identifies threats and vulnerabilities, estimates impact 📓
- BIA: identifies critical functions, downtime tolerance, MTD/RTO/RPO 📓⭐
- BCP: keeps business operating (people, manual processes, alternate sites) 📓
- DRP: technical sub-component, restores IT infrastructure 📓
### 4.12.1 Classification of operations and criticality analysis
- Primary BIA objective: critical functions, recovery priorities, RTO/RPO targets 📓⭐
- Risk ranking: impact from critical recovery period times likelihood of disruption
- System classification
  - Critical: only replaceable by identical capability; not manual; very low interruption tolerance
  - Vital: manual only briefly; restore within about five days or less
  - Sensitive: manual at tolerable cost for extended period; extra staff needed
  - Nonsensitive: can be interrupted long with little cost; little catching up
- Annual loss expectancy (ALE)
  - Example: US$10 million impact x 0.1 percent = US$10,000 over five years
- Risk ranking done with IS processing and end-user personnel
- Next phase: recovery strategies using RPO, RTO, MTTR
