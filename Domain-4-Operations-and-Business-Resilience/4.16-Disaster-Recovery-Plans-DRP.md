# 4.16 Disaster Recovery Plans (DRP)

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- DR planning: internal control to manage availability and restore critical IT services
- Continuous process; importance from BIA with RPO and RTO
- Two outcomes
  - IT DR strategies: infrastructure, process, organization changes over three to five years
  - DRPs: from department-level procedures to modular multitiered plans
- Goal: respond to incidents affecting people, operations, compliance
- Third-party compliance applies; human safety first (evacuate before restoring service)
- Third-party disaster: customer may wait for vendor DRP
### 4.16.1 RPO, RTO and MTTR ⭐
- RPO: maximum acceptable data loss; sets backup frequency 📓⭐
  - Earliest acceptable recovery point; e.g., 4 hours before disaster
  - Catch-up data: transactions since RPO to re-enter
  - Orphan data: data still lost after re-entry
  - Affects data protection technology
  - RPO in minutes: mirroring or real-time replication
- RTO: maximum acceptable downtime 📓
  - Earliest point operations must resume
  - Affects availability technology: warm site, hot site, clusters
  - RTO in minutes: hot site, dedicated spares, clustering
  - Lower RTO means lower disaster tolerance
- Not all critical data retained means RPO was defined wrongly 📓
- RTO/RPO technologies
  - RPO: tape backups, log shipping, disk-based backups, snapshots, delayed replication, mirroring, real-time replication
  - RTO: cold standby, hot standby, active-passive clustering, active-active clustering
  - Nearer zero means higher cost
- MTTR: average time to repair a failed system
  - Total repair time divided by number of repairs
  - Factors: complexity, spare parts, technician skill and number, maintenance procedures
  - High MTTR means lost productivity and revenue
- Other recovery parameters
  - Interruption window: maximum wait from failure to restoration
  - Service delivery objective (SDO): service level during alternate mode
  - Maximum tolerable outages (MTO): maximum time in alternate mode
### 4.16.2 Recovery strategies
- Identifies best way to recover systems; guides detailed procedures
- Present all alternatives to senior management; management selects and accepts residual risk
- Selection depends on
  - Criticality of process and applications
  - Cost
  - Time to recover
  - Security
- Recovery cost: fixed (redundant computers, alternate routing, testing) and variable (use)
  - Fixed costs insurable; variable generally not
  - Premiums lower with suitable plan
- Each IT platform supporting a critical function needs a strategy
### 4.16.3 Recovery alternatives
- Hot site: storage capacity must match the primary site 📓
- Hot site concerns: server specs differ, shared non-expiring accounts 📓
- Warm, cold, mobile, reciprocal agreements
- Cold site
  - Space and basic infrastructure; no IT equipment, data, or office support
  - Plan must provide for acquiring and installing equipment
- Mobile site
  - Modular processing facility on transportable vehicles
  - Plan specifies locations, right-of-access, ancillary infrastructure (roads, water, power, communications)
- Warm site
  - Infrastructure partially configured with IT; network connections, peripherals
  - Equipment may be less capable; load current programs and data on activation
- Hot site (full description)
  - All IT and communications equipment plus office furniture
  - Installed program versions; data duplicated real or near-real time
  - Staff transferred from primary site
- Mirrored site
  - Fully redundant, real-time replication, equipped and staffed
  - No interruption perceived by users
- Reciprocal agreements
  - Between similar companies to share IT facilities
  - Not viable: compatibility, security/privacy compliance, enforcement
- Reciprocal agreements with other organizations
  - Organizations with unique equipment or applications assist each other
  - Less frequently used than traditional reciprocal agreements
- Fastest recovery costs most; management compares BIA costs to option costs
- Alternate site beyond geographic area affected by disruptive events
- Plan includes redundant network communication to alternate site
- Alternate facility third-party or owned; multiple companies may compete for it
- Contractual provisions
  - Configurations: adequate; updates and tests over time
  - Disaster: definition broad enough
  - Access: exclusive or shared; guaranteed access; conflict resolution
  - Priority: precludes preemption by governmental entities
  - Availability and speed of availability
  - Subscribers per site and per area
  - Preference in common or regional disasters; backup for backup facilities
  - Insurance for employees at site
  - Use period and technical support
  - Communications adequacy
  - Warranties and liability limits
  - Audit: right-to-audit logical, physical, environmental security
  - Testing rights; possible premium reduction
  - Reliability: UPS, limited subscribers, technical management, compatibility guarantees
  - Security per company policy
- Procuring alternative hardware
  - Cold and warm sites: acquire hardware and software on activation
  - Standard readily available systems versus unique or outdated systems
  - Pre-acquire hard-to-get components to meet RTO
  - Telecommunications recovery
    - Network disaster prevention, alternative routing, diverse routing
    - Long-haul diversity, local loop protection, voice recovery
    - Circuits and bandwidth availability; server DRPs
### 4.16.4 Development of DR plans
- Strategy follows BIA and risk assessment
- Implementation changes: IT systems, networks, processing sites, organization structure, processes
- Plan documented in simple language
- IT DRP contents
  - Procedures for declaring a disaster (escalation)
  - Criteria for plan activation and covered scenarios
  - Linkage to emergency response, crisis management, BCPs
  - Persons responsible for each function
  - Recovery teams and responsibilities
  - Contact and notification lists
  - Step-by-step recovery: where, when, what, order
  - Recovery procedures per IT system or component
  - Vendor and supplier contacts
  - Resources required for recovery
- IT DRP scenarios
  - Loss of network connectivity
  - Loss of a key IT system
  - Loss of processing site (server room)
  - Loss of critical data
  - Loss of an office
  - Loss of key service provider (e.g., cloud)
  - Plan for worst case (sales peak, period end)
- Recovery procedures
  - High-level sequence per major scenario; refer to detailed procedures
- Organization and assignment of responsibilities
  - Team-to-disruption-level matrix
  - Team designations (not tested by name) ➕
    - Incident response team: receives incident reports; postmortem input
    - Emergency action team: first responders, fire wardens; evacuation, human life
    - Information security team: maintain security at alternate site
    - Damage assessment team: assess damage and recovery time
    - Emergency management team: coordinates teams; activates BCP; finance, legal, PR
    - Offsite storage team: ships media and records to recovery facility
    - Software team: restores system packs, loads OS software
    - Applications team: restores user packs and programs; monitors database integrity
    - Emergency operations team: shift operators at recovery site
    - Network recovery team: reroutes WAN voice and data traffic
    - Communications team: installs communications hardware; works with carriers
    - Transportation team: finds recovery site; moves employees
    - User hardware team: terminals, printers, photocopiers
    - Data preparation and records team: updates databases; record salvage
    - Administrative support team: clerical, message center, payroll
    - Supplies team: office and computer supplies logistics
    - Salvage team: relocation; reconstruction versus relocation advice; insurance claims
    - Relocation team: move from hot site to restored location
    - Coordination team: across offices and offshored providers
    - Legal affairs team: legal issues from incidents
    - Recovery test team: tests plans and analyzes results
    - Training team: BC and DR training for users
### 4.16.5 DR testing methods
- Based on risk assessment and BIA; testing schedule
- Untested plans likely fail; plan objectives for measurable benefit
- Independent third party (IS auditor) monitors and evaluates
- Testing steps
  - Develop test objectives
  - Execute test
  - Evaluate test
  - Develop recommendations
  - Follow-up on implementation
- No recommendations means test was not challenging enough
- Types of tests
  - Checklist review: preliminary; distribute and confirm current checklists
  - Structured walk-through: team implements plans on paper step by step
  - Simulation test: role-play scenario; no processing at recovery site
  - Parallel test: recovery site at readiness; primary operations continue
  - Full interruption test: shift operations to recovery site; most rigorous, costly, disruptive
- Start simple, increase gradually; full interruption annually after individual tests
- Surprise tests realistic but disruptive
- Schedule at low-impact times (long weekends); key team members involved
- Testing
  - Verify plan completeness and precision
  - Evaluate personnel performance
  - Appraise awareness of non-team staff
  - Evaluate coordination with vendors and suppliers
  - Measure backup site capacity
  - Assess vital records retrieval
  - Evaluate relocated equipment and supplies
  - Measure overall IS processing performance
  - Phases: pretest, test (applications fail over), posttest (restore to primary, delete data from third-party systems)
  - Diaries document observations, problems, resolutions
- Test results
  - Quantitative metrics
  - Time: was RTO met
  - Data: was RPO met; recovery point aligned across interconnected applications
  - Amount: backup-site throughput
  - Percentage/number: critical systems recovered
  - Accuracy: data entry and processing versus normal
- Greatest concern in a DR test: backup systems defective or failing 📓⭐
### 4.16.6 Invoking DR plans
- BCP and DRP closely aligned
- Designated individual notified; escalation protocol (spokesperson, top management, regulators)
- Mobilize teams after matching incident to tested scenario
- Evaluate response options by ability to meet RPO and RTO
- Follow documented procedures; evaluate options at each step
- Document all decisions; update procedures after normal service
### Case study (ORM)
- Pinkwater Bank: 16 branches, microwave radio connectivity proposed
- Most important microwave concern: lack of encryption (easy to tap)
- Wireless network risk: isolate on firewalled subnet
- Hot site contract: review desktop and server needs quarterly
- Do not store backup media at hot site
- Branch recovery: branches back each other up for critical staff
- Adding branches to hot site contract is costlier
