# 4.13 System and Operational Resilience

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- System resilience: withstand and adapt to disruptions while keeping core functions
- Built with redundant components, failover mechanisms, backup strategies
- Threats: hardware failures, network outages, cyberattacks, software bugs
### 4.13.1 Application resiliency and disaster recovery methods
- Clustering
  - Agent installed on each node; management software controls and tunes
  - Protects against single point of failure; goal is higher availability
  - Active-passive
    - App runs on one active node; passive nodes take over on failure
    - Agents watch and restart app; app need not be cluster-aware
    - Data replicated from active to other nodes
  - Active-active
    - App runs on every node; load balanced; concurrent data access coordinated
    - Little downtime; app must be cluster-aware
    - Greater demand on network latency; shared storage needed
  - Combined: active-active at site, active-passive between sites
  - Metro-clusters: one city
  - Geo-clusters: across cities, countries, continents
  - In-house cluster software rarely economical
### 4.13.2 Telecommunication networks resiliency and DR methods
- Organization, not local exchange carrier, ensures communication continuity
- Unique risks: central switching office disasters, cable cuts, software glitches, hacking, human errors
- IPF BCP capabilities: voice circuits, WANs, LANs, third-party EDI
- Identify capacity needs for outage thresholds (e.g., 2, 8, 24 hours)
- UPS sized for telecommunications and computer equipment
- Redundancy
  - Extra capacity; second LAN cable on alternate route
  - Multiple paths between routers
  - Dynamic routing protocols: OSPF, EIGRP
  - Failover devices for routers, switches, firewalls
  - Save device configurations to TFTP servers
- Alternative routing
  - Alternate medium (copper, fiber) or different networks, circuits, endpoints
  - Counter-rotating fiber rings in separate sheaths
  - Cellular, microwave, couriers as alternatives
- Diverse routing: split-cable or duplicate-cable facilities 📓⭐
  - Different sheaths may share one conduit
  - Dual entrance facilities: costly and slow to obtain
  - Ground-based cables at risk from aging infrastructure
- Alternative routing, long-haul network diversity, last-mile circuit protection
  - Long-haul diversity: multiple long-distance carriers; automatic rerouting; auditor verifies at recovery facility
  - Last-mile protection: redundant local carrier circuits, microwave, coaxial
- Voice recovery: redundant cabling, VoIP
- Satellite connectivity: broadband or temporary satellite
