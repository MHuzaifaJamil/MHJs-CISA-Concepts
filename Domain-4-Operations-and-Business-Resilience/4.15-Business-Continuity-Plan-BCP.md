# 4.15 Business Continuity Plan (BCP)

> **Markers:** 📓 = in my Google Doc notes · ⭐ = frequently tested · ➕ = not in the review manual, verify · unmarked = gap to study. Numbering follows the ISACA CISA Review Manual 28th Ed.

- Purpose: continue operations through any disturbance
- First step: identify business processes of strategic importance
- Formal executive policy states recovery target and empowers planners
- Risk assessment on key processes; risk proportional to impact and probability
  - Identify resources (human, data, infrastructure, third-party) supporting key processes
  - Identify vulnerabilities and threats
  - Estimate threat probabilities
  - Assess existing countermeasure efficiency and effectiveness
- Senior managers are primarily responsible
- BCP provides reduced but sufficient functionality during recovery
- BCP considers critical operations and supporting human/material resources
- BCP includes DRP and restoration plan
  - DRP: recover inoperable facility, relocate operations
  - Restoration plan: return to normal in restored or new facility
- Multiple plans allowed but must be consistent; single integrated plan preferred
- Solutions differ by scenario and contract (online versus back-office)
### 4.15.1 IT business continuity planning
- Same approach as enterprise BCP, with IT processing threatened
- IT BCP aligned with organization strategy; application value tied to strategy role
- IT plan must be consistent with corporate BCP
- Alternate facilities ready, compatible, with current plans
- Reduce disruption likelihood
  - Data center not on flood plain, earthquake fault, or near explosives/toxic materials
  - Resilient topologies: loop or mesh with alternative facilities built in
- Countermeasures: duplicate facility, redundant networks, resilient hardware, backup, replication
- Risk assessment output: dependency map of processes, applications, IT components
- Risk estimation: qualitative or quantitative
- BIA gives maximum downtime and data loss per application
- Results feed IS business continuity strategy and roadmap
- Plan changes and is retested as infrastructure changes
- BCP covers business actions: where staff report, order-taking, vendor calls
- IT DRP: subcomponent restoring systems, communications, applications, data
- Cost should never exceed benefit; not all systems need recovery strategy
- BIA groups systems by recovery time to guide technology choice
- Implement ISMS since controls may fail during disaster
- Third-party (cloud) disruptions may not be recoverable by the customer
### 4.15.2 Disasters and other disruptive events
- Disasters render information resources inoperative for minutes to months
- Natural causes: earthquakes, floods, tornados, storms, fire
- Utility loss: electricity, telecommunications, gas
- Human causes: terrorist attacks, hacker attacks, human error
- Other causes
  - System malfunctions
  - Accidental file deletions
  - Untested application releases
  - Loss of backup
  - DoS attacks, intrusions, viruses
- Help desk is early warning; creeping disasters go undetected
- Short term: alternate processing facility; long term: new permanent facility
- Pandemic planning
  - Outbreaks of infectious disease spreading widely
  - Differs from traditional BCP; scale and duration hard to determine
- Damage to image, reputation or brand
  - Rumors; loss of trust; PR activities contain damage
  - Regulated or high-impact industries need elaborate protocols
  - Trained spokesperson (legal counsel or PR officer); only they speak
  - Pre-drafted generic announcement; no cause stated, no liability assumed
  - Do not blame system or process
  - Senior executives with shared responsibilities do not travel together
- Unanticipated / unforeseeable events
  - Black swan: surprise, major effect, rationalized in hindsight
  - Cannot be planned for; consider contingency thinking for critical processes

### 4.15.4 Business continuity policy
- Approved by top management; defines extent and scope of effort
- Public and internal sections
- Purposes
  - Message to internal stakeholders: commitment of resources
  - Reassure external stakeholders (shareholders, regulators)
  - Empower those responsible
  - State principles: risk assessment, BIA, redundancy targets
- Should be proactive: detect, prevent, mitigate
- BCP/IT DRP is most critical corrective control
  - Depends on incident management and backup and recovery
- Controls that reduce likelihood
  - Infrastructure monitoring, capacity management, incident management (help desk), risk management
- Controls that mitigate consequences
  - Backup and recovery, BCP or IT DRP, vendor/supplier contract clauses
  - Spare processing site, UPS or power generator
- Incident management group needs staffing, support, crisis training
- BCP well designed, documented, drill tested, funded, audited
### 4.15.5 BCP incident management
- Incidents are dynamic; management must be dynamic, proactive, documented
- Incident: any unexpected event, even without damage
- Classification categories
  - Negligible: no perceptible damage (brief OS crash, momentary outage with UPS)
  - Minor: no material impact; localized (single-office Internet outage)
  - Major: material impact on processes, other systems, clients (ransomware)
  - Crisis: major incident seriously disrupting operations; severity grows with elapsed time
- Document and revisit minor, major, crisis until resolved
- Negligible incidents analyzed statistically for systemic causes
- Severity driven largely by estimated downtime vs SLA
- Other criteria: impact on data or platforms, degree of functional impact
  - Figure 4.38 data example: database loss of integrity
  - Platform examples: viruses, worms, hardware failure ➕
  - Crisis action: follow business continuity plan ➕
  - Major action: prepare for business continuity plan ➕
  - Minor action: correct, clean, restore or replace ➕
- Fail-safe approach: provisional level 3 for any non-negligible incident; reevaluate regularly
- Incident response (firecall) team reevaluates level
- Security officer notified of relevant incidents; follows escalation protocol
  - Escalation: spokesperson, top management, regulators
### 4.15.6 Development of business continuity plans
- Inputs: BIA, criticality analysis, selected recovery strategy
- Factors in developing/reviewing plan
  - Predisaster readiness and incident response management
  - Evacuation procedures
  - Procedures for declaring a disaster (rating, escalation)
  - Circumstances for declaring a disaster (e.g., uncontained virus)
  - Clear responsibilities and responsible persons per function
  - Contact information
  - Step-by-step recovery processes
  - Resources required for recovery and continued operation
- Written in simple language; team-structured; copies offsite
### 4.15.7 Other issues in plan development
- Management and user involvement vital; users identify critical systems and recovery times
- Three personnel divisions
  - Support services: detect first signs
  - Business operations: may suffer from incident
  - Information processing support: run recovery
- Cover whole organization, not only IS processing
- Plan includes
  - Staff list for short, medium, long term with redundant contacts
  - Building facilities, desks, chairs, telephones
  - Resources to resume operations (not only IT)
### 4.15.8 Components of a business continuity plan
- BCP should include
  - Continuity of operations plan
  - DRP
  - Business resumption plan
- BCP may also include
  - Continuity of support plan / IT contingency plan
  - Crisis communications plan
  - Incident response plan
  - Transportation plan
  - Occupant emergency plan
  - Evacuation and emergency relocation plan
- NIST SP 800-34 plan types
  - BCP: sustains business operations during recovery from significant disruption
  - COOP: sustains mission essential functions at alternate site up to 30 days
  - Crisis communications plan: internal and external communications; controls rumors
  - Critical infrastructure protection (CIP) plan
  - Cyber incident response plan: mitigate cyberattack (virus, worm, Trojan); isolate, clean up
  - DRP: activated after major system disruption with long-term effects
  - Information system contingency plan (ISCP): single-system recovery
  - Occupant emergency plan (OEP): minimize loss of life and injury; initiated first
- Agree before planning
  - Governing policies
  - Goals and products per phase
  - Alternate facilities
  - Critical information resources
  - Persons responsible
  - Available resources
  - Prioritized scheduling
- Plan copies offsite: recovery facility, media storage, homes of key personnel, mirrored website
- Key decision-making personnel
  - Call tree: prioritized notification directory, often automated
  - Redundant, hard copy, intranet, regularly updated
  - Directory contents
    - Prioritized contacts; primary and emergency numbers
    - Equipment and software vendors
    - Supply/equipment/service providers
    - Recovery facility contacts (hot site, network rerouting)
    - Offsite media storage contacts and authorized retrievers
    - Insurance agents, contract personnel services
    - Legal, regulatory, government agencies
    - Procedure to count people reached
- Backup of required supplies
  - Hard copy procedures for unfamiliar staff
  - Special forms (check stock, invoices, order forms) offsite
  - Data entry hardware and software at hot site
  - Cryptographic equipment and keys (RSA tokens, USB keys)
- Insurance
  - Specifics not tested; know what policies should include
  - IT equipment and facilities (like kind and quality replacement)
  - Media (software) reconstruction
  - Extra expense
  - Business interruption
  - Valuable papers and records
  - Errors and omissions
  - Fidelity coverage
  - Media transportation
  - Covers financial loss at historical performance; valuation must be current
  - No compensation for reputation, image, goodwill
### 4.15.9 Plan testing
- Most tests fall short of full-scale; still do full or partial tests
- Schedule to minimize disruption (weekends); simulate prime-time conditions
- Test types
  - Desk-based evaluation (tabletop/paper test): walk-through; precedes preparedness test
  - Preparedness test: localized version of full test; incremental improvement
  - Full operational test: one step from real disruption
- Specifications
  - Verify BCP completeness and precision
  - Evaluate personnel performance
  - Appraise training and awareness of all employees
  - Evaluate coordination with vendors and suppliers
  - Measure backup site capacity
  - Assess vital records retrieval
  - Evaluate relocated equipment and supplies
  - Measure overall IT processing performance
- Test execution
  - Pretest: set the stage (tables, backup telephone equipment)
  - Test: real operational activities evaluated by observers
  - Posttest: cleanup, return resources, delete company data from third-party systems, evaluate plan
- Documentation of results
  - Team diary forms of observations, problems, resolutions
  - Useful in real disaster, for insurers and authorities
- Results analysis
  - Time: elapsed time for tasks, equipment, personnel arrival
  - Amount: work performed at backup site
  - Count: vital records, supplies, systems recovered
  - Accuracy: data entry and processing versus normal
- Testing focus: gaps, assumptions, timelines, strategies, personnel, accurate plans
- Annual full test; also after revisions and key personnel/technology changes
- Information security validated during test
- Plan maintenance
  - Triggers: process or department changes, new applications, strategy changes, environment changes, new events, personnel changes
  - Include BCP in SDLC
  - BCP coordinator responsibilities
    - Schedule periodic reviews; unscheduled revisions after changes
    - Update within set days (e.g., 30 days)
    - Coordinate tests at least once per year
    - Training within 30 days of revision or test
    - Maintain records of maintenance, testing, training
    - Update notification directory at least quarterly
  - Software tools can track maintenance

### 4.15.11 Auditing business continuity
- Auditor tasks
  - Evaluate strategy and link to business objectives
  - Review BIA findings for current priorities and controls
  - Evaluate BCP adequacy against standards, regulations, RTO, RPO
  - Verify effectiveness via prior test results
  - Evaluate cloud-based mechanisms
  - Verify secure transport of backup media
  - Evaluate personnel emergency response (procedures, training, drills)
  - Ensure plan maintenance process is in place and effective
  - Check manuals are simple; stakeholders understand roles
- Third-party audit rights may be limited by contracts
- Reviewing the BCP
  - Review the document
    - Obtain policy, strategy, BCP, latest BIA (RTO, RPO)
    - Sample distributed manuals for currency
    - Plan supports strategy; invocation procedures effective
    - Update procedure and maintenance responsibility documented
  - Review applications covered
    - Critical applications (including PC applications) identified and prioritized
    - Tolerance level reviewed for all applications
    - Secondary site has correct, compatible system software versions
  - Review business continuity teams
    - Member lists and backup facility agreements
    - Call a sample of contacts; verify numbers and manual copies
    - Interview for assigned responsibilities
  - Plan testing
    - Evaluate test documentation procedures
    - Review backup procedures per DRP area
    - Emergency procedures complete, current, understandable
    - Recovery transactions identifiable separately from normal ones
    - Teams have written procedures; user recovery procedures documented
    - Plan covers movement to and from recovery site
    - Items to rebuild IPF stored offsite (blueprints, hardware inventory, wiring diagrams)
  - Questions to consider
    - Who administers and keeps the plan current; where it is stored
    - Systems and equipment not covered, and why; stated assumptions
    - Rendezvous points for emergency management team
    - Disasters of varying degrees; telecommunication backups
    - Backup facility site; relocation to new IPF
    - Merging master file data and media management data into predisaster files
    - Loading manually processed data into automated system
    - Backup procedures, responsibilities, methods, frequency, media
    - Personnel training on backup equipment and procedures
    - Offsite storage and recovery documentation adequacy
    - Testing and training schedule; SLA disaster requirements
  - Evaluation of prior test results
    - BCP coordinator keeps history; corrections incorporated
    - Review for problem trends and resolutions
- Evaluation of offsite storage
  - Media and documentation present, synchronized, current
  - Covers data files, application and system software, documentation, supplies, special forms, BCP copy
  - Detailed inventory test: dataset names, volume serial numbers, accounting periods, bin locations
  - Compare documentation currency with production
  - Review transport method for ISMS weakness
  - Evaluation of security at offsite facility
    - Limit access to authorized users
    - Raised flooring, humidity and temperature controls
    - Specialized circuitry, UPS
    - Water detection, smoke detectors, fire extinguishing
    - Current inspection and calibration tags
- Interviewing key personnel
  - Understand responsibilities; up-to-date detailed task documentation
- Reviewing the alternative processing contract
  - Check vendor references; verify promises in writing
  - Contract clear and understandable; legal review of terms
  - Confirm rules for sites shared with other subscribers
  - Insurance ties in with disaster expenses
  - Regular hot site tests allowed
  - Evaluate communications requirements for backup site
  - Lawyer reviews enforceable source code escrow
  - Determine limitation of recourse if breached
- Reviewing insurance coverage
  - Must reflect actual cost of recovery
  - Review media damage, business interruption, equipment replacement, continuity processing
  - Risk areas from BIA, customer contracts, SLAs, regulatory impacts
  - Know critical provisions for insurance policies
